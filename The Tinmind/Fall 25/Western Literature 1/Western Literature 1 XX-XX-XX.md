West Lit Final
(margin) Odyssey: Homer
- Epic placed in ancient Greece. Dactylic Hexameter (6 per line)
- Nostos, Xenia, Polytropos (man of many ways)
- Odysseus, Telemakhos, Penelope, Athena,
- Poseidon hates O. because he baind his son Polyphemos (the cyclops)
- Alcinous is the King of the Phaeacians who hosts O. and listens to his story
- Calypso is a nymph that holds O. captive
- Circe is a witch that turns O.'s men into pigs and directs O. to the underworld.
- Eumaeus: Swineherd
- Antinous: leader of the suitors and most antagonistic to O. when he returns
(margin) Aeneid: Virgil
- Epic placed in ancient Rome. Dactylic hexameter.
- Fatum, Furor, Pietas, Moles (burden – to rebuild and suffer against Juno)
- Aeneas, Dido, Juno, Jupiter, Venus, Ascanius
- Turnus: Rutilian King who fights to keep A. out of Italy.
- Pallas: Son of evander that is killed by Turnus – an important turning point.
(margin) Dante:
- Comedy, Epic in 14th century Italy. Terza Rima (3 interlocking rhymes, 3 lines)
- Contra passo, Classical Virtues (Temperance, Courage, Wisdom, Justice.) Exams of faith, hope, and love.
- Dante, Virgil, Beatrice
- Homer, Lucan, Ovid in Hell (limbo)
(margin) Milton:
- Epic in 17th century england. Blank Verse
- Enjambment (to carry a thought across more than one line), Miltonic sublime (points up to the highest; points out that the visible world reveals God.
(margin) Epic Conventions:
- Invocation of the Muse, In medias res, statement of theme, Cosmic sweep, Epic Hero, Supernatural forces, Epic Simile, Epithet, Decent into the underworld, geneologies.
