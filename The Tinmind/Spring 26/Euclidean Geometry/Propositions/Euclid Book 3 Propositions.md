## Proposition 3.1

> *To find the center of a given circle.*

## Proposition 3.3

>*If in a circle a straight line through the center bisect a straight line not through the center, it also cuts it at right angles; and if it cut it at right angles, it also bisects it.*

## Proposition 3.9

>*If a point be taken within a circle, and more than two equal straight lines fall from the point on the circle, the point taken is the center of the circle.*

- Not on the test.

## Proposition 3.16

> *The straight line drawn at right angles to the diameter of a circle from its extremity will fall outside the circle, and into the space between the straight line and the circumference another straight line cannot be interposed; further the angle of the semicircle is greater, and the remaining angle less, than any acute rectilineal angle.*

- This one is a proof by contradiction.
- Not on the test.

## Proposition 3.17

>*From a given point to draw a straight line touching a given circle.*

## Proposition 3.20

>*In a circle the angle at the center is double of the angle at the circumference, when the angles have the same circumference as base.*

## Proposition 3.21

>*In a circle the angles in the same segment are equal to one another.*

- Not on the test.

## Proposition 3.22

>*The opposite angles of quadrilaterals in circles are equal to two right angles.*

## Proposition 3.25

> *Given a segment of a circle, to describe the complete circle of which it is a segment.*

## Proposition 3.27

>*In equal circles angles standing on equal circumferences equal one another whether they stand at the centers or at the circumferences.*

## Proposition 3.31

> In a circle the angle in the semicircle is right, that in a greater segment less then a right angle, and that in a less segment greater than a right angle; and further the angle of the greater segment is greater than a right angle, and the angle of the less segment less than a right angle.

## Proposition 3.32

>*If a straight line touches a circle, and from the point of contact there is drawn across, in the circle, a straight line cutting the circle, the angles which it makes with the tangent will be equal to the angles in the alternate segments of the circle.*
