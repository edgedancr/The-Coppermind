9/5/26
#### Why its hard for gen Z to get jobs
- Entry-level jobs have fallen since 2024.
- Only 8% of hiring managers believe gen Z is workforce ready.
- This is our competition & reputation before we walk into the room. There is nothing you can do about it.
- USE this reputation to your benefit. It is easy to stand out. If you are given the chance to stand out, TAKE IT.
#### The Resume
- It is not a biography, instead it is a targeted argument.
- It is not a list of duties, instead a list of evidence that you can do the job.
- Think like a marketer, not like an intern.
- They will give 10 seconds at most, and most likely not more than 2 seconds to your resume. It needs to stand out.
#### The Cover Letter
- It answers the question: Why should the company hire you specifically?
- It is not a prose version of your resume. Restating bullet points will lose you the job.
- Create a vision of a mutually beneficial future.
- Structure
	- Why this organization (1 paragraph)
	- Your strongest case (1 paragraph)
	- Closing, inviting next steps (1 paragraph)
- If they ever put the cover letter as optional, write one anyway.
#### LinkedIn and others
- Mirror your resume.
- Recruiters increasingly check LinkedIn before or instead of your resume.
- Real headshot, a real headline, a filled-out profile — not one photo and no summary.
- Remember your internet history, public info can come back and bite you.
#### Letters of Reference
- Ask early, 2-4 week's minimum
- Ask in-person or over email
- Give your recommender real material: your resume, the posting, 2-3 points you'd like them to mention.
- Follow up with a thank you. Hand write one if you can.
- On campus supervisors and work-study bosses are real professional references too — same reputation, same seriousness

#### Reputation at PHC
- PHC is a small campus. People will talk about what you do.
- Background investigators for a security clearance often visit campus directly and talk to anyone that has anything to say about you. This includes service workers and other classmates.
#### Resume Sections in Order
1. Contact info (name(B. Harrison Nickles), photo, professional email, LinkedIn, city/state)
2. Summary or objective line. (maybe, if needed)
3. Education (school, major, minors, GPA if it is 3.3+, relevant coursework, honors)
4. Clearance status.
5. Experience (jobs, internships, research, relevant campus roles)
6. Skills (Technical, language, tools—specific not vague)
7. Activities/leadership (clubs, athletics, ROTC, service)
8. Certification, publications (like the Anthropic Claude cert)

#### What To Put on a Resume
- Evidence of judgement, initiative, and follow-through
- Coursework project (research papers, case studies, graded group projects)
- Campus leadership (club office, team captain, dorm leadership)
- Volunteer work that has a measurable outcome.
- Independent projects—language, skills, outside class.

#### Formatting Basics
- Standard fonts (Calibri, Garamond, Georgia, Arial), 10-12 pt. font, 0.5-1 inch margins
- No tables, text boxes, columns, icons, or graphic if the resume passes through AI.
- Check the file format that the posting wants.
- Use consistent tense
- Federal / USAJOBS resumes are a different document
	- 2 pages
	- Narrative, duty-and-accomplishment style rather than terse bullets
	- Requires specifics most students leave off
#### Disagreements
- Do you actually need 2 pages? no
- Chronological vs functional
	- Reverse chronological is the standard
	- Functional, organized by skills. Can be seen as hiding something.
- Include GPA?
	- If its 3.3+
	- If you have a low GPA, leverage that. "I chose hard classes on purpose, I took a hard language."

#### Do's
- [Action verb] + [what you did] + [How/context] + [result or scale, if measurable]
- Be specific, but be honest. The words you use must be accurate.
- Look at the lingo they use in the listing, & use it yourself.
- Keep a master resume
	- Plot everything you've ever done on there, doesn't matter what.
	- Never send the master resume
- Tailor resumes to specific jobs

#### Don'ts
- Typos, inconsistency, generic words, broken parallel structure.
- List every job, club, and honor since middle school.
- List obvious, assumed skills
- sloppy file naming
- mismatched contact info
- lying or inflating

#### AI and the Resume
- 82% or companies are using AI to review resumes.
- 49% auto dismiss AI resumes.
- AI does not sound or look like you.
- Use AI to brainstorm phrasing, check grammar, reverse engineer keywords.
- The test: could you talk about every claim for 5 minutes?
- You need to communicate that you can think without AI

#### Standing out as an intern
- Apply to places you want, even if you won't get it. Years of application to one company can help you get noticed.
- ASK for work. Be productive. Stand out.

#### The Interview
- Research their mission, recent news, publicly stated priorities.
- Prepare 3-5 STAR format stories (Situation, task, action, result)
- **ASK QUESTIONS.** Ask about taskings, culture, what you can do to prepare.
- Respond quick.
- Early interviews are better, 9-11am. You set the standard for later interviews.
- arrive early—not to the parking lot, but to the inside.
- Bring copies of your resume and a notepad.
- What DQ's people
	- Lack of confidence, underselling yourself, rambling, badmouthing, appearing not to have researched at all, not being able to explain something on the resume, having no questions for the interviewer.
