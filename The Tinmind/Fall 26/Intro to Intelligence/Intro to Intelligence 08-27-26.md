- "The enemy always gets a vote"
	- Even with the most detailed of information, there is always the chance that the enemy is thinking differently or making a different decision that you are expecting.
- ##### **Guest Lecture: CDR Blocker**
	- IC is very broad, and is largely carried by the small number of people with a moral backbone.
	- You need to have a strong moral conscious to be a good intelligence officer.
	- Intelligence seeks to see the world as it truly is. Christian intelligence officers have another layer to that.
	- Information shapes decisions. Decisions shape history.
	- Our job, at least at first, will be to provide information to the deliver information to the decision makers. We need to be learning from those above us as to how to make good decisions, because one day we might be the ones making decisions.
	- Case Studies:
		- D-Day
			- We needed to make the germans think we weren't attacking where we were.
		- 9/11
			- CIA knew the terrorists were in the US. There wasn't enough communication to put the pieces together in time. They thought they could turn the terrorists.
		- Global monitoring
			- Things like satellites over countries provide so much data, but it's hard to go over all of it at once.
		- Venezuela
	- Things everyone in intelligence needs:
		- Curiosity
			- Seek Truth *with Humility*
				- Never stop learning
				- Ask better questions
				- Challenge assumptions
				- Admit what you don't know
					- Also admit that you don't know what you don't know.
			- The moment we think we have all the answers is the moment we stop learning.
		- Character
			- Character means putting others before yourself
			- Serve others with courage
				- Courage is the willingness to do what is right, even when it is difficult.
				- Service is a commitment to put others and the mission before yourself.
			- Without character, intelligence can become manipulation, power can become self-interest, and knowledge can be misused.
		- Competence
			- Competence involve the skills, methods, tools, discipline, and judgment.
			- Applying tradecraft and wisdom
				- Tradecraft
					- Helps us gain knowledge and see the world clearly
				- Wisdom
					- Helps us discern what matters and what we should do.
		- Only at the intersection of all three, Curiosity, Character and Competence, do we find professionals worthy of carrying the weight of real responsibility.
