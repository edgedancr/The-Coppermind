##### Guest Lecture: Detective Mennen
- emennen@nynjhidta.org
- Detective in NYP intelligence, Domestic and International Liaison Unit.
	- Lives in the DC area (Purcellville) and gives info to the NYPD when they need info on the DC area.
	- There are lots of other places around the world that do the same: working with local police and reporting intel back to the NYPD when they need it.
	- Having people around the world helps because NY is filled with people from around the world entering and leaving every day.
- Day-to-day work
	- Intel threat analysis for events. This could look like researching past attacks against similar events in the past, and trying to determine what you should do to protect against those.
	- Religious/cultural celebrations: What could happen at the Thanksgiving day parade? What should we do to fortify and defend?
	- Racially Ethnically Motivated Extremists (REMVE) are a huge problem.
	- Keeping in mind significant anniversaries like Hitler's birthday, etc... Because people like to act on those days for symbolic reasons.
- How Intel Supports Operations, and How Students Get In the Door
	- Types of officers
		- Sworn personal have a gun and a badge — a police officer who has gone through the academy.
		- A civilian has never gone through an academy, but have been hired to be an analyst or something similar.
	- Types of operations
		- Tactical
			- Immediate. Officer safety, suspect ID, TTPs, location, pattern of a crime series happening now.
			- Breaking in a door.
		- Operational
			- Weeks to months. Networks, finances, comms/messaging, targeting packages that drive a case or task force.
			- Financing terrorist organizations.
		- Strategic
			- Moths to years. Trends, threat assessments, and resource decisions for command staff.
			- Long term trends that will undermine entire intel operations.
	- What changed?
		- Intel is now part of the operational cycle, not a packet handed off after the fact.
		- Digital evidence, open source (OSINT), CCTV video, and financial records overwhelm a detective working alone.
			- They can access anything. (example: FB messenger)
		- NVRIC (Northern Virginia Retail Intel Center), Virginia Fusion Center, HIDTA (High Intensity Drug Trafficking Area) Inv Sup Cntrs, and real-time crime centers sit between agencies.
			- Real-time crime centers are rooms in big office buildings with cameras, LPRs (license plate readers), and other things that constantly monitor crime to ensure that nothing is happening.
	- Intel-led Policing is the norm
		- Commanders expect analysts to identify prolific offenders and networks be ore had next cluster.
		- In good shops, analysts and investigators said together.
		- AI and link analysis speed the first cut. Vetting, law, and ethics still belong to people.
			- AI is not taking jobs, but being able to use it effectively is necessary for an intel analyst. AI will mess up or hallucinate, so verifying it is necessary.
- Internships
	- Law Enforcement Internships
		1. Start early
			- Freshmen or sophomore year. FBI paperwork can take a year.
		2. Use your networks
			- Faculty, SI alumni, and older students who recently finished.
		3. Target clearances sponsors
			- A first tour that only starts the investigation is a win.
		4. Apply federal and local
			- Federal = clearance, prestige.
			- Local = case work, bosses, and people who hire.
		5. Build the package
			- Clean background, GPA, intel-style writing sample, mission-focused letter.
		6. Tell the truth
			- Drugs, social media, foreign contacts, finances.
			- It doesn't matter if the truth is ugly, tell the truth. Saying "I experimented with marijuana in college" is much better than get caught lying about doing drugs.
	- Realistic Northern Virginia internship targets
		- FBI
			- Paid. Field office or HQ. Pauline to TS clearance.
		- WASH/Balt HIDTA Inv Sup Cntr
			- Reston / Greenbelt. Real case support and products
		- Loudoun County Sheriff
			- Credit internship; check current windows.
		- Fairfax PD / Sheriff
			- Larger agency, more specialized units.
		- Arlington PD CID
			- Investigative assistant internship works with detectives.
		- Prince William PD
			- Paid summer internship when offered.
		- HSI / NCIS / DEA / ATF
			- Smaller cases; high federal LE relevance.
		- VSP Fusion Center
			- Analyst path; unpaid; VSP Student Internship Program.
	- What makes a "good" internship for full time hiring?
		1. You produce real work products
			- Bulletins, link charts, OSINT packages, deconfliction — something someone used.
		2. Proximity to operators
			- Sit close enough that investigators can judge your writing, judgment, and reliability.
		3. Background in motion
			- A Secret or TS investigation started is a hiring advantage two years later.
		4. A named supervisor
			- Someone who will take a call from a hiring manager and speak for you.
		5. Both sides of the cycle
			- See how analysts build the product and how a boss actually uses it.
		6. A second-tour path
			- Agencies prefer people they have already trained/test. Paid is nice. Trust is better.
- Takeaways
	- Operations without intelligence is activity. Intelligence without operations is a briefing.
	- Intel and ops are part of one cycle
		- Agencies do not separate "desk" from "street."
	- Clearance + work sample
		- That combination beats a title. Work towards vote before graduation.
	- Start now, quietly
		- Network through people, not public profiles. Make the first impression count.
	- FBI or local first?
		- Get the clearance and the product. The hiring path follows the work.
