##### SI Writing Lab
- **Topic Sentences**
	- ==99% of people will not read your whole report. They will only read your topic sentences and summary. This is why topic sentences are incredibly important.==
	- Types of Topic Sentences
		- General Topic Sentence
			- Identifies the main idea of the paragraph but remains broad. (example)
		- Clueing Topic Sentence
			- Names the main idea and indicates that several details will be explored without naming them explicitly (example I gave my parents three reasons for wanting a raise in my allowance)
		- Specific Topic Sentence (The Analytical Gold Standard)
			- Names the main idea and explicitly lists the specific sub-topics or analytical categories to be covered. This provides a tight, logical roadmap for the reader and guarantees paragraph unity. 
	- Common Faults
		- Merely a Fact
			- Stating a static or historical fact provides no analytical path forward.
		- Overly Broad or Vague
			- 
		- Supporting Detail is Too Specific
			- Using highly specific stats, source attributions, or narrow examples belongs in the body of the paragraph as supporting evidence, not at the head.
	- We want to probe deeper into sentences and identify causal relationships between the separate items.
	- A topic sentence is not better because it is longer, **but because it makes CAUSATION EXPLICIT**
	- Strong topic sentences demonstrate causal insight that is reflected in sentence structural choices. Most strong combinations rely on **==four patterns:==**
		- **Coordination** *joins* ideas of roughly equal weight
			- with words such as: *and, but, or yet*.
		- **Subordination** *ranks* one idea under another or *sequences* ideas 
			- with words such as: *because, although, when, if, since, or after.*
			- **==There is no such thing as an unordered list.==** People will always rank items in their mind when reading, you need to make sure you are communicating your goal correctly.
				- Something like although is an inverter word.
		- **Apposition** renames a noun for efficiency
			- as in: "The committee chair, **a former auditor,** demanded revisions."
		- **Modification** compresses information
			- into adjective, adverb, participial, or prepositional *phrases*.
##### Geometry of Surveillance
- Good Ole Fashioned Surveillance (GOFS)
	- Using binoculars, hiding around corners, using your eyes to watch people or things.
	- Usually it involves trying to make yourself invisible while watching something visible.
- Arial Surveillance
	- A guy called Nadar in 1858 got in a balloon and took a picture above Paris. This works, but has lots of problems: Everyone knows you're there, your image wont be very good, the winds will blow you away, your easy to shoot down.
	- Going even 1000 or 3000 feet up with a drone can give you so much surveillance.
