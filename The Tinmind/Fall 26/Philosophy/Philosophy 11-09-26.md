George Berkeley (1685–1753): Three dialogues concerning Philonous
- How is God concerned in the regularities of the world?
- Background
	- Education
	- Motivations
		- Avoid skepticism (like Locke's ideas)
		- Avoid atheism
	- Thomas Reid on Berkeley
		- Berkeley is crazy. Everything would be an idea only
	- Hylas = Hule = Matter
	- Philinous = Phileo (Love) = nous (mind) = mind-lover,
- Metaphysics
	- <u>Immaterialism</u>. Matter doesn't exist. Only ideas. God gives us senses.
	- Idealism. To be is to be perceived (or to percieve)
	- Is it some form of skepticist?
	- His epistemology (empericism) drives his metaphysics (idealism)
	- Substance is just bundles of ideas.
	- Neither causation nor the self have any sensory correlate
- Berkeley's argument for idealism.
