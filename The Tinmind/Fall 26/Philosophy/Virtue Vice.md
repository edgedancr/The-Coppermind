## Nicomachean Ethics
- Bk 4.1, 1121b25-35 (pg. 287)
	- Stems from generosity
	- The problems come either from a deficiency in giving or a excess in taking.
	- Some people will hold on to what they have so that they will never be compelled to do anything shameful.
	- Some people don't try to interfere so that they aren't in turn interfered with.
- Bk 4.6, (pg. 293)
	- Some people praise everything to please people and never cross them.
	- The virtue is almost like friendship, except for you don't have to be fond of the person.
	- Being overly people pleasing would stem from an excess in friendship, making you a roundabout flatterer, even if you didn't mean to be.
- Bk 8.8 (pg. 333)
	- By wishing the most good for a friend, you would necessarily isolate them from you due to their status. 
	- Each man ultimately wishes the most good for himself.
	- A flatterer pretends to love more than he is loved.
	- Real friendship consists in loving others, rather than being loved.
	- The erotic lover assumes that he will be loved as much as he loves.
- Bk 9.4 (pg. 341-342)
	- The excellent person does good things for himself because they are good, and treats himself as one of his friends.
	- The excellent person finds it pleasant to spend time with himself. 
	- Vicious people cannot be with themselves, and need others around them at all times so that they can forget about their bad things. They have no love for themselves.
- Bk 9.7 (pg. 343-344)
	- Benefactors love the people that they love more than those people love them back.-
	- Those people he loves are his product. He finds enjoyment in them.
	- Loving is like production, while being loved is like being acted on.
- Bk 9.8 (pg. 344-345)
	- The self-lover gratifies the most controlling part of himself.
- Bk 9.9 (pg. 345-347)
	- Having friends seems to be the greatest external good.
	- The solitary person's life is hard since it is hard to be continuously active all by himself.
	- The good of your friend is ultimately good for you too, and you should want it for them.
- Bk 9.10 (pg. 347)
	- Those who have many friends and treat everyone as close to them seem to be friends to no one. These people are regarded as ingratiating.
	- It is impossible to be many people's friend for their virtue and for themselves.
- Bk 9.11 (pg. 347-348) Calling on friends sparingly; not being keen to receive (~1171b)
	- The very presence of friends is pleasant.
	- Everyone tries to avoid causing pain to their friends.
	- Eagerness to be benefitted is not fine.
- Bk 9.12 (pg. 348-349) Friends share whatever they love most in life
	- We are related to our friend as we are related to ourselves.
