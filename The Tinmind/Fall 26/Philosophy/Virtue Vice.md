## Nicomachean Ethics
- 1121b25-35 (pg. 287)
	- Stems from generosity
	- The problems come either from a deficiency in giving or a excess in taking.
	- Some people will hold on to what they have so that they will never be compelled to do anything shameful.
	- Some people don't try to interfere so that they aren't in turn interfered with.
- 1126b11-14 (pg. 293)
	- Some people praise everything to please people and never cross them.
	- The virtue is almost like friendship, except for you don't have to be fond of the person.
	- Being overly people pleasing would stem from an excess in friendship, making you a roundabout flatterer, even if you didn't mean to be.
- 1159a-1159b24 (pg. 333)
	- By wishing the most good for a friend, you would necessarily isolate them from you do to their status. 
	- Each man ultimately wishes the most good for himself.
	- A flatterer pretends to love more than he is loved.
	- Real friendship consists in loving others, rather than being loved.
	- The erotic lover assumes that he will be loved as much as he loves.
- Bk 9.4 (pg. 341)
	- The excellent person does good things for himself because they are good, and treats himself as one of his friends.
	- The excellent person finds it pleasant to spend time with himself.
