- What makes some physical objects seem spiritually unified?
	- Telos?
- Aristotle on Plato's forms. (Problems)
	- Plato's forms don't account for substance.
	- No internal principle of motion/change.
	- No effect on their participants
	- Doesn't explain the unity of a living substance or the unity of the participants of the form
	- Negative forms? – Bloated ontology.
- Aristotle's Natures (forms)
	- Natures are naturely occurring, not man-made.
		- Paradigm case: living organisms
		- Artificial objects don't have natures, only homonymously
	- Natures are principles (of motion, rest, growth)
	- They contain potentiality and telos.
	- They're "in" substances (not Plato's heaven)
	- Material or immaterial?
	- ontologically dependant on the matter. Matter is determined by the form.
	- Universals or particulars.
