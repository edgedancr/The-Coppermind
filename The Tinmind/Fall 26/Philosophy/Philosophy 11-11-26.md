Berkeley (wrap up)
- You can have knowledge without being certain.
- Berkeley has the same certainty as Descartes
	- But at what cost?
- God gives our minds consistant ideas of what things are when we sense them. This makes them no less real or certain.

Hume (1711–1776)
- Prove: There is a world outside of my mind.
	- How do you escape Solipsism?
- Preliminaries
	- Significance
		- William Edward Morris: "The most important philosopher ever to write in English"
		- "Patron Saint" of logical positivism and philisophical naturalism
	- Epistemology
		- Strict Empericist
		- "Academic" skeptic
			- Paul Vitz: Faith of the fearless
		- Non-foundationalist, because he can find no adequate foundation.
	- Metaphysics (761)
		- You cannot have knowledge.
- Doubting Causation
	- Relations of Ideas
		- E.g., definitions, the Pythagorean Theorem
		- Their contrary is impossible
	- Matters of Fact
		- contrary is possible
	- Cause and Effect
		- Always a posteriori
		- Constant Conjunction
		- Ultimate principles of nature are unknowable
	- Habbit vs. proof
- Academic Skepticism
	- A technical and therefore harmless point
		- Conflict with verses like 1 John 5:13 "... so that you may <u>know</u> that you have eternal life."
