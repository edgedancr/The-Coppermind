- Book 2: Of Ideas
	- Ideas and their source (ch 1)
		- Sensation. Any sensory experience is an idea. The raw data of knowledge
		- Reflection. Manufacturing ideas from sensations.
	- Of simple ideas (ch 2 & 7)
		- Simple
			- Clear and distinct from one another.
			- Neither created, nor destroyed by us
				- E.g., blind cannot create ideas of color.
		- Complex ideas: Built out of simple ideas (or other complex ideas)
			- We <u>can</u> create them

These ideas all interact.

- Simple idea of reflection (ch. 7)
	- E.G., Existence, unity, power, and succession.
- Locke is sort of a nominalist, because he thinks we categorize our experience with the world rather than the world itself.
- Qualities (ch. 8)
	- Qualities defined (p. 538)
		- Primary. What is objectively true.
			- Solidity, extension, figure (shape), motion or rest, and number, etc...
			- resemble the objects that generate them.
		- Secondary. The power to generate ideas in those who sense him.
			- red, sweet, middle C, etc...
			- Don't resemble the objects that generate them.
- Of Complex Ideas (ch 12)
	- Compounding (eg., beauty)
	- Substances (eg, Tucker)
	- Relations (eg, Smaller Than)
	- Generalizations (eg, cat)
