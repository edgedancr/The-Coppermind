- Hume is the most consistent empiricist.
	- Empiricism leads to either Berkeleyan idealism or Humean Skepticism
- Humean Empiricism's impact on other worldview matters
	- Moral knowledge: began to evaporate.
	- Irrational Faith: believing without evidence
	- Anti-realist science: Pragmatism
	- Subjective beauty

Hume on Miracles
- Preliminaries on Miracles
	- What are miracles?
	- Are miracles possible?
	- Should we ever believe one has occurred?
	- Should we ever believe on the testimony of another that a miracle has occurred?
- ★ Hume's argument believing that a miracle has occurred
	- One ought to proportion one's belief to the evidence.
	- First-hand experience is better evidence than testimony.
	- Therefore, when there is a conflict between first-hand experience and testimony, one ought to believe according to first-hand experience (P. 743)
		- Hume: the only exception would be if it is more miraculous that a miracle report is false than that it occurred (which it never is) (p. 737)
- The Miracles of the Bible and the Definition of Faith (p. 745)
