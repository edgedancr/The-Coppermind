Rousseau, Social Contract
- The foundation of the Social Contract is the general will.
	- General will is the public, unanimous will of the people.
- Our natural self is governed by self-preservation & pity for others.
- The Social Contract offers a new, better begining to a politically equal society.
- (margin: Ch. 8) Man becomes civil when duty replaces instincts, and right replaces appetite
- Civilized man: has developed and exercised faculties, broadened ideas, ennobled feelings, and an elevated soul.
- (margin: Bk. 4 Ch. 8) You need a religious belief to support the general will.
- Christians were good, but place religion over the state. He is critical of most Christians
- ★ Religion is needed, but it shouldn't really be a private belief. It needs to be public.
- Christianity is either too weak or too strong for the social contract, and they don't help the unification of the state.
- Each citizen needs a religion that pushes him to support the civil state.
- The dogmas of each religion ought to be simple, easy to follow.
- Each religion needs:
	- Divinity; some higher power
	- A life to come
	- To reward the just
	- Punishment to the unjust
	- Sanctity of the Social Contract and laws.
- No religion can be intolerant. Unless you keep the intolerance to yourself, it cannot be allowed.
