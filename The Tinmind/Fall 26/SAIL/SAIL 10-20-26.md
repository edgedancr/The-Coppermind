##### AI in Government
- AI fundamentally reshapes the intelligence production process
	1. Collection Enhancement
		- AI dramatically expands what analysts can see and hear—volume, speed, and cross-language translation. The analysts job shifts from finding information to judging it.
		- Real world example: NGA AI-assisted GEOINT and Babel Street/Palantir Gotham.
	2. Predictive Threat Modeling
		- AI Correlates historical data to forecast instability and attack likelihood. However, it is structurally blind to genuinely novel threats. The unknown unknown problem cannot be solved by simply adding more data.
		- Real world example: IARPA ACE Program and DHS Threat/Hazard Identification.
	3. Policy-Making Under AI
		- AI-generated assessments are now reaching the highest levels of decision-making. The core risk is automation bias—the tendency to over-trust confident AI outputs at the Oval Office level
		- Real World Example: CIA Internal LLMs (2023-24).
- The Analyst's Placement in the AI Pipeline
	- AI should be in the speed, volume, scale, and language areas.
		- Collection
		- Processing
		- Part of analyzing
	- Humans should be in the judgement, accountability, and policy areas.
		- Part of analyzing
		- Deciding
		- Acting
	- The INT Names Define the Output
		- OSINT, GEOINT, and SIGINT name the finished, human-analyzed product—not the raw collection act.
	- The Time Compression Trap
		- AI Dramatically compresses time at the Collect/Process states, creating immense institutional pressure to skip the crucial Analyze step.
	- The Oval Office Vulnerability
		- Automation bias at the Decide stage means confident AI outputs are often mistaken for verified intelligence.
	- If the Presidents daily brief contains AI-assisted analysis, should the President know?
##### AI in Warfighting
- AI Compresses the Lethal Engagement Cycle
	1. Battlefield Awareness & Sensor Fusion
		- AI Fuses air, land, sea, space, and cyber feeds into a near-real-time common operating picture. It compresses Jon Boyd OODA loop, allowing commanders to act inside the adversaries decision cycle.
	2. Targeting & The Kill chain
		- AI is now active in finding, fixing, tracking, and targeting. However, the decision to engage legally and ethically requires a human.
	3. Autonomous System & Adversarial threats
		- Peer competitors are removing humans from the engagement loop. Meanwhile, U.S. system remain vulnerable to data poisoning, sensor spoofing, and model manipulation.
- The Kill Chain: locating the human decision point
	- What AI and Humans do
		- AI: Classification, tracking, collateral estimation
			- Find
			- Fix
			- Track
			- Target
		- Human: Legal, moral, and policy requirement.
			- Engage
		- AI: Battle damage assessment algorithms
			- Assess
	- The LOAC Mandate
		- The Law of Armed conflict requires distinction and proportionality. Neither can be delegated to an algorithm.
	- The Compression Threat
		- The faster AI makes the early stages, the heavier the systemic pressure becomes to remove the human from the decision point.

##### The Cognitive Shield: Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH)
> Developed by Richards HEUER at the CIA (1999) to counteract confirmation bias.
- The Great Inversion
	- Normal Analysis / Advocacy
		- Intuition ➔ Conclusion ➔ Gather evidence to support it.
	- ACH Logic / Rigor
		- Generate ALL plausible hypotheses ➔ Use evidence to DISPROVE each ➔ The survivor is the answer.
- The AI Blindspot
	- LLMs inherently pattern-match to the most plausible, fluent narrative. By default, they do normal analysis (confirmation bias), not the elimination logic of ACH.
- The Six Stages of Hypothesis Disproval with ACH
	1. Hypotheses
		- Generate all plausible explanations (minimum of 4, including deeply uncomfortable ones).
	2. Evidence
		- List what is actually known verses what is merely assumed.
	3. Matrix (The Engine)
		- Intersect evidence and hypotheses. Mark as Supports (S), Contradicts (C), or Neither (N)
	4. Refine
	5. Conclude
	6. Sensitivity Check
- Executing ACH
	- Do
		- Force the generation of at least 4 hypotheses, explicitly including political or internal adversarial motives
		- Prompt the AI strictly step by step (hypotheses, then evidence, then matrix)
		- Demand honest contradiction marks. If the matrix has no contradictions, the AI is hallucinating agreement.
		- Run the step 6 sensitive check to identify the critical point of failure.
	- Don't
		- Never ask the AI for a conclusion first. It will instantly generate a plausible narrative, skipping analysis entirely.
		- Never trust a matrix polluted entire by S or N marks. That is confirmation bias rendered in table format.
		- Never mistake the AI's structural formatting fluency for analytic rigor.
		- Never confuse "would I sign this polished memo?" With "is this conclusion actually correct?"
