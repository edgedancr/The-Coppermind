## What It Is
Rob Johnston's *Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community: An Ethnographic Study* (CIA Center for the Study of Intelligence, 2005), a 173-page book with a foreword by Gregory F. Treverton and an afterword by Joseph Hayes, a retired CIA operations officer ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=8|Johnston, contents]]). Johnston, an anthropologist, began the study as a Director of Central Intelligence postdoctoral fellow four days after 9/11, using interviews (489 at the time of writing), direct and participant observation, and focus groups ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=13|Johnston, p. xiii]]). Part I gives his definitions and findings; Part II, a taxonomy of the variables that affect analysis and a systems model of the intelligence cycle; Part III, chapters on specific improvements and his recommendations; Part IV, notes on methodology. Wiki pages cite the book's printed page numbers, linked to the matching PDF page (e.g. "Johnston, p. 13"); co-written chapters and the foreword and afterword are cited by their authors.

The course's file name spells the author "Johnson"; the title page reads "Dr. Rob Johnston" ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=5|Johnston, title page]]).

## Reliability Notes
**Proposed B-2** (usually reliable; probably true). It is a CIA-published study based on hundreds of interviews and two years of observation, triangulated across methods ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=23|Johnston, pp. 3, 10]]), and reviewed by 50 peer reviewers ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=7|Johnston, acknowledgements]]). Johnston is candid about its limits: it was gathered under the shadow of 9/11 with no baseline to compare against ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=13|Johnston, p. xiii]]), he grew to empathize with analysts ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=30|Johnston, p. 10]]), and he calls it a progress report rather than a final report ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=20|Johnston, p. xx]]). The book carries the usual disclaimer that its views are the authors' and not official CIA positions ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=2|Johnston, disclaimer page]]). Its findings are interviewees' perceptions plus his interpretation, and they describe the Intelligence Community in the years right after 9/11. Harrison sets the final rating.
