## Slide 2

STYLE AND PURPOSE

Intelligence writing should be clear, concise, and informative.
Its goal is not to stimulate the reader’s more refined literary sensibilities with flowery language and creative descriptions, but to give the facts about a certain situation and either…- recommend a course of action based on those facts; or- explain the relevant implications of their analysis.

## Slide 3

WRITER’S JOB

Distill the situation’s most important pieces of information into the smallest amount of space with the greatest possible clarity.
“Omit needless [facts and] words!”
Analyze the relevant information.

## Slide 4

ORGANIZATION

The first sentence of should be the article’s Recommendation or Implications.
“The United States government has failed to adequately protect the U.S. from Chinese cyberwarfare and must take immediate action to neutralize this growing threat.”
The reader should be able to immediately understand the article’s purpose and perspective.

See ICD 203 Analytic Standards (2015), section D.6.e(5).

## Slide 6

PARAGRAPH STRUCTURE

Just as the opening sentence of an article or paper should state the article’s purpose, so also should function the first sentence of each paragraph.
The first sentences of each paragraph should form an outline of the piece’s key points and conclusions.

## Slide 7

SENTENCE STRUCTURE

Make each sentence count – no fluff, no unneeded description.
Use the active voice.
Avoid dangling prepositions and long dependent clauses.
Do not use qualifiers like “very” or “fantastic” or “horrible.” This reduces objective undertone.

## Slide 8

EXAMPLE

Despite the incredible efforts by the Bush administration to combat the horrible menace of al-Qaeda, Islamic extremism remains a tangible threat to U.S. national security. Not only this, but there is a growing threat on the southern border from violence generated by half-stoned Mexican drug cartels. Al-Qa'ida and similar terrorist groups like al-Shabaab do not lack motivation or the resources to launch attacks from. Because of there have been numerous credible reports to come in over the past several days showing that Iran three days ago sold a nuclear weapon to al-Qaeda, the United States should redirect its collection efforts to determine the location and intended detonation point of this bombing-awesome nuclear weapon.
