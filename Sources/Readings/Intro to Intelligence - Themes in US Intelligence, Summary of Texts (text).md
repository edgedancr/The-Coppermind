| | 7 | Process | Consumer-Producer Relations | The distinct line that is drawn between policy and intelligence leads to questions about how intelligence producers and consumers should relate to each other. The issue is the degree of proximity that is desirable. Two schools of thought have been evident in this debate in the United States. The distance school argues that the intelligence establishment should keep itself separate from the policy makers to avoid the risk of providing intelligence that lacks objectivity and favors or opposes one policy choice over others. This group believes that too close a relationship increases the risk of politicized intelligence. The proximate group argues that too great a distance raises the risk that the intelligence community would be less aware of policy makers' needs and therefore produce less useful intelligence. This view believes that proper training and internal reviews could avoid politicization of intelligence. | Theme Definition. | | 9 | Process | Denial and Deception | A targeted nation can use knowledge about the collection capabilities of an opponent to avoid collection (denial); the target can use the same knowledge to transmit false or incomplete information to a collector (deception). Such a display may reveal actual capabilities or may be staged to present a false image of strength. The use of decoys or dummies to fool imagery, or false communications to fool SIGINT, also falls into this category. The IC has devoted ever-increasing resources to the issue of denial and deception (D&D). Intelligence officials seek to know which nations are practicing D&OD, determine how they may have obtained the intelligence that made D&D possible, and then seek to design countermeasures to circumvent D&D. As more information about US intelligence sources and methods becomes publicly available, D&D is an increasing constraint on US collection. D&D is also a complex analytical issues. One cannot simply assume that failed collection is a result of D&D. The completely innocent state and the state with very good D&D both may look identical to the observer. | Theme Definition. | | 10 | Process | Mirror Imaging | Assuming that other states or individuals will act just the way a particular country or person does. It assumes a certain level of shared rationality and leaves no room for irrational actions or actions based on different or unfamiliar norms. One of the most frequent flaws of analysis, that assumes "they're just like us." Fails to take into account differences of motivation, perception, or action based on national differences, subtle differences of circumstances, different rationales, or the absence of rationale. rThe opposite of mirror imaging is sometimes referred to as "clientism" (or 'going native'). Analysts can spend their efforts apologizing for the actions of the nations they cover instead of analyzing them. | | 12 | Tech | Heavy Reliance on Technology for Analysis & Dissemination | American info-warriors will need ever quicker answers. "It was there five minutes ago" may have been good enough for those warriors in Desert Storm when they confronted an Iraqi tank column; it will not be nearly good enough for their counterparts in 2015 facing threats from fifth-generation Scud missiles. This traditional view of intelligence dates from the postwar and Cold War eras and presumes obolete notions about consumers and their needs, as well as static standoffishness in relations between intelligence and policy. In the intelligence world of today, and still more tomorrow, would-be consumers will not throw their requests over the transom, then wait for their answer to be delivered the same way. And intelligence will not be used to support discrete "decisions" taken by "the government." Rather, it will provide a stream of information to a variety of coalition partners, actual and potential. Information is available in torrents, the response time of collectors is becoming shorter and shorter, and communication now permits intercepted signals to be relayed to US pilots' cockpits as the mission takes off -- or permits Washington-based policy-makers or analysts to be in direct contact, continuous if they choose, with collectors of information abroad. The new technical possibilities open the way to a revolution in intelligence...but the implications for existing organizations and structure are radical. The IT revolution is not an end in itself, at least for intelligence. Instead, it is--or should be--a means by which the intelligence community can perform certain tasks more efficiently...Technology is a means to this end, but it cannot make sharing happen on its own. ..To date, the search for improved analytical tools has not been particularly successful, and for some it has taken on the aspect of a hunt for the Holy Grail. | Theme Definition.
