Collection Discipline Overview

| Collection Discipline | Strength | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HUMINT. From human sources. Includes overt data collected by personnel in diplomatic and consular posts, as well as clandestine sources of information, debriefings of foreign nationals and U.S. citizens who travel abroad, official contacts with foreign governments, and direct observation | Insights into plans, intentions Relatively inexpensive | Risks lives, political fallout Requires time to acquire & validate sources Problems of dangles, false feeds, double agents |
| OSINT. From open sources. Publicly available information appearing in print or electronic forms, including radio, television, newspapers, journals, the Internet, commercial databases, videos, graphics, and drawings | Readily available Usefulness as place to start all collection | Large volume Credibility of sources Less likely to offer insights available from clandestine INTs |
| GEOINT. Information describing, visually depicting, and accurately locating physical features and human activities on the Earth. Examples of GEOINT products include imagery, analyses, maps, and navigation charts and GEOINT data is collected by satellites, planes, UAVs, and people with cameras. Imagery intelligence (IMINT, including non-literal imagery) is a subset of GEOINT. | Graphic and compelling Familiar to policy-makers Ready availability of some targets Done remotely | Requires interpretation Static snapshot Problems of Wx, spoofing, D&D Expensive Overly compelling |
| SIGINT. Signals Intelligence is information gathered from data transmissions, Includes communications intelligence (COMINT), electronic intelligence (ELINT), and foreign instrumentation signals intelligence (FISINT). | Insight to plans, intentions Voluminous material Military targets tend to communicate in regular patterns Can be done remotely | Signals may be encrypted or encoded Voluminous material May use silence, secure lines, spoofing Expensive |
| MASINT. Measurement and Signature Intelligence is information produced by quantitative and qualitative analysis of physical attributes of targets and events in order to characterize and identify them. | Extremely useful for issues such as proliferation Can be done remotely | Expensive Little understood by most users Requires extensive processing and exploitation |
| FININT. Financial intelligence is the gathering of information about the financial affairs of entities of interest, to understand their nature and capabilities, and predict their intentions. Generally the term applies in the context of law enforcement | Extremely useful for issues such as counterterrorism and proliferation Can reveal covert relationships Can be done remotely | Often depends on fragile sources of information Requires time for actions to be effective Implemented by commercial entities |
| ABI/POL/ADTECH. Acronyms.Definitions: ABI – Activity Based Intel. A methodology that rapidly integrates data from multiple sources to discover relevant patterns, determine and identify change, and understanding through spatial and temporal correlation across multiple data sets. POL – Pattern of Life. The specific set of behaviors and movements associated with a particular entity over a given period of time. ADTECH – Advertisement Technology. “Digital Exhaust” or “behavioral surplus” (Zuboff) left behind by people traversing the digital info domain (e.g., websites visited; ads looked at; and metadata from electronic communications) or surveillance of users of electronics with or without their awareness and permission to create behavioral prediction products. | Can reveal covert relationships Can be done remotely Extremely useful for issues such as CI | Large volume Requires interpretation Expensive Implemented by commercial entities Sources fragile if targets become aware of surveillance Incomplete coverage where internet usage is limited or avoided Varying legal constraints in US and Europe |
