Clarifying with “SEE-IT”

Objective: Get it clear in your own mind; and help clearly communicate it to others.

S: State it--brief, clear statement; definition or thesis; authoritative source.
“The question or topic is …”

E: Elaborate--explain it more fully, in your own words but at greater length so the reader gets more of the full content by explaining in more depth and detail.
“In other words…”

E: Exemplify--give a good example; a well-chosen one that provides additional insights. Best if chosen personally, fits well with the statement and with the elaboration, and is appropriate for the intended audience.
“For example, …”

IT: Illustrate It--give an illustration—metaphor, simile, analogy, diagram, concept map, etc.—to clarify by providing a mental or actual picture.
“It is like…”

Potential Applications of SEE-IT:

Writing: Developing each main idea and developing it in your paper with an SEE-I.

Testing: You will demonstrate a firm grasp of concepts or ideas if you can accurately narrate an SEE-I for them.

Thinking About Thinking

Functions of Critical Thinking

Purpose

Take time to state the purpose clearly

Distinguish your purpose from related purposes

Check periodically to be sure you are still on target

Choose significant and realistic purposes

Question at Issue

State the question at issue clearly and precisely

Express the question in several ways to clarify its meaning and scope

Break the question into sub-questions

Distinguish definitive answers from matters of opinion and those requiring consideration from multiple viewpoints

Assumptions

What are the main assumptions?

What is the theory on this topic or discipline?

Implications & Consequences

Identify the implications of the conclusions

List the costs and benefits

What would logically follow (pro/con)

Likelihood of positive/negative consequences

Information

Identify the available data and sources

Relevant information used or not?

What is information vs interpretations?

How well is the information organized?

Concepts

What concepts are in play—defined?

Main or critical concept to the argument?

Refine, compare, contrast concepts

Conclusions & Interpretations

State the solutions, inferences, interpretations

Compare, contrast to other views

Identify trends, patterns, schools of thought

Point of View

Define author’s vs alternative POV’s

Compare, contrast, evaluate POV
