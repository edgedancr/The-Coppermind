# Step 4 — Analysis & Linchpins

## Objective

Interpret the completed matrix using disconfirmation logic. Identify which hypotheses survive, which evidence items are driving the conclusion, and where the analysis is most fragile.

## Prompt

```
Analyze the completed ACH matrix. Follow these steps in order:

1. RANK HYPOTHESES BY INCONSISTENCY.
   The hypothesis with the fewest I and II ratings is the leading candidate — not because it has the most evidence in its favor, but because the least evidence argues against it. Present the ranking from most likely to least likely, with the I/II count for each.

2. IDENTIFY THE MOST DIAGNOSTIC EVIDENCE.
   List the 3-5 evidence items with the highest diagnosticity ratings from the matrix. For each, explain in one sentence WHY it is diagnostic — what does it distinguish between?

3. IDENTIFY LINCHPIN EVIDENCE.
   A linchpin is an evidence item where changing its rating would change the relative ranking of the top hypotheses. For each of the top 2 ranked hypotheses, identify:
   - Which specific evidence item(s) are linchpins?
   - What is the current rating, and what would it need to change to in order to shift the ranking?
   - How confident are we in this evidence? Assess as High / Medium / Low, with a one-sentence justification.
   - Is this evidence single-source or corroborated?
   - What additional collection or research could strengthen or weaken it?

4. IDENTIFY INFORMATION GAPS.
   List any hypotheses that are difficult to evaluate primarily because of missing information rather than contradictory evidence. For each:
   - What specific information is missing?
   - What collection or research would fill the gap?
   - Is the hypothesis being kept alive by absence of evidence (which is not the same as evidence in its favor)?

Do NOT state a final conclusion. We will stress-test the analysis first.

Checkpoint.
```

## Analyst Checkpoint

- **Does the ranking make sense?** Compare it to your own intuition. If they diverge, that's not automatically a problem — ACH is designed to challenge intuition — but examine why.
- **Are linchpins correctly identified?** Mentally change the rating of each linchpin and confirm it would actually shift the outcome.
- **Are confidence assessments honest?** If a linchpin is rated "High confidence" but comes from a single unverified source, push back.
- **Are information gaps actionable?** Can you identify specific collection requirements or research tasks that would fill them? If so, note these for the final report.
