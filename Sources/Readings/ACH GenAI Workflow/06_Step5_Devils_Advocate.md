# Step 5 — Devil's Advocate & Sensitivity Check

## Objective

Actively attempt to break the leading hypothesis. This step exists because ACH, even when done well, can still produce a result that feels rigorous but rests on fragile assumptions. The devil's advocate step is not optional.

## Prompt

```
Conduct a devil's advocate and sensitivity review of the analysis.

1. ATTACK THE LEADING HYPOTHESIS.
   Construct the strongest possible argument AGAINST the current leading hypothesis. Assume it is wrong. What evidence would you expect to see that is not present? What alternative interpretation of the existing evidence would undermine it? Be aggressive — your job here is to find weaknesses, not to be balanced.

2. DEFEND THE RUNNER-UP.
   Construct the strongest possible argument FOR the second-ranked hypothesis. What would need to be true for it to be the correct explanation? How plausible is that scenario? What evidence currently rated as Inconsistent with it could be reinterpreted?

3. TEST LINCHPIN ASSUMPTIONS.
   For each linchpin identified in Step 4:
   - What if this evidence is wrong, outdated, fabricated, or misinterpreted?
   - Specifically change its rating in the matrix and describe how the overall ranking shifts.
   - How confident should we be that this evidence is solid?

4. CHECK FOR DECEPTION.
   Could an adversary have planted, manipulated, or selectively revealed evidence to lead analysts toward the current leading hypothesis? Which evidence items are most vulnerable to adversarial manipulation? If deception is plausible, does it change the ranking?

5. CHECK FOR MIRROR IMAGING.
   Are we assuming the actor behind this situation thinks the way we do, values what we value, or would act the way we would act? Identify any ratings in the matrix that depend on assumptions about the adversary's rationality, risk tolerance, or objectives. Flag these.

6. REASSESS.
   After completing steps 1-5, does the ranking of hypotheses change? Present one of the following:
   - "Ranking holds" — with a brief explanation of why the leading hypothesis survived the challenge.
   - "Ranking shifts" — with the new ranking and the specific reason for the change.
   - "Confidence reduced" — the ranking is the same, but the confidence level should be lowered. Explain why.

Checkpoint.
```

## Analyst Checkpoint

- **Did the devil's advocate surface anything new?** If the attack on the leading hypothesis was weak or perfunctory, push the AI harder: *"That argument is too gentle. Assume H1 is definitively wrong and explain what happened instead."*
- **Did the deception check raise real concerns?** In intelligence analysis, adversarial deception is not a theoretical exercise. If specific evidence items are vulnerable to manipulation, note this for the final report.
- **Did mirror imaging appear?** If any matrix ratings assumed the adversary would behave "rationally" by your standards, those ratings may need revision.
- **What is your final assessment of confidence?** This is the last checkpoint before the report. Decide whether the analysis warrants High, Moderate, or Low confidence, and be prepared to justify that assessment.
