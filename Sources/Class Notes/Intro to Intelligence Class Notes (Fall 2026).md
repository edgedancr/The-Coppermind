##### Executive Order 12333: The Architecture of U.S. Intelligence
- Signed by Reagan in 1981. It defines the power, restraint, and ecosystem of the modern IC.
- Updated in 2003 after 9/11, in 2004, and again in 2008.
- Part 1: Definitions
	- Section 3.5: Intelligence
		- Section 3.5(e) — Foreign Intelligence
			- Information relating to the capabilities, intentions, or activities of foreign governments, foreign organizations, foreign persons, or international terrorists, regardless of how or where it was collected.
		- Section 3.5(i) — National Intelligence
			- All intelligence, regardless of the source from which derived, gathered within or outside the United States, that involves threats to the U.S. or its interests.
		- The Post 9/11 Evolution
			- "National Intelligence" reflects a paradigm shift. It legally establishes that all intelligence is a shared enterprise—regardless of whether it was collected domestically by the FBI or overseas by the CIA—demanding total integration to the counter transnational threats.
	- Section 1.3: The Director of National Intelligence
		- Section 1.3(c) — Non-Abrogation
			- The DNI directs the implementation of the National Intelligence Program but does not command the day-to-day operations of all agencies. The statutory authority of departmental heads (e.g., Secretary of Defense, Attorney General) remains intact.
		- Responsibilities:
			- Head of the Intelligence Community and unifier of the national intelligence effort.
				- Principal adviser to the President
				- Principal adviser to the National Security Council (NSC)
				- Principal adviser to the Homeland Security Council
			- Sec 1.3(b)(1)
				- Set the priorities and guidance for the entire IC to ensure timely and effective collection, processing, analysis, and dissemination.
			- Sec 1.3(b)(17)
				- Determine requirements, direct the tasking of national collection assets, and resolve operational conflicts between IC elements.
	- Section 1.7: The Agency Landscape & Operational coordination
		- Independent
			- ODNI, CIA
		- Department of Defense
			- NSA, NGA, NRO, DIA, Intel elements of the Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps
		- Departmental
			- FBI/DEA (Justice), INR (State), OIA (Treasury), I&C (Energy), I&A / Coast Guard (Homeland Security).
	- Functional Managers of Intel
		- HUMINT
			- CIA Director is designated as the Functional Manager for human intelligence (Sec 1.3(b)(12)(A)(ii))
		- SIGINT
			- NSA Director designated as Functional Manager for signal intelligence (Sec 1.3(b)(12)(A)(i))
		- GEOINT
			- NGA Director designated as Functional Manager for geospatial intelligence
		- The Role of a Functional Manager (Sec 1.3(b)(12)(A)(iii))
			- Advise the DNI on resource management and collection gaps while setting the absolute standards for training, tradecraft, and technical architecture across the entire IC.
- Part 2: Parameters
	- Restraint 1 — Undisclosed Participation (Sec 2.9)
		- No IC element may join or participate in any U.S. organization without disclosing their intelligence affiliation.
		- Exceptions
			- Only permitted if essential to lawful purposes, approved by the Attorney General, and not undertaken to influence the organization's activities.
	- Restraint 2 — Human Experimentation (Sec 2.10)
		- Requirement: Subject's informed consent must be documented.
	- Restraint 3 — The Assassination Ban (Sec 2.11)
		- "No person employed by or acting on behalf of the United States Government shall engage in, or conspire to engage in, assassination."
		- Stemming from the 1970s Church Committee revelations, this definitely separates U.S. intel statecraft from unsanctioned lethal action, forcing complex legal calculations around the definition of "assassination" versus lawful targeting in armed conflict.
	- Sections 2.12 and 2.13
		- Restraint 4 — Indirect Participation (Sec 2.12)
			- No element of the IC shall participate in or request any person to undertake activities forbidden by this order.
		- Restraint 5 — Domestic Cover Action (Sec 2.13)
			- No covert action may be conducted which is intended to influence Unite States political processes, public opinion, policies, or media.
		- Together, these two sections prevent the IC from outsourcing illegal acts to third-party proxies, and strictly firewall foreign covert influence operations from generating domestic political feedback. This is intended to close the loopholes.

##### Style and Purpose in Intel Writing
- Intelligence writing should be **clear**, **concise**, **informative**, **timely**, and **tailored**.
- Its goal is not to stimulate the reader's more refined literary sensibilities with flowery language and creative descriptions, but to give the facts about a certain situation and either
	- recommend a course of action based on those fact; or
	- explain the relevant implications of their analysis.
- The first sentence should be the article's **Recommendations** or **Implications.**
	- We are not writing a story that tries to keep the reader engaged. We are trying to provide information as fast as possible.
- The reader should be able to immediately understand the article's **purpose** and **perspective.**
- Paragraph Structure
	- First sentence should summarize the paragraph.
	- The difference between the topic and concluding sentence is that the topic sentence assumes that the reader doesn't have the paragraph's information yet, while the concluding sentence assumes that the reader has, which means you can include different information than the topic sentence.
- Sentence structure
	- Make each sentence count — no fluff, no unneeded description.
	- Use the active voice.
	- Avoid dangling prepositions and log dependent clause.
	- Do not use qualifiers like "very" or "horrible." We need specifics, not opinions.
- Become your own ruthless editor. Leave your writing after you are finished, at least for 30 minutes, then come back and re-read it. You will find improvements you can make.

		- The IC gathers the raw data used to produce finished intel products. Collection can be from open sources or from clandestine sources (technical means, etc...)
	- Processing
		- The IC converts the information that is collected into a usable format (e.g., language translation or decryption)
	- Analysis and Production
		- The IC analyze processed information to turn it into finished intel (e.g., drafting reports, evaluating reliability of sources, or resolving data conflicts.)
	- Dissemination
- OSINT — Open Source Intelligence
	- Technically, the CIA is the functional manager of OSINT. This means that they have the responsibility for the community to advance and carry forward the development of tools, new technology, training, etc... for not only their own agency but for the whole IC. 
	- Anything that is publicly available to a standard citizen.
	- How do we handle personal info?
		- Very carefully.
		- It must be anonymized. You can cite information, but you must use pseudonyms, redact, or otherwise protect personal information. 
	- OSINT does not require specialized *collection* techniques.
- HUMINT — Human Intelligence
	- CIA is the functional manager of HUMINT.
	- Data from human sources. This includes overt collection from personnel, clandestine operations, political intrigue, official contacts with foreign government, and direct observation.
	- Strengths
		- This is where you can get insights into plans and intentions of people.
		- The collection process for HUMINT is monetarily cheap, but the human cost can be very high.
	- Weaknesses
		- Humans can be unreliable.
		- Often puts lives at risk during collection.
- SIGINT — Signals Intelligence
	- COMINT — Communications Intelligence
		- Any communications media that is digitized or broadcast in analog form
	- ELINT
		- Anything that is emitted from electronics, either from an antenna, or even unintendedly from its own private signals.
	- FIS — Foreign Instrumentation Signals
		- How we stay informed about foreign abilities and 
	- The difference between SIGINT and MASINT is that SIGINT tries to interpret signals, while MASINT is trying to prove that the signals exist.

	- Radio frequencies, Radar, etc...
- GEOINT
	- Information describing visually depicting events.
