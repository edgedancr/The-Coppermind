## Executive Summary
This Intro to Intelligence handout teaches SEE-IT, a four-step method for making an idea clear in your own mind and to others: state it, elaborate it, exemplify it, and illustrate it.[^1] It suggests using the method to develop each main idea in a paper and to show a firm grasp of concepts on tests.[^2] It works an example on critical thinking, defined with CriticalThinking.org as disciplined, comprehensive, standards-based thought.[^3] It then lists Gerald Nosich's elements of critical thinking, from purpose to point of view,[^4] and a table of intellectual standards with questions.[^5] A final section adapts the method to best-case and worst-case scenarios.[^6]

## Main Points
- Each step has a sentence starter: "The question or topic is…", "In other words…", "For example…", "It is like…".[^1]
- Critical thinking assumes the quality of our thinking shapes the quality of our lives.[^3]

## Themes
- Clarity before argument.
- Thinking about one's own thinking.

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: "You will demonstrate a firm grasp of concepts or ideas if you can accurately narrate an SEE-I for them".[^2]
