## Executive Summary
This Intro to Intelligence handout teaches SEE-IT, a four-step method for making an idea clear in your own mind and to others: state it, elaborate it, exemplify it, and illustrate it ([[SRC - SEE-IT Checklist for Critical Thinking|SEE-IT Checklist, 'Clarifying with SEE-IT']]). It suggests using the method to develop each main idea in a paper and to show a firm grasp of concepts on tests ([[SRC - SEE-IT Checklist for Critical Thinking|SEE-IT Checklist, 'Potential Applications of SEE-IT']]). It works an example on critical thinking, defined with CriticalThinking.org as disciplined, comprehensive, standards-based thought ([[SRC - SEE-IT Checklist for Critical Thinking|SEE-IT Checklist, 'SEE-IT Example']]). It then lists Gerald Nosich's elements of critical thinking, from purpose to point of view ([[SRC - SEE-IT Checklist for Critical Thinking|SEE-IT Checklist, 'Functions of Critical Thinking']]), and a table of intellectual standards with questions ([[SRC - SEE-IT Checklist for Critical Thinking|SEE-IT Checklist, 'Standards for Critical Thinking']]). A final section adapts the method to best-case and worst-case scenarios ([[SRC - SEE-IT Checklist for Critical Thinking|SEE-IT Checklist, 'Clarifying with SEE-I']]).

## Main Points
- Each step has a sentence starter: "The question or topic is…", "In other words…", "For example…", "It is like…" ([[SRC - SEE-IT Checklist for Critical Thinking|SEE-IT Checklist, 'Clarifying with SEE-IT']]).
- Critical thinking assumes the quality of our thinking shapes the quality of our lives ([[SRC - SEE-IT Checklist for Critical Thinking|SEE-IT Checklist, 'SEE-IT Example']]).

## Themes
- Clarity before argument.
- Thinking about one's own thinking.
