## Executive Summary
Jim Mitre and Joel B. Predd of RAND argue that the emergence of [[Artificial General Intelligence|artificial general intelligence]] (AGI) is plausible and should be taken seriously by the U.S. national security community, though its pace and consequences are deeply uncertain ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "About This Paper"]]). They contrast AI with the atomic bomb: frontier AI has not yet had an "atom-splitting moment" showing a clear path to a wonder weapon ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "opening"]]). The paper names five hard problems: wonder weapons, systemic shifts in power, nonexperts empowered to build weapons of mass destruction, artificial entities with agency, and instability ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "About This Paper"]]). The authors offer the five as a common language and a rubric for judging strategies, because progress on one can undermine another ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "About This Paper"]]). They assess current U.S. strategy as constructive but inadequate for a disruptive breakthrough ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "Toward a Robust Strategy"]]).

## Main Points
- Uncertainty about scaling, thresholds, and timing is "endemic," so a strategy over-optimized for one future is high-risk ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "Endemic Uncertainty"]]).
- Each problem rests on different assumptions, from sudden breakthrough to gradual adoption ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "The Five Problems"]]).
- Relying heavily on compute and export controls could prove brittle ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "Toward a Robust Strategy"]]).

## Themes
- Planning under uncertainty rather than prediction.
- Historical analogy: the Manhattan Project, the nuclear arms race, and the Industrial Revolution.

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: develop high-regret policy options and contingency plans in advance, and run scenario exercises on post-AGI futures ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "Toward a Robust Strategy"]]).
- [inferred] The authors want the five problems weighed together rather than one at a time.
