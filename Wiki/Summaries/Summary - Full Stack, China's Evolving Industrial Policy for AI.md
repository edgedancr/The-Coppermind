## Executive Summary
Kyle Chan and four RAND co-authors examine how Beijing is using industrial policy across the full [[AI Technology Stack|AI technology stack]], from chips to applications, toward its goal of global AI leadership by 2030 ([[RAND - Full Stack, China's Evolving Industrial Policy for AI (2025).pdf|Chan et al., "opening"]]). They judge that the policy will likely accelerate China's AI progress, especially through research, talent, subsidized compute, and applications ([[RAND - Full Stack, China's Evolving Industrial Policy for AI (2025).pdf|Chan et al., "opening"]]). They also find waste, such as inefficient chip allocation, and bottlenecks that state support can't easily overcome, chiefly U.S.-led [[Semiconductor Export Controls|export controls]] ([[RAND - Full Stack, China's Evolving Industrial Policy for AI (2025).pdf|Chan et al., "opening"]]). They note that Beijing frames AI around economic and industrial applications rather than a "race to AGI" ([[RAND - Full Stack, China's Evolving Industrial Policy for AI (2025).pdf|Chan et al., "Beijing's AI Policy Goals and Tools"]]). Their bottom line is that China will likely remain at least a close second to the United States ([[RAND - Full Stack, China's Evolving Industrial Policy for AI (2025).pdf|Chan et al., "opening"]]).

## Main Points
- Three bottlenecks decide success: domestic chips, talent, and energy ([[RAND - Full Stack, China's Evolving Industrial Policy for AI (2025).pdf|Chan et al., "Will China's AI Policies Work?"]]).
- The policy may backfire by forcing immature domestic tools, misallocating chips, and drawing foreign restrictions ([[RAND - Full Stack, China's Evolving Industrial Policy for AI (2025).pdf|Chan et al., "Will China's AI Policies Work?"]]).
- Private firms such as DeepSeek, not state firms, have led China's AI development ([[RAND - Full Stack, China's Evolving Industrial Policy for AI (2025).pdf|Chan et al., "Will China's AI Policies Work?"]]).
- State support reaches from energy and chips to open source, data, and applications ([[RAND - Full Stack, China's Evolving Industrial Policy for AI (2025).pdf|Chan et al., "China's Layered State Support for AI"]]).

## Themes
- Self-reliance under export controls.
- Blurred lines between public and private AI development.

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: export controls slow China's AI development but are unlikely to halt it ([[RAND - Full Stack, China's Evolving Industrial Policy for AI (2025).pdf|Chan et al., "U.S. Export Controls to Constrain China's Compute"]]).
- [inferred] The authors locate China's AI strength mainly in its private sector, with state policy as an amplifier.
