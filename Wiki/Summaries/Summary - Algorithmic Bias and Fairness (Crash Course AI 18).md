## Executive Summary
The episode argues that because algorithms are created by people and use people's data, biases in the real world are mimicked or even exaggerated by AI systems ([[Crash Course AI 18 - Algorithmic Bias and Fairness (transcript)|Crash Course AI, intro]]). It separates bias, which everyone has, from discrimination, which can be prevented ([[Crash Course AI 18 - Algorithmic Bias and Fairness (transcript)|Crash Course AI, intro]]). It names five types of [[Algorithmic Bias|algorithmic bias]] ([[Crash Course AI 18 - Algorithmic Bias and Fairness (transcript)|Crash Course AI, intro]]) and illustrates them with image search, facial recognition, essay-grading AI, [[PredPol]], and Microsoft's Tay chatbot ([[Crash Course AI 18 - Algorithmic Bias and Fairness (transcript)|Crash Course AI, ch. 2–6]]). A hypothetical hiring system, "HireMe!," shows how a feedback loop and an unexplained correlation can produce unfair or illegal decisions ([[Crash Course AI 18 - Algorithmic Bias and Fairness (transcript)|Crash Course AI, ch. 7]]). The episode concludes that humans must stay critical of AI recommendations and monitor them for bias ([[Crash Course AI 18 - Algorithmic Bias and Fairness (transcript)|Crash Course AI, ch. 8]]).

## Main Points
- Training data can carry hidden social biases, and [[Protected Class|protected classes]] can re-enter through [[Correlated Feature|correlated features]] such as zip code ([[Crash Course AI 18 - Algorithmic Bias and Fairness (transcript)|Crash Course AI, ch. 2]]).
- Too few examples of a group, and qualities that are hard to quantify, both degrade predictions ([[Crash Course AI 18 - Algorithmic Bias and Fairness (transcript)|Crash Course AI, ch. 3–4]]).
- Algorithms can create [[Positive Feedback Loop|feedback loops]], and people can manipulate training data on purpose ([[Crash Course AI 18 - Algorithmic Bias and Fairness (transcript)|Crash Course AI, ch. 5–6]]).
- Proposed remedies: [[Algorithmic Transparency|transparency]], more data on protected classes, and critical interpretation of outputs ([[Crash Course AI 18 - Algorithmic Bias and Fairness (transcript)|Crash Course AI, ch. 8]]).

## Themes
- Bias enters through data and design, not only through intent.
- The tension between collecting protected-class data to check for discrimination and protecting those groups' privacy.

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: be critical of AI recommendations instead of accepting that "the computer said so," and advocate for careful interpretation of algorithmic outputs ([[Crash Course AI 18 - Algorithmic Bias and Fairness (transcript)|Crash Course AI, ch. 8]]).
- [inferred] Responsibility for fairness stays with the people who build and use the system.
