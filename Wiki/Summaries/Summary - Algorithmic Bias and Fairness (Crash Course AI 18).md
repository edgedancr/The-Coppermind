## Executive Summary
The episode argues that because algorithms are created by people and use people's data, biases in the real world are mimicked or even exaggerated by AI systems.[^1] It separates bias, which everyone has, from discrimination, which can be prevented.[^1] It names five types of [[Algorithmic Bias|algorithmic bias]][^1] and illustrates them with image search, facial recognition, essay-grading AI, [[PredPol]], and Microsoft's Tay chatbot.[^2] A hypothetical hiring system, "HireMe!," shows how a feedback loop and an unexplained correlation can produce unfair or illegal decisions.[^3] The episode concludes that humans must stay critical of AI recommendations and monitor them for bias.[^4]

## Main Points
- Training data can carry hidden social biases, and [[Protected Class|protected classes]] can re-enter through [[Correlated Feature|correlated features]] such as zip code.[^5]
- Too few examples of a group, and qualities that are hard to quantify, both degrade predictions.[^6]
- Algorithms can create [[Positive Feedback Loop|feedback loops]], and people can manipulate training data on purpose.[^7]
- Proposed remedies: [[Algorithmic Transparency|transparency]], more data on protected classes, and critical interpretation of outputs.[^4]

## Themes
- Bias enters through data and design, not only through intent.
- The tension between collecting protected-class data to check for discrimination and protecting those groups' privacy.

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: be critical of AI recommendations instead of accepting that "the computer said so," and advocate for careful interpretation of algorithmic outputs.[^4]
- [inferred] Responsibility for fairness stays with the people who build and use the system.
