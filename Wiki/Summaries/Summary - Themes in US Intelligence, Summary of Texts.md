## Executive Summary
This Intro to Intelligence spreadsheet defines 13 recurring themes in U.S. intelligence, in four categories: organization, people, process, and technology.[^1] Its definitions quote two textbooks, Mark Lowenthal's *Intelligence: From Secrets to Policy* and Gregory Treverton's *Reshaping National Intelligence*, with page references.[^2] The organization themes cover culture, collection synergy, and the deliberately redundant analytic agencies.[^3] The people themes cover strategic vs. tactical needs, how policymakers use intelligence, and [[Politicized Intelligence|politicization]].[^4] The process themes cover the producer-consumer relationship, secrecy vs. openness, [[Denial and Deception|denial and deception]], and [[Mirror Imaging|mirror imaging]].[^5] The technology themes cover reliance on technology for collection and for analysis, and encryption.[^6]

## Main Points
- Treverton: the CIA's standard is "good enough to act," the FBI's "beyond a reasonable doubt".[^7]
- Lowenthal: an innocent state and one with good denial and deception can look identical.[^8]
- Treverton: many [[Intelligence Error and Intelligence Failure|intelligence failures]] come from assumptions, often from mirror imaging.[^9]

## Themes
- Persistent tensions rather than solvable problems.
- Technology as a tool, not a solution.

## Key Takeaways
- [inferred] The themes give a common vocabulary for analyzing any intelligence case.
