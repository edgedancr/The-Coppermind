## Executive Summary
This Intro to Intelligence spreadsheet defines 13 recurring themes in U.S. intelligence, in four categories: organization, people, process, and technology ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, themes 1–13]]). Its definitions quote two textbooks, Mark Lowenthal's *Intelligence: From Secrets to Policy* and Gregory Treverton's *Reshaping National Intelligence*, with page references ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary]]). The organization themes cover culture, collection synergy, and the deliberately redundant analytic agencies ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, themes 1–3]]). The people themes cover strategic vs. tactical needs, how policymakers use intelligence, and politicization ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, themes 4–6]]). The process themes cover the producer-consumer relationship, secrecy vs. openness, denial and deception, and mirror imaging ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, themes 7–10]]). The technology themes cover reliance on technology for collection and for analysis, and encryption ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, themes 11–13]]).

## Main Points
- Treverton: the CIA's standard is "good enough to act," the FBI's "beyond a reasonable doubt" ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 1]]).
- Lowenthal: an innocent state and one with good denial and deception can look identical ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 9]]).
- Treverton: many intelligence failures come from assumptions, often from mirror imaging ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 10]]).

## Themes
- Persistent tensions rather than solvable problems.
- Technology as a tool, not a solution.
