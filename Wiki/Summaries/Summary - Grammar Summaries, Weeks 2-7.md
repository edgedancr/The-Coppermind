## Executive Summary
Intro to Intelligence's "I2I Grammar Summaries" is a week-by-week glossary of terms for weeks 2 through 7 of the course.[^1] Most entries define terms from Mark Lowenthal's *Intelligence: From Secrets to Policy*, chapter by chapter, with page numbers.[^2] Others summarize the 9/11 Commission Report, Kaiser and Pherson's guide to analytic writing, an interview with former House Intelligence Committee chairman Mike Rogers, and, in week 7, readings on cyber, the Internet of Things, and artificial intelligence.[^1] Most weeks end with discussion questions and answers, on topics such as the [[National Intelligence Strategy]], congressional oversight, and [[Just War Theory|just war theory]].[^3] The document states no thesis; [inferred] it works as the course's study vocabulary.

## Main Points
- Lowenthal treats intelligence as "proximate reality" rather than truth;[^4] see [[Intelligence (National Security)]].
- The IC's machinery: budget programs, the [[National Intelligence Priorities Framework|priorities framework]], and directives.[^4]
- Analysis and its pitfalls: [[Analytic Confidence|confidence levels]], [[Competitive Analysis|competitive analysis]], [[Groupthink|groupthink]], and [[Politicized Intelligence|politicization]].[^5]
- Collection, counterintelligence, and oversight: the disciplines' strengths and weaknesses, [[Counterintelligence|counterintelligence]], and the congressional committees.[^6]
- The [[September 11 Attacks (2001)|9/11 attacks]], with their background and the problems in sharing and warning beforehand.[^7]

## Themes
- [inferred] Tension between protecting secrets and sharing them, as in [[Originator Controlled|ORCON]], [[Stovepipe|stovepipes]], and [[The Wall (FBI)|"The Wall"]].
- [inferred] The pull between what policymakers want and what intelligence can give.

## Key Takeaways
- No explicit call to action. [inferred] Students should be able to define each term and place it in the IC's structure and process.
