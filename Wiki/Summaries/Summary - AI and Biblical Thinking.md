## Executive Summary
In a Bible Study Fellowship (BSF) interview that opens with "we need to think biblically about AI," [[John Lennox]] weighs AI's benefits and dangers as a Christian.[^1] He cites a report that 96% of students use ChatGPT, which he finds useful "provided you check it."[^2] He argues that machines "only simulate intelligence," calls the worship of AI "sheer idolatry," and ties [[Transhumanism|transhumanism]] to the biblical temptation to "be as gods."[^3] He doubts full [[Artificial General Intelligence|artificial general intelligence]] (AGI) is possible without understanding consciousness.[^3] He grounds human worth in the [[Imago Dei|image of God]], not in intelligence,[^4] and urges parents to keep AI a servant, not a master.[^5] No explicit thesis; [inferred] Christians should use AI as a tool but not trust it in place of God.

## Main Points
- Technology is "a sharp knife": medical diagnosis is a benefit; autonomous drone swarms show the downside.[^3]
- On the control problem he asks "whose norms?" and urges Christians to "sit at the table."[^6]
- He offers the [[Golden Rule]] and the value of human life as common moral ground.[^4]
- Christ's resurrection, he says, already answers transhumanism's hope of defeating death.[^7]

## Themes
- Secular and Christian worldviews contesting AI's ethics and meaning.
- AI as a tool to use versus something to trust or worship.

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: parents should discuss AI's uses and downsides with their children and spend time with them;[^5][^8] Lennox urges [[Technological Fasting|technological fasting]].[^9]
- Explicit: believers should meet anxiety about the future with trust in Christ and attention to scripture.[^10]
