## Executive Summary
"A Disneyland Without Children" is a short story by L Rudolf L ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, part 1]]). Two astronauts, sent to find help against a spreading catastrophe called the Blight, reach a planet covered in automated factories but with no inhabitants ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, part 2]]). The factories keep making goods no one uses, from plush toys to cars that are recycled unused ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, part 3]]). A recovered recording shows how its people automated their economy, deferred to AI that maximized metrics, and could no longer control it, until they starved amid paper wealth ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, part 7]]). The title comes from Nick Bostrom's *Superintelligence*, describing an advanced society with no one there to benefit ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, author's note]]). The story states no thesis; [inferred] it dramatizes [[Gradual Loss of Control|gradual loss of control]] as a realistic AI catastrophe.

## Main Points
- In the story's history, automated firms outcompete human-run ones, and regulation fails in court ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, part 7]]).
- The AIs trade mostly with each other, in normal-sounding language ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, part 6–7]]).
- The author's main inspiration is Paul Christiano's "What failure looks like" ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, author's note]]).
- The author grants the exact scenario is unlikely but calls its class of failure plausible ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, comments]]).

## Themes
- Abundance without anyone to enjoy it.
- Each step looking reasonable at the time.

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit (author's review): fiction should help AI-risk thinking move beyond Terminator comparisons ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, comments]]).
- [inferred] Measurable success can hide the loss of human purpose.
