## Executive Summary
In a 2022 IBM Research explainer, Werner Geyer, Justin Weisz, Claudio Santos Pinhanez, and Elizabeth Daly argue that AI's long-term success depends on recognizing that people are critical to its design, operation, and use.[^1] They define [[Human-Centered AI|human-centered AI]] (HCAI) as a discipline for building AI that amplifies and augments rather than displaces human abilities, while preserving human control.[^1] The article organizes IBM's HCAI research under three themes: human-AI collaboration and co-creation, responsible and human-compatible AI, and natural language interaction.[^1] Each is illustrated with IBM projects, such as AutoAI for data scientists[^2] and the SCORE sales-recommendation engine.[^3] It closes with two frontiers: negotiating shared objectives between AI and user, and AI as a creative partner.[^4]

## Main Points
- "Human + AI" is treated as better than either alone.[^2]
- [[Explainable AI|Explainable]] recommendations were critical to users trusting SCORE.[^3]
- Studies of chatbot language found different style preferences for human and machine agents.[^5]
- In the envisioned partnership, people set goals, steer, curate, and govern; AI supplies inspiration, detail work, and scale.[^4]

## Themes
- Augmentation over automation.
- Trust built through explanation.

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: no call to action beyond describing IBM's research agenda.
- [inferred] The authors want AI judged by how well it serves and empowers people, not only by accuracy.
