## Executive Summary
Dwarkesh Patel interviews [[Richard Sutton]], a founder of [[Reinforcement Learning|reinforcement learning]] (RL), who argues that [[Large Language Model|large language models]] (LLMs) are a dead end.[^1] Sutton says LLMs mimic what people say rather than learning what happens, so they lack a real [[World Model|world model]], a goal, and any ground truth.[^2] He holds that intelligence comes from experience: a stream of sensation, action, and reward that an agent learns from continually.[^3] He expects experience-based systems to supersede LLMs, another instance of his [[Bitter Lesson]].[^2] Patel argues LLMs could be the foundation for that learning.[^1] Sutton also sees succession to AI as inevitable and urges people to view it positively.[^4]

## Main Points
- Animals learn by prediction and trial and error, not imitation or supervised learning.[^5]
- Current methods generalize poorly from one state to another.[^6] See [[Out-of-Distribution Generalization]].
- An agent has four parts: policy, value function, perception, and a transition model of the world.[^3]

## Themes
- Learning from experience versus learning from people.
- Simple, scalable methods beating human-engineered knowledge.

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: Sutton urges people to think positively about [[AI Succession|succession]], while seeking change that is voluntary.[^4]
- [inferred] Patel wants listeners to weigh both views on whether LLMs can lead to general intelligence.
