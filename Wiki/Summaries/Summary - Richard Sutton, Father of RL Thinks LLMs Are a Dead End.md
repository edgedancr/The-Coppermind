## Executive Summary
Dwarkesh Patel interviews [[Richard Sutton]], a founder of [[Reinforcement Learning|reinforcement learning]] (RL), who argues that [[Large Language Model|large language models]] (LLMs) are a dead end ([[SRC - Richard Sutton, Father of RL Thinks LLMs Are a Dead End|Sutton interview, introduction]]). Sutton says LLMs mimic what people say rather than learning what happens, so they lack a real [[World Model|world model]], a goal, and any ground truth ([[SRC - Richard Sutton, Father of RL Thinks LLMs Are a Dead End|Sutton interview, "Are LLMs a dead end?"]]). He holds that intelligence comes from experience: a stream of sensation, action, and reward that an agent learns from continually ([[SRC - Richard Sutton, Father of RL Thinks LLMs Are a Dead End|Sutton interview, "The Era of Experience"]]). He expects experience-based systems to supersede LLMs, another instance of his [[Bitter Lesson]] ([[SRC - Richard Sutton, Father of RL Thinks LLMs Are a Dead End|Sutton interview, "Are LLMs a dead end?"]]). Patel argues LLMs could be the foundation for that learning ([[SRC - Richard Sutton, Father of RL Thinks LLMs Are a Dead End|Sutton interview, introduction]]). Sutton also sees succession to AI as inevitable and urges people to view it positively ([[SRC - Richard Sutton, Father of RL Thinks LLMs Are a Dead End|Sutton interview, "Succession to AI"]]).

## Main Points
- Animals learn by prediction and trial and error, not imitation or supervised learning ([[SRC - Richard Sutton, Father of RL Thinks LLMs Are a Dead End|Sutton interview, "Do humans do imitation learning?"]]).
- Current methods generalize poorly from one state to another ([[SRC - Richard Sutton, Father of RL Thinks LLMs Are a Dead End|Sutton interview, "Current architectures generalize poorly out of distribution"]]). See [[Out-of-Distribution Generalization]].
- An agent has four parts: policy, value function, perception, and a transition model of the world ([[SRC - Richard Sutton, Father of RL Thinks LLMs Are a Dead End|Sutton interview, "The Era of Experience"]]).

## Themes
- Learning from experience versus learning from people.
- Simple, scalable methods beating human-engineered knowledge.

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: Sutton urges people to think positively about [[AI Succession|succession]], while seeking change that is voluntary ([[SRC - Richard Sutton, Father of RL Thinks LLMs Are a Dead End|Sutton interview, "Succession to AI"]]).
- [inferred] Patel wants listeners to weigh both views on whether LLMs can lead to general intelligence.
