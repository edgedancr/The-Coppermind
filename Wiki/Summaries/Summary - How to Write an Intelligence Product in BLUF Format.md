## Executive Summary
The handout teaches the bottom-line-up-front (BLUF) format using a Justice Department drug intelligence assessment as its model.[^1] Intelligence products must be tailored to busy policymakers and commanders, and BLUF lets them find and understand what they need quickly.[^2] Each paragraph opens with a sentence that covers everything in it, and the details follow from most to least important.[^3] It walks through a product's parts, from background and analysis to context and outlook, and stresses accuracy, brevity, and clarity.[^4] The title, executive summary, and introduction are written last.[^5]

## Main Points
- The BLUF "umbrella" and the parts of a product: [[Bottom Line Up Front]].[^6]
- The title as "the ultimate BLUF": [[Analytic Title]].[^7]
- Footnotes carry background for novice readers without cluttering paragraphs for expert ones.[^8]
- Keep records of sources and methods, even though government products rarely include bibliographies.[^9]

## Themes
- Writing for the decision maker's time and needs.
- Accuracy, brevity, and clarity as protection against misreading.

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: there is no place for the analyst's opinion or editorializing.[^8]
- Explicit: editing improves the product at every stage.[^10]
