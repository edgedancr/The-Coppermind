## Executive Summary
Stephen Marrin argues that ICD 203 sets an unattainable standard by requiring analysts to perform their functions with objectivity ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "introduction"]]). The standard descends from Sherman Kent and the 1940s hope of making social science a science ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "History of analytic objectivity in the US IC"]]). In practice bias cannot be eliminated, and analysis has a built-in bias toward warning ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Analytic objectivity is not attainable"]]). Even if attainable, objectivity may reduce analysts' influence, since policymakers usually ignore the inconvenient fact ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Analytic objectivity decrease relevance and influence"]]). The ideal survives in myths and slogans such as "speaking truth to power" ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Analytic objectivity embedded in idealistic myths and slogans"]]), and it rests on an idealized view of science that scientists themselves have revised ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Analytic objectivity derived from idealized version of science"]]). He proposes aiming at objectivity rather than claiming it ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Preliminary conclusions"]]).

## Main Points
- The standard, its history, and its limits: [[Analytic Objectivity]].
- The slogan it supports: [[Speaking Truth to Power]].
- Intelligence analysts resemble public policy analysts, who face the same trade-off: closeness risks integrity, distance risks influence ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Learning from other kinds of public policy analysts"]]).

## Themes
- The gap between the ideal ethos of analysts and their practice.
- Fact and value are hard to separate in national security decisions.

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: shift the ethos from absolutes like "speak truth to power" toward honesty, integrity, and "call it as you see it" ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Preliminary conclusions"]]).
- Explicit: Marrin says this is not a case for assessments that support policies without a reasonable basis in fact ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Preliminary conclusions"]]).
