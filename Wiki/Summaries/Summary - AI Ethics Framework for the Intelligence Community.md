## Executive Summary
ODNI's *Artificial Intelligence Ethics Framework for the Intelligence Community* (v1.0, June 2020) is an ethics guide for [[United States Intelligence Community|Intelligence Community]] personnel who procure, design, build, use, protect, consume, and manage AI.[^1] It states no single thesis; its premise is that AI can enhance the intelligence mission but must be used in line with IC principles to prevent unethical outcomes.[^1] ODNI calls it not a checklist but a living document meant to support reasoned judgment and documentation across the AI lifecycle.[^1] It lists ten things AI should do, then poses questions under ten matching headings, from purpose and legal authorities to periodic review and stewardship.[^1] It asks that many kinds of stakeholders, from developers to civil liberties officers and legal counsel, use it together.[^1]

## Main Points
- AI should be used only when appropriate to a defined purpose, after weighing risks, including to civil liberties and privacy.[^1][^2]
- The assessed risk sets where and how much a human is involved, and an accountable human should be named.[^3] See [[Human Decision Point]].
- The IC aims to mitigate [[Undesired Bias|undesired bias]], not the "biases" it builds in on purpose.[^3]
- AI should be tested, version-controlled, documented, explainable, and periodically reviewed.[^4][^5][^6]

## Themes
- Accountability: nearly every heading asks who is responsible and how decisions are documented.[^1][^6]
- Risk-proportionate effort: testing, human involvement, and explainability scale with the risk of the use.[^3][^4][^5]

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: identify, before deployment, who maintains the AI and who can "modify, limit, or stop" its use.[^6]
- [inferred] The questions are meant to be answered and recorded, so the answers can be audited.
