## Executive Summary
A CIA analyst writing as Bobby W. argues that policymakers expect intelligence to tell them what will happen, while analysts can reliably forecast only linear trends.[^1] Forecasts fail at discontinuities, at the timing of tipping points, and on complex human systems, limits no commission can fix.[^2] Analysts should therefore educate their customers and adopt a more modest ethos, presenting themselves as answerers of five questions policymakers keep asking.[^3] Agencies should favor foundational research and speculative analysis, hold analysts accountable for accuracy, build diverse teams, and hire for the traits of good forecasters.[^4] AI will help but will not remove the limits.[^5]

## Main Points
- The three limits, the five questions, and the reforms: [[Limits of Prediction]].
- Casey's 1983 panel and the [[Prejudice Toward Continuity]].[^2]
- Scenario analysis depends on [[Indicators and Signposts]].[^6]
- Hiring for the traits of a [[Superforecaster]].[^5]

## Themes
- Analytic humility, and educating the consumer.
- The future is plural: scenarios, signposts, and conditional judgments.

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: "An educated consumer is our best customer," as former CIA analyst Dennis Wilder put it.[^7]
