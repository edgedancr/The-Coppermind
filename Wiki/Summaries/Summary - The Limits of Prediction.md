## Executive Summary
A CIA analyst writing as Bobby W. argues that policymakers expect intelligence to tell them what will happen, while analysts can reliably forecast only linear trends ([[Bobby W. - The Limits of Prediction (2019).pdf#page=1|Bobby W., p. 7]]). Forecasts fail at discontinuities, at the timing of tipping points, and on complex human systems, limits no commission can fix ([[Bobby W. - The Limits of Prediction (2019).pdf#page=2|Bobby W., pp. 8–9]]). Analysts should therefore educate their customers and adopt a more modest ethos, presenting themselves as answerers of five questions policymakers keep asking ([[Bobby W. - The Limits of Prediction (2019).pdf#page=2|Bobby W., pp. 8–12]]). Agencies should favor foundational research and speculative analysis, hold analysts accountable for accuracy, build diverse teams, and hire for the traits of good forecasters ([[Bobby W. - The Limits of Prediction (2019).pdf#page=6|Bobby W., pp. 12–14]]). AI will help but will not remove the limits ([[Bobby W. - The Limits of Prediction (2019).pdf#page=8|Bobby W., p. 14]]).
