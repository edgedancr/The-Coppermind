## Executive Summary
A Then & Now video explains [[Martin Heidegger]]'s 1954 essay "The Question Concerning Technology" and applies it to social media ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 1]]). As the video presents it, Heidegger argues that the essence of technology "is by no means anything technical": technology is not just a means to an end but a way of revealing, of bringing something hidden into the open ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 2–4]]). Modern technology, the video says, reveals the world as [[Standing Reserve|standing reserve]], resources stockpiled and ready for use, and Heidegger calls this [[Enframing|enframing]] ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 4]]). Where enframing reigns, Heidegger writes, there is danger "in the highest sense" ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 5]]). The narrator applies this to social media, where users' data and connections become standing reserve presented back to them as the status quo ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 5]]).

## Main Points
- The video maps Aristotle's [[Four Causes|four causes]] onto social media: hardware and content, code and algorithms, the desire for connection, and the platform's owner along with its users ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 2]]).
- Users shape social media, except for its hardware and its algorithm ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 3]]).
- The video's answer is to question: who controls the algorithm, and do we need to stockpile at all? ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 6]])

## Themes
- Technology as a way of seeing the world, not just a tool.
- Losing sight of how a technology began once it feels natural.

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: it is up to us to think before we use technology, so it doesn't take us somewhere we don't want to go ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 6]]).
- [inferred] The video treats the algorithm's owners as holding the most power over what users see.
