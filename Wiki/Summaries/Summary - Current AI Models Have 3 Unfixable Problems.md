## Executive Summary
Sabine Hossenfelder argues that current AI models will not reach [[Artificial General Intelligence|artificial general intelligence]] with more time, and discusses what would be needed instead ([[Sabine Hossenfelder - Current AI Models Have 3 Unfixable Problems (transcript)|Hossenfelder, intro]]). Both [[Large Language Model|large language models]] and image- and video-generating diffusion models, she notes, are deep neural nets ([[Sabine Hossenfelder - Current AI Models Have 3 Unfixable Problems (transcript)|Hossenfelder, intro]]). Her first problem is that they are "purpose bound," trained to find patterns in particular kinds of data rather than serving as an abstract thinking device ([[Sabine Hossenfelder - Current AI Models Have 3 Unfixable Problems (transcript)|Hossenfelder, ch. 2]]). She considers [[Hallucination|hallucination]] less serious ([[Sabine Hossenfelder - Current AI Models Have 3 Unfixable Problems (transcript)|Hossenfelder, ch. 2]]), but calls [[Prompt Injection|prompt injection]] basically impossible to solve ([[Sabine Hossenfelder - Current AI Models Have 3 Unfixable Problems (transcript)|Hossenfelder, ch. 3]]). Finally, she says the models can't generalize beyond their training data ([[Sabine Hossenfelder - Current AI Models Have 3 Unfixable Problems (transcript)|Hossenfelder, ch. 4]]). Her thesis: for these reasons the current generation of generative AI "will not go far," and the revenue expected to justify AI companies' valuations will evaporate ([[Sabine Hossenfelder - Current AI Models Have 3 Unfixable Problems (transcript)|Hossenfelder, ch. 4]]).

## Main Points
- Hallucinations typically happen when the answer wasn't in the training data, or appeared only a few times; she expects rewarding models for admitting uncertainty to reduce the problem, though not end it ([[Sabine Hossenfelder - Current AI Models Have 3 Unfixable Problems (transcript)|Hossenfelder, ch. 2]]).
- LLMs can't tell instructions from the input they should work on ([[Sabine Hossenfelder - Current AI Models Have 3 Unfixable Problems (transcript)|Hossenfelder, ch. 3]]).
- Quoting Gary Marcus, the models "interpolate, they don't extrapolate," which she calls the biggest obstacle to using them in science ([[Sabine Hossenfelder - Current AI Models Have 3 Unfixable Problems (transcript)|Hossenfelder, ch. 4]]).
- She sees abstract reasoning networks, with world models and neurosymbolic reasoning as steps, as what will take over ([[Sabine Hossenfelder - Current AI Models Have 3 Unfixable Problems (transcript)|Hossenfelder, ch. 5]]).

## Themes
- Limits built into the architecture, not fixable with scale.
- Useful tools versus general intelligence.

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: current models "do have their uses" and will keep improving, but not toward AGI ([[Sabine Hossenfelder - Current AI Models Have 3 Unfixable Problems (transcript)|Hossenfelder, ch. 4]]).
- No call to action.
