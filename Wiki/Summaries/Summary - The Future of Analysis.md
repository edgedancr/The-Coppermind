## Executive Summary
Joseph Gartin argues that intelligence analysis stands at an inflection point: decades of accomplishment behind it, and ahead a future shaped by AI, big data, and machine learning ([[Gartin - The Future of Analysis (2019).pdf#page=1|Gartin, p. 1]]). Much of it still resembles Sherman Kent's day, a matter of reading and writing by very smart, very similar people, whose repeated failures show the limits of that model ([[Gartin - The Future of Analysis (2019).pdf#page=1|Gartin, pp. 1–2]]). Five drivers have reshaped the profession since then ([[Gartin - The Future of Analysis (2019).pdf#page=3|Gartin, p. 3]]). He imagines an analyst in 2030 working with a digital assistant that tracks prediction markets, base rates, sources, and her own accuracy ([[Gartin - The Future of Analysis (2019).pdf#page=4|Gartin, p. 4]]). He closes with five suggestions and his optimism that the IC will adapt ([[Gartin - The Future of Analysis (2019).pdf#page=5|Gartin, p. 5]]).
