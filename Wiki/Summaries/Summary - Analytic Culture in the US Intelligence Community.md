## Executive Summary
[[Rob Johnston]], an anthropologist, studied intelligence analysts from September 2001, using hundreds of interviews and two years of observation.[^1] He defines intelligence analysis as a social as well as cognitive process, in which analysts start from their agency's previous products and negotiate meaning with peers and supervisors.[^2] His findings describe a culture shaped by secrecy, time pressure, a focus on current production, rewards for volume, the idea of analysis as "tradecraft," [[Confirmation Bias|confirmation bias]] tied to defending an agency's official line, a weak professional identity, and agency-by-agency training.[^3] He recommends a formal infrastructure to measure and improve analytic performance, research into analytic methods and cognition, realistic recruiting, and tracking of the culture itself.[^4] Ch. 3 proposes a taxonomy of the variables that affect analysis,[^5] and ch. 4 argues that the traditional [[Intelligence Cycle|intelligence cycle]] misrepresents the analyst's burden.[^6] This summary covers the front matter and ch. 1–4 and 8–9; batch 1e-3 adds ch. 5–7.

## Main Points
- **Definitions:** intelligence, [[Intelligence Analysis|intelligence analysis]], and [[Intelligence Error and Intelligence Failure|error and failure]].[^7]
- **Findings:** [[Analytic Culture]], including [[Secrecy vs. Efficacy]], [[Corporate Judgment]], and [[Tradecraft vs. Science in Intelligence Analysis]].[^3]
- **Taxonomy:** [[Taxonomy of Intelligence Analysis Variables]].[^5]
- **The intelligence cycle:** [[Systems Model of the Intelligence Cycle]].[^6]
- **Recruiting:** [[Anticipatory Socialization]].[^8]
- **Recommendations:** [[Johnston's Analytic Culture Recommendations]] and the [[Performance Improvement Infrastructure]],[^9] including [[Community of Practice|communities of practice]].[^10]
- **The core structural problem:** current reporting competes with [[Indications and Warning|indications and warning]] for time and resources.[^11]

## Themes
- Culture and product are inseparable.[^12]
- Openness and measurement as conditions for improvement, against a culture of secrecy.[^13]
- Lessons from other high-stakes professions such as surgery and spaceflight.[^14]

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: "Using data-driven metrics means that expertise is not a function of tenure".[^15]
- Explicit: Johnston calls the work a progress report, not a final report.[^16]
- [inferred] Johnston treats analytic failure as an organizational problem more than an individual one.
