## Executive Summary
Rob Johnston, an anthropologist, studied intelligence analysts from September 2001, using hundreds of interviews and two years of observation ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=13|Johnston, pp. xiii, 3]]). He defines intelligence analysis as a social as well as cognitive process, in which analysts start from their agency's previous products and negotiate meaning with peers and supervisors ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=24|Johnston, pp. 4–6]]). His findings describe a culture shaped by secrecy, time pressure, a focus on current production, rewards for volume, the idea of analysis as "tradecraft," confirmation bias tied to defending an agency's official line, a weak professional identity, and agency-by-agency training ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=29|Johnston, pp. 9–29]]). He recommends a formal infrastructure to measure and improve analytic performance, research into analytic methods and cognition, realistic recruiting, and tracking of the culture itself ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=113|Johnston, pp. 97–116]]). Ch. 3 proposes a taxonomy of the variables that affect analysis ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=52|Johnston, pp. 33–44]]), and ch. 4 argues that the traditional intelligence cycle misrepresents the analyst's burden ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=64|J. M. Johnston & R. Johnston, pp. 45–57]]). This summary covers the front matter and ch. 1–4 and 8–9; batch 1e-3 adds ch. 5–7.

## Main Points
- **Definitions:** intelligence, [[Intelligence Analysis|intelligence analysis]], and [[Intelligence Error and Intelligence Failure|error and failure]] ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=23|Johnston, pp. 3–8]]).
- **Findings:** [[Analytic Culture]], including [[Secrecy vs. Efficacy]], [[Corporate Judgment]], and [[Tradecraft vs. Science in Intelligence Analysis]] ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=29|Johnston, pp. 9–29]]).
- **Taxonomy:** [[Taxonomy of Intelligence Analysis Variables]] ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=52|Johnston, pp. 33–44]]).
- **The intelligence cycle:** [[Systems Model of the Intelligence Cycle]] ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=64|J. M. Johnston & R. Johnston, pp. 45–57]]).
- **Recruiting:** [[Anticipatory Socialization]] ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=113|Konya & Johnston, pp. 97–106]]).
- **Recommendations:** [[Johnston's Analytic Culture Recommendations]] and the [[Performance Improvement Infrastructure]] ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=123|Johnston, pp. 107–116]]), including [[Community of Practice|communities of practice]] ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=128|Johnston, p. 112]]).
- **The core structural problem:** current reporting competes with [[Indications and Warning|indications and warning]] for time and resources ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=123|Johnston, p. 107]]).

## Themes
- Culture and product are inseparable ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=132|Johnston, p. 116]]).
- Openness and measurement as conditions for improvement, against a culture of secrecy ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=16|Johnston, p. xvi]]).
- Lessons from other high-stakes professions such as surgery and spaceflight ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=14|Johnston, pp. xiv–xvi]]).

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: "Using data-driven metrics means that expertise is not a function of tenure" ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=126|Johnston, p. 110]]).
- Explicit: Johnston calls the work a progress report, not a final report ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=20|Johnston, p. xx]]).
- [inferred] Johnston treats analytic failure as an organizational problem more than an individual one.
