## Executive Summary
The SAIL ACH GenAI Workflow presents a prompt sequence for running [[Analysis of Competing Hypotheses]] with a generative AI assistant ([[SRC - ACH GenAI Workflow|ACH Workflow, Orchestrator]]). It says the AI should be a structured reasoning partner, not a replacement for analytic judgment ([[SRC - ACH GenAI Workflow|ACH Workflow, Orchestrator]]). The sequence has seven steps, 0 to 6, each with a prompt, guardrails for the AI, and an analyst checkpoint ([[SRC - ACH GenAI Workflow|ACH Workflow, Orchestrator]]). The document says the analyst owns the product and must verify every factual claim ([[SRC - ACH GenAI Workflow|ACH Workflow, Orchestrator]]). It says the Devil's Advocate step must never be skipped ([[SRC - ACH GenAI Workflow|ACH Workflow, Orchestrator]]).

## Main Points
- Steps 0–1 set the rules (disconfirmation, no fabricated evidence) and generate at least four hypotheses, including one involving deception ([[SRC - ACH GenAI Workflow|ACH Workflow, Steps 0–1]]).
- Step 2 builds the evidence list in one of three modes, tagging each item's verification status and including absent evidence ([[SRC - ACH GenAI Workflow|ACH Workflow, Step 2]]).
- Steps 3–4 rate evidence against hypotheses, rank them by fewest inconsistent ratings, and identify linchpins ([[SRC - ACH GenAI Workflow|ACH Workflow, Steps 3–4]]).
- Steps 5–6 stress-test the leading hypothesis, then produce a report with confidence, milestones, and any dissent ([[SRC - ACH GenAI Workflow|ACH Workflow, Steps 5–6]]).

## Themes
- Analyst control: every step ends in a checkpoint.
- Distrust of unverified AI output, including [[Hallucination]] and [[Automation Bias]].
- Disconfirmation over confirmation.

## Key Takeaways
- Explicit: treat each checkpoint as a real decision, verify AI-researched evidence, and treat confidence labels as qualitative ([[SRC - ACH GenAI Workflow|ACH Workflow, Orchestrator]]).
- [inferred] The document locates rigor in the process and the analyst's review, not in the AI's output.
