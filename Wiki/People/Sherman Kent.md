> [!summary]
> Sherman Kent was a founding father of Allied [[Intelligence Analysis|intelligence analysis]], and CIA's Sherman Kent School for Intelligence Analysis is named for him.[^1] Joseph Gartin compares his writings on analysis to Machiavelli's *The Prince*: more talked about than read.[^1]

## Key Ideas and Contributions
- **[[Analytic Objectivity|Objectivity]]:** in 1949 he wanted analysts to know the "probable objective situation" of other countries through systematic research, while admitting that no human being probably ever apprehends the true objective fact.[^2]
- **Independence from policy:** he wanted analysts kept separate from policymakers so they could call attention to "the stubborn fact" policymakers may be neglecting.[^2]
- **The elevated debate:** the finest minds, steeped in history and current events, organized around a shared analytic process.[^1]
- **Analyzing analysis:** [[Rob Johnston]] credits Kent, who saw intelligence as a "special category of knowledge," with showing that the analytic process could itself be analyzed; Kent divided it into basic descriptive, current reporting, and estimative elements.[^3]
- **Estimates:** his work on confidence, probability, estimative statements, and dissents still underpins all-source analysis.[^4] He claimed not infallibility but the "most deeply and objectively based and carefully considered" estimates.[^5] See [[Estimative Language]].
- **The Cuba estimate:** the 1962 Special [[National Intelligence Estimate]] concluded the Soviet Union would not place strategic weapons in Cuba. Kent defended it in 1964: "We were not brought up to underestimate our enemies".[^4]
