> [!summary]
> Khalid Sheikh Mohammed (KSM) was, in the 9/11 Commission's words, ==the "principal architect of the [[September 11 Attacks (2001)|9/11 attacks]]"== and the chief manager of the [[Planes Operation|"planes operation"]].[^1] The Commission calls him the clearest model of the [[Terrorist Entrepreneur|terrorist entrepreneur]]: a field commander with considerable autonomy, who saw al-Qaeda as a source of the money and operatives his ideas needed.[^2]

## Key Ideas and Contributions
- **Background:** he grew up in Kuwait, studied in North Carolina, and earned a mechanical engineering degree in December 1986.[^3] In 1987 he joined the anti-Soviet jihad under the Afghan leader Abdul Rasul Sayyaf.[^4]
- **First plots:** in 1992 he wired $660 to a co-conspirator of his nephew Ramzi Yousef, the 1993 World Trade Center bomber.[^5] In 1994 he and Yousef planned the Manila air ("Bojinka") plot to bomb 12 U.S. jumbo jets over the Pacific, his first time planning an operation.[^5]
- **The proposal:** at Tora Bora in mid-1996 he pitched [[Usama Bin Ladin|Bin Ladin]] an operation to train pilots to crash planes into U.S. buildings.[^6] His original plan had ten aircraft, the last of which he would land himself to make a speech; the Commission calls it "a spectacle of destruction with KSM as the self-cast star".[^7]
- **In al-Qaeda:** after Bin Ladin approved the plot in 1999,[^7] KSM moved to Kandahar, supervised its planning, and led al-Qaeda's media committee, but says he refused to swear a formal oath of allegiance.[^8] Colleagues called him an intelligent, efficient manager; Nashiri said he rarely conceived a specific operation himself.[^8]
- He was captured in 2003.[^9]

> [!question] Whose view is this?
> Much of this account comes from KSM's own statements under interrogation. The Commission could not question him or his interrogators, and treats detainee reporting with caution.[^4]
