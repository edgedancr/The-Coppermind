> [!summary]
> C. S. Lewis (1898–1963) wrote the essay "On Living in an Atomic Age," first published in 1948 and later collected in *Present Concerns*, edited by Walter Hooper.[^1] He appears in the Coppermind for that essay, assigned in SAIL Module 5, which asks how people should live under a new threat of destruction.

## Key Ideas and Contributions
From "On Living in an Atomic Age":
- **Don't exaggerate the novelty:** we should live in an atomic age as people lived with plague or Viking raids; everyone was "already sentenced to death before the atomic bomb was invented".[^1]
- **Keep doing human things:** if the bomb comes, let it find us "praying, working, teaching, reading, listening to music, bathing the children," not "huddled together like frightened sheep." ==They may break our bodies, "but they need not dominate our minds."==[^1]
- **The real question** is not whether civilization will be destroyed, but whether Nature is the only thing that exists.[^2] See [[Naturalism]].
- **We are strangers here:** humans are "spirits, free and rational beings," who come from "another world," which is why we do not feel at home in Nature.[^3]
- **Never put survival first:** we should live by "the law of love and temperance," not "the law of competition and grab," and the survival of our species is not worth having unless it comes by "honourable and merciful means".[^4] "Nothing is more likely to destroy a species or a nation than a determination to survive at all costs."[^4]
