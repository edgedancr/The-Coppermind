> [!summary]
> Martin Heidegger was a German philosopher who had long been interested in technology. In 1954, nine years after a world war in which, in the video's words, technology was used "to destroy half of Europe and all of Nagasaki and Hiroshima," he published the essay "The Question Concerning Technology".[^1] A Then & Now video explains it for SAIL's Module 2.

## Key Ideas and Contributions
- **[[Enframing]]:** modern technology reveals the world as [[Standing Reserve|standing reserve]], resources stockpiled and ready for use, and where enframing reigns there is danger "in the highest sense".[^2]
- **Technology as revealing:** the essence of technology "is by no means anything technical"; it is a way of bringing something hidden into the open.[^3]
- **A critique of Western philosophy:** the video says Heidegger saw 2,000 years of Western philosophy as obsessed with Aristotle's [[Four Causes|four causes]] and with measuring and quantifying, missing what the causes reveal about being itself.[^4]
- He was concerned that new technologies weren't just natural progress, and that this mentality could lead down "dark and dangerous paths".[^1]
