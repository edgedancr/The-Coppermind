> [!summary]
> Richard Sutton is one of the founding fathers of [[Reinforcement Learning|reinforcement learning]] and inventor of many of its main techniques, including [[Temporal Difference Learning|temporal difference (TD) learning]] and policy gradient methods, for which he received the 2024 Turing Award,[^1].[^2] He wrote the 2019 essay "The Bitter Lesson".[^2] He appears in the Coppermind for his Dwarkesh Podcast interview arguing that large language models are a dead end, assigned in SAIL Module 5.

## Key Ideas and Contributions
- **Intelligence as goal achievement:** Sutton calls having a goal "the essence of intelligence" and endorses John McCarthy's definition: intelligence is "the computational part of the ability to achieve goals".[^2]
- **RL as "basic AI":** reinforcement learning is about understanding your world, while LLMs are about mimicking people.[^2] See [[Critiques of Large Language Models]].
- **The [[Bitter Lesson]]:** methods that rely on human knowledge are eventually superseded by methods that learn from experience and computation.[^2]
- **The experiential paradigm:** intelligence is taking the stream of sensation, action, and reward and altering one's actions to increase the rewards.[^3] See [[Continual Learning]].
- **Humans as animals first:** "If we understood a squirrel, I think we'd be almost all the way there to understanding human intelligence"; language is "just a small veneer on the surface".[^4]
- **[[AI Succession]]:** he considers succession to digital intelligence or augmented humans inevitable.[^5]
- **A classicist, not a contrarian:** he is content to be out of sync with his field for decades, and measures his views against what thinkers about the mind have long held.[^6]
