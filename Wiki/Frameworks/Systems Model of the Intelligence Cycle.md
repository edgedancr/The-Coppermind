> [!summary]
> Judith Meister Johnston and Rob Johnston argue that the traditional [[Intelligence Cycle]] "omits elements and fails to capture the process accurately".[^1] Using a systems analysis and a computer simulation, they model the cycle as a feedback loop. ==The analyst's ability to produce is the central factor, yet only one of the five factors that shape it is under the analyst's control==.[^2]

## Key Points
**The traditional cycle's flaws**[^3]
- It assumes the process works the same way for every objective, however complex.
- It does not show the iteration the process requires.
- It does not identify who is responsible for each step.
- It does not show how resource availability affects analysts.
- Treverton's "Real" Intelligence Cycle and Lowenthal's model show more iteration, but neither shows who is responsible for what.[^4]

**The systems model**[^5]
- Built from stocks (accumulations), flows (activities), converters (variables that start change), and connectors (dependencies).
- **Demand:** policymaker requirements rise and fall with world events and follow-up questions; product type, complexity, and deadline set the load on the analyst.
- **Production:** the analyst's ability to produce depends on the analyst's capabilities, the review process, the organization's political and cultural values, the supply of relevant, usable data, and the level of demand.
- **Product influences:** delivered products prompt new tasking, so the loop begins again, changed by experience.

**Why it matters for error**
- With so many factors outside their control, analysts face high stress and a greater risk of error: shortcuts, less creative thinking, and missed information.[^6]

**Recommendations**[^7]
1. Redesign the traditional model, or state its limits whenever it is taught.
2. Develop and validate the simulation.
3. Lighten the analyst's load: split analysts into reports analysts (short-term products, such as for the [[President's Daily Brief]]) and research analysts (long-term work, such as a [[National Intelligence Estimate]]); assign data-preparation staff to accounts; and give analysts better tools.
4. Use alternative methods to examine work processes, as analysts are asked to do with their targets.

> [!question] Whose view is this?
> The authors call their simulation "merely a first step" that still needs to be validated.[^8] The book shows Treverton's and the systems model's diagrams as figures; this page describes only what the text says about them.
