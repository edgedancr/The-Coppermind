> [!summary]
> Just war theory judges both whether going to war is justified (**jus ad bellum**, "the right to war") and whether a war is fought justly (**jus in bello**, "right in war") ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/29/26|Intro notes, 9/29/26]]). Intro to Intelligence taught it with criteria drawn from Aquinas and Augustine ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/29/26|Intro notes, 9/29/26]]).

## Components
**Jus ad bellum: thresholds for starting a war** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/29/26|Intro notes, 9/29/26]])
1. **Legitimate authority:** can the acting body raise military forces and actually control and end the conflict? This excludes private citizens and banditry.
2. **Just cause:** based on Aquinas, does the action rectify an existing injustice or prevent a wrongful attack?
3. **Right intention:** is the ultimate goal Augustine's peace, or masked egoism, revenge, and material gain?
4. **Last resort:** have negotiation, economic sanctions, and mediation been exhausted, and has clear signaling given the enemy time to reflect and back down?
5. **Reasonable chance of success.**

**Jus in bello: discrimination and the boundaries of control** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/29/26|Intro notes, 9/29/26]])
- **Legitimate targets:** armed forces, guerrillas, and civilians meeting the ICRC's 2009 standard of "direct participation in hostilities."
- **The gray zone:** civilian infrastructure, and the risk of **double effect**: unintended harm to civilians from a strike on a legitimate military target.
- **Protected status:** women, children, the infirm, wounded prisoners of war, and culturally or religiously valued objects.

**The midterm list** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/13/26|Intro notes, 10/13/26]]): just cause, just intentions, proper authority, last resort, probability of success, ==proportionality of means to objectives==, and discrimination of control.

## How to Use It
- Work through the jus ad bellum thresholds before force is authorized, then judge conduct by jus in bello ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/29/26|Intro notes, 9/29/26]]).

## Limits and Cautions
- SAIL taught the related [[Law of Armed Conflict]] as requiring distinction and proportionality, neither of which can be delegated to an algorithm ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/20/26|SAIL notes, 10/20/26]]).
