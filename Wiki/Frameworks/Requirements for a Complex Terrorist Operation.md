> [!summary]
> The 9/11 Commission set out what al-Qaeda's leaders would have needed in order "to organize and conduct a complex international terrorist operation to inflict catastrophic harm".[^1] It concluded that ==by the spring and summer of 2000, al-Qaeda "was able to meet these requirements"==.[^2]

## Components
The Commission's list:[^3]
1. Leaders able to evaluate, approve, and supervise the planning and direction of the operation.
2. Communications sufficient to plan, and to direct the operatives and those helping them.
3. A personnel system to recruit, vet, indoctrinate, and train candidates.
4. An intelligence effort to gather information and assess enemy strengths and weaknesses.
5. The ability to move people.
6. The ability to raise and move the necessary money.

## How to Use It
- Read each requirement as a capability to assess, and as a place to look for vulnerability: the Commission's account of the plot shows al-Qaeda meeting each one, through its leadership, couriers, camps, casing flights, document forgery, and funding.[^4] See [[Planes Operation]] and [[Al-Qaeda Financing]].
- Compare the Commission's discussion of capabilities and intentions in [[Capabilities vs. Intentions]].

## Limits and Cautions
- The list is the Commission's own reconstruction: it says "we believe such a list of requirements would have included" these items, not that al-Qaeda wrote one.[^1]
- For where such operations find these conditions, see [[Terrorist Sanctuary]].
