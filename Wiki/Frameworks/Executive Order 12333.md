> [!summary]
> Executive Order 12333, signed by President Reagan in 1981, defines the powers, restraints, and structure of the modern U.S. Intelligence Community ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/10/26|Intro notes, 9/10/26]]). Intro to Intelligence taught it in two parts: definitions and roles, and the parameters that restrain intelligence activity ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/10/26|Intro notes, 9/10/26]]).

## Components
**History:** amended in 2003 after 9/11, in 2004, and again in 2008 ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/10/26|Intro notes, 9/10/26]]).

**Definitions (Section 3.5)** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/10/26|Intro notes, 9/10/26]])
- **Foreign intelligence (3.5(e)):** information relating to the capabilities, intentions, or activities of foreign governments, organizations, persons, or international terrorists, regardless of how or where it was collected.
- **National intelligence (3.5(i)):** all intelligence, from any source, gathered inside or outside the United States, that involves threats to the U.S. or its interests.
- ==After 9/11, "national intelligence" made all intelligence a shared enterprise==, whether collected at home by the FBI or abroad by the CIA, and demanded full integration to counter transnational threats.

**The Director of National Intelligence (Section 1.3)** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/10/26|Intro notes, 9/10/26]])
- Head of the Intelligence Community and principal adviser to the President, the National Security Council, and the Homeland Security Council.
- Sets priorities and guidance for the whole IC (1.3(b)(1)); determines requirements, directs the tasking of national collection assets, and resolves conflicts between IC elements (1.3(b)(17)).
- **Non-abrogation (1.3(c)):** the DNI directs the National Intelligence Program but does not command every agency's day-to-day operations; department heads keep their statutory authority.

**Functional managers** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/10/26|Intro notes, 9/10/26]]): the CIA Director for human intelligence (1.3(b)(12)(A)(ii)), the NSA Director for signals intelligence (1.3(b)(12)(A)(i)), and the NGA Director for geospatial intelligence. A functional manager advises the DNI on resources and collection gaps and sets the standards for training, tradecraft, and technical architecture across the IC (1.3(b)(12)(A)(iii)).

**Agency landscape (Section 1.7):** see [[United States Intelligence Community]].

**Restraints (Part 2)** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/10/26|Intro notes, 9/10/26]])
1. **Undisclosed participation (2.9):** no IC element may join a U.S. organization without disclosing its affiliation, unless essential to a lawful purpose, approved by the Attorney General, and not meant to influence the organization.
2. **Human experimentation (2.10):** the subject's informed consent must be documented.
3. **Assassination ban (2.11):** "No person employed by or acting on behalf of the United States Government shall engage in, or conspire to engage in, assassination." It stems from the 1970s Church Committee revelations.
4. **Indirect participation (2.12):** no IC element may ask anyone to do what the order forbids.
5. **Domestic covert action (2.13):** no [[Covert Action]] may be meant to influence U.S. political processes, public opinion, policies, or media.

## Limits and Cautions
- Together, 2.12 and 2.13 stop the IC from outsourcing illegal acts to proxies and keep foreign covert influence from feeding back into domestic politics ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/10/26|Intro notes, 9/10/26]]).
