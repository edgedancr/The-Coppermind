> [!summary]
> ICD 203 is the [[Intelligence Community Directive]] that sets the IC Analytic Standards, which "govern the production and evaluation of analytic products".[^1] DNI James Clapper signed the current version in January 2015.[^2] ==All IC analytic products "shall be consistent with" five analytic standards, the fifth of which contains nine analytic tradecraft standards==.[^3]

## Key Points
**The five analytic standards**[^4]
1. **Objective:** analysts work "with objectivity and with awareness of their own assumptions and reasoning," use techniques that reveal and mitigate bias, and consider alternative perspectives and contrary information. See [[Analytic Objectivity]].
2. **Independent of political consideration:** assessments "must not be distorted by, nor shaped for, advocacy" of an audience, agenda, or policy viewpoint. See [[Politicized Intelligence]].
3. **Timely:** disseminated in time to be actionable.
4. **Based on all available sources:** analysts identify critical information gaps and work with collectors to close them. See [[All-Source Intelligence]].
5. **Implements and exhibits the analytic tradecraft standards** (below).

**The nine analytic tradecraft standards**[^5]
1. Properly describes the quality and credibility of underlying sources, data, and methods.
2. Properly expresses and explains uncertainties, using fixed terms of likelihood: see [[Estimative Language]].[^6]
3. Distinguishes underlying intelligence information from [[Analytic Assumption|assumptions]] and judgments.[^7]
4. Incorporates analysis of alternatives, especially when low-probability events could have high impact.[^8]
5. Demonstrates customer relevance and addresses implications.
6. Uses clear and logical argumentation, with the main analytic message up front (see [[Bottom Line Up Front]]).
7. Explains change to, or consistency with, earlier judgments.
8. Makes accurate judgments, without avoiding "difficult judgments in order to minimize the risk of being wrong".[^9]
9. Uses effective visual information where appropriate.[^10]

**Beyond the standards**
- They are the criteria for each IC element's program of product evaluation, and a common foundation for analytic training.[^11]
- Analysts can take concerns about how the standards are applied to the [[Analytic Ombuds]].[^12]
- Products include personally identifiable information only for a specific analytic purpose.[^13]
- The directive does not apply to "purely law enforcement information".[^14]

## Why It Matters
- Its likelihood scale, like the earlier guide it resembles, has no "never" and no "always".[^2]
- Stephen Marrin argues that its objectivity standard is unattainable as written, so every analyst falls short of it.[^15] See [[Analytic Objectivity]].
