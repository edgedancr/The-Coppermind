> [!summary]
> ICD 203 is the [[Intelligence Community Directive]] that sets the IC Analytic Standards, which "govern the production and evaluation of analytic products" ([[ODNI - ICD 203, Analytic Standards (2015).pdf#page=1|ICD 203, §B.1]]). DNI James Clapper signed the current version in January 2015 ([[Lowenthal - Intelligence Is NOT About Telling Truth to Power (2021).pdf#page=4|Lowenthal, p. 3]]). ==All IC analytic products "shall be consistent with" five analytic standards, the fifth of which contains nine analytic tradecraft standards== ([[ODNI - ICD 203, Analytic Standards (2015).pdf#page=2|ICD 203, §D.6]]).

## Key Points
**The five analytic standards** ([[ODNI - ICD 203, Analytic Standards (2015).pdf#page=2|ICD 203, §D.6.a–e]])
1. **Objective:** analysts work "with objectivity and with awareness of their own assumptions and reasoning," use techniques that reveal and mitigate bias, and consider alternative perspectives and contrary information. See [[Analytic Objectivity]].
2. **Independent of political consideration:** assessments "must not be distorted by, nor shaped for, advocacy" of an audience, agenda, or policy viewpoint. See [[Politicized Intelligence]].
3. **Timely:** disseminated in time to be actionable.
4. **Based on all available sources:** analysts identify critical information gaps and work with collectors to close them. See [[All-Source Intelligence]].
5. **Implements and exhibits the analytic tradecraft standards** (below).

**The nine analytic tradecraft standards** ([[ODNI - ICD 203, Analytic Standards (2015).pdf#page=2|ICD 203, §D.6.e]])
1. Properly describes the quality and credibility of underlying sources, data, and methods.
2. Properly expresses and explains uncertainties, using fixed terms of likelihood: see [[Estimative Language]] ([[ODNI - ICD 203, Analytic Standards (2015).pdf#page=3|ICD 203, §D.6.e(2)]]).
3. Distinguishes underlying intelligence information from [[Analytic Assumption|assumptions]] and judgments ([[ODNI - ICD 203, Analytic Standards (2015).pdf#page=3|ICD 203, §D.6.e(3)]]).
4. Incorporates analysis of alternatives, especially when low-probability events could have high impact ([[ODNI - ICD 203, Analytic Standards (2015).pdf#page=4|ICD 203, §D.6.e(4)]]).
5. Demonstrates customer relevance and addresses implications.
6. Uses clear and logical argumentation, with the main analytic message up front (see [[Bottom Line Up Front]]).
7. Explains change to, or consistency with, earlier judgments.
8. Makes accurate judgments, without avoiding "difficult judgments in order to minimize the risk of being wrong" ([[ODNI - ICD 203, Analytic Standards (2015).pdf#page=4|ICD 203, §D.6.e(8)]]).
9. Uses effective visual information where appropriate ([[ODNI - ICD 203, Analytic Standards (2015).pdf#page=4|ICD 203, §D.6.e(5)–(9)]]).

**Beyond the standards**
- They are the criteria for each IC element's program of product evaluation, and a common foundation for analytic training ([[ODNI - ICD 203, Analytic Standards (2015).pdf#page=1|ICD 203, §D.2–3]]).
- Analysts can take concerns about how the standards are applied to the [[Analytic Ombuds]] ([[ODNI - ICD 203, Analytic Standards (2015).pdf#page=5|ICD 203, §E.2]]).
- Products include personally identifiable information only for a specific analytic purpose ([[ODNI - ICD 203, Analytic Standards (2015).pdf#page=2|ICD 203, §D.5]]).
- The directive does not apply to "purely law enforcement information" ([[ODNI - ICD 203, Analytic Standards (2015).pdf#page=1|ICD 203, §C.2]]).

## Why It Matters
- Its likelihood scale, like the earlier guide it resembles, has no "never" and no "always" ([[Lowenthal - Intelligence Is NOT About Telling Truth to Power (2021).pdf#page=4|Lowenthal, p. 3]]).
- Stephen Marrin argues that its objectivity standard is unattainable as written, so every analyst falls short of it ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Preliminary conclusions"]]). See [[Analytic Objectivity]].
