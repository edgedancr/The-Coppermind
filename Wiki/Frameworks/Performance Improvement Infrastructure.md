> [!summary]
> Rob Johnston's central recommendation is a formal infrastructure for improving analytic performance through a repeated cycle of measuring, comparing, and intervening ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=124|Johnston, p. 108]]). He argues that proposed reorganizations, including those after the 9/11 Commission, seldom explain why one change would work better than another; ==what is needed is data, not a priori notions of effectiveness== ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=125|Johnston, p. 109]]).

## Key Points
**The process** ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=124|Johnston, p. 108]])
1. Measure actual analytic performance to create baseline data.
2. Determine ideal analytic performance and standards.
3. Compare actual with ideal performance.
4. Identify performance gaps.
5. Create interventions to improve performance.
6. Measure performance again to evaluate the interventions.

**Supporting assets** ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=124|Johnston, p. 108]]): basic and applied research programs, knowledge repositories, [[Community of Practice|communities of practice]], and performance improvement techniques.

**Measuring performance**
- Counting publications says little about quality ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=125|Johnston, p. 109]]).
- **Error and failure metrics:** he asks what an "analytic morbidity and mortality ratio" would be, and proposes accuracy and surprise as measures; see [[Intelligence Error and Intelligence Failure]] ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=126|Johnston, p. 110]]).
- With such data, expertise becomes "a function of performance," not tenure ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=126|Johnston, p. 110]]).

**Research programs** ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=127|Johnston, pp. 111–115]])
- Analytic performance research: baselines and validated metrics.
- Institutional memory: lessons-learned centers like those of the Defense and Energy Departments and NASA ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=128|Johnston, p. 112]]).
- Analytic psychology and cognition, building on [[Richards Heuer]] and others ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=129|Johnston, p. 113]]).
- Development and validation of analytic methods; see [[Tradecraft vs. Science in Intelligence Analysis]] ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=130|Johnston, p. 114]]).
- Training effectiveness: test student performance before and after courses ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=130|Johnston, pp. 114–115]]).
- Organizational culture: track cultural markers such as language, norms, and taboos ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=131|Johnston, p. 115]]).

**Access:** research needs open, regular access between researchers and the Intelligence Community, and "access equals trust" ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=132|Johnston, p. 116]]).
