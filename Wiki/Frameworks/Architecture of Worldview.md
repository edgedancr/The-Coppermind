The Architecture of Worldview is a layered model of the questions every worldview answers, from theology at the core out to culture ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#8/27/26|SAIL notes, 8/27/26]]). SAIL called theology "the invisible core around which all existence is ordered and animated" ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#8/27/26|SAIL notes, 8/27/26]]), and later applied the model to AI: ==when creating a worldview for an AI, start with the theology, then the pillars of philosophy, then society and culture== ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/29/26|SAIL notes, 10/29/26]]).

## Components
1. **Theology:** Who is God? What is His nature and character, His relationship to creation, and His authority?
2. **Ontology (reality):** What is real? What is the nature and structure of existence?
3. **Epistemology (knowledge):** How do we know what is true? What are the sources of knowledge and legitimate authority?
4. **Teleology (purpose):** What is the intended end? The trajectory of creation, history, and human life.
5. **Anthropology (human nature):** What is a human being? The source of identity, dignity, agency, and purpose.
6. **Ethics and morality:** What is right and wrong? What should we do?
7. **Societal and political organization:** How should authority, institutions, relationships, and communities be ordered?
8. **Cultural norms and symbols:** What practices, stories, customs, and institutions express and reinforce the underlying worldview?

Source for the list: ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#8/27/26|SAIL notes, 8/27/26]])

## How to Use It
1. Work through the layers in order, starting from theology. SAIL taught this order for building a worldview for an AI ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/29/26|SAIL notes, 10/29/26]]).
2. Write the answers out explicitly. SAIL warned that telling an AI to "think with a Christian worldview" will not make it think like you; you must feed it the worldview you want it to have ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/3/26|SAIL notes, 9/3/26]]). See [[Prompt Engineering]].
