> [!summary]
> Intro to Intelligence's "Collection Discipline Overview" compares the main collection disciplines, or "INTs," by what each one is good and bad at.[^1] The course's theme on collection synergy makes the same point from the other side: combining disciplines "compensate[s] for the shortcomings of each" and profits from their combined strengths.[^2] See [[All-Source Intelligence]].

## Components
All from [[SRC - Collection Discipline Overview|Collection Overview]], by row.

| Discipline | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|
| **HUMINT** (human sources) | Insight into plans and intentions; relatively inexpensive | Risks lives and political fallout; takes time to acquire and validate sources; dangles, false feeds, double agents |
| **OSINT** (open sources) | Readily available; the place to start all collection | Large volume; credibility of sources; less likely to offer what the clandestine INTs can |
| **GEOINT** (geospatial, including IMINT) | Graphic and compelling; familiar to policymakers; some targets readily available; done remotely | Requires interpretation; a static snapshot; weather, spoofing, and [[Denial and Deception\|denial and deception]]; expensive; ==overly compelling== |
| **SIGINT** (COMINT, ELINT, FISINT) | Insight into plans and intentions; voluminous; military targets communicate in regular patterns; done remotely | Signals may be encrypted or encoded; voluminous; targets may use silence, secure lines, or spoofing; expensive |
| **MASINT** (measurement and signature) | Extremely useful for issues such as proliferation; done remotely | Expensive; little understood by most users; needs extensive processing and exploitation |
| **FININT** (financial) | Extremely useful for counterterrorism and proliferation; can reveal covert relationships; done remotely | Often depends on fragile sources; actions take time to work; carried out by commercial entities |
| **ABI / POL / ADTECH** | Can reveal covert relationships; done remotely; extremely useful for issues such as counterintelligence | Large volume; requires interpretation; expensive; carried out by commercial entities; sources fragile if targets learn of the surveillance; incomplete where internet use is limited; legal limits differ between the U.S. and Europe |

## How to Use It
- The course's grammar summaries give largely the same strengths and weaknesses, citing Lowenthal,[^3] and add a contrast: IMINT (now GEOINT) essentially tells you what has happened, while SIGINT generally tells you what may happen.[^4]
- Some traits appear on both sides of the table: SIGINT lists "voluminous material" as a strength and a weakness, and GEOINT is "graphic and compelling" but also "overly compelling".[^5]
- For what each discipline is and who manages it, see [[Intelligence Collection Disciplines]].

## Limits and Cautions
- The overview defines ADTECH, advertising technology, as the "digital exhaust" or "behavioral surplus" (Zuboff's term) people leave online, collected "with or without their awareness and permission" to make behavioral prediction products.[^6] See [[Intelligence Collection Disciplines]] for ABI and POL.
