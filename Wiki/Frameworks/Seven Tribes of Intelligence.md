> [!summary]
> Intro to Intelligence described intelligence culture as seven "tribes," and taught that ==where an organization locates its power, or its most coveted positions, shows its mission priorities and culture== ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/8/26|Intro notes, 9/8/26]]).

## Components
1. **National** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/8/26|Intro notes, 9/8/26]])
	- *National Security Agency (NSA):* power positions in the field sites (RSOCs). Culture: collection capability development; technical smarts; "hobby shop."
	- *National Reconnaissance Office (NRO):* power positions in the ground stations. Culture: collection capability development; technical and management smarts; billion-dollar programs.
	- *Central Intelligence Agency (CIA):* power positions in the embassies and NOCs (foreign undercover work). The CIA is the functional manager of human intelligence (HUMINT) and runs covert operations (see [[Covert Action]]).
2. **Military** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/8/26|Intro notes, 9/8/26]])
	- *Air Force:* power in the wing. Culture: deliberation; intelligence.
	- *Army:* power in the company. Culture: urgency; dedication and hard work.
	- *Navy:* power in the battle group or carrier. Culture: independence of action; self-contained resources.
3. **Law enforcement** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/8/26|Intro notes, 9/8/26]])
	- *FBI:* power in the field office. Culture: law-enforcement urgency; dedication and hard work; numbers of agents.

5. **NGOs and media**
6. **Academic**
7. **Business**
