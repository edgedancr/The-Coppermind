> [!summary]
> Rob Johnston's 2005 ethnographic study, *Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community*, ends with recommendations that a course handout sums up in five points ([[SRC - Johnston Recommendations Summary|Johnston summary, heading]]). The book itself starts from one structural problem: current reporting competes with [[Indications and Warning|indications and warning]] (I&W) intelligence for time and resources ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=123|Johnston, p. 107]]). It closes with the warning that ==culture and product "are inseparable"==, so an organization must manage its culture "or to be managed by it" ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=132|Johnston, p. 116]]).

## Key Points
1. **Improve organizational socialization:** give recruits accurate career information and realistic job previews; improve screening with cognitive measures, structured and panel interviews, and situational exercises; and expand degree programs in intelligence analysis ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=120|Konya & Johnston, pp. 104–106]]). See [[Anticipatory Socialization]].
2. **Recognize that current reporting dominates:** current intelligence brings face time with the policymakers who fund the community, while in-depth warning research pays off only in the abstract and successful warnings are taken for granted ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=123|Johnston, p. 107]]). Johnston asks whether reporting and warning should be separate career tracks ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=124|Johnston, p. 108]]). See [[Current vs. Long-Term Intelligence]].
3. **Build a performance improvement infrastructure:** measure performance, set standards, find the gaps, intervene, and measure again, supported by research programs ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=124|Johnston, pp. 108–115]]). See [[Performance Improvement Infrastructure]].
4. **Make training effective:** formal basic and advanced training is the best route to consistent methods, but agencies' programs differ and rarely evaluate student performance ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=130|Johnston, p. 114]]); he wants measures before and after each course ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=131|Johnston, p. 115]]).
5. **Develop cultural markers:** baseline ethnographic data and regular measurement of language, norms, and taboos, so interventions can be adjusted before they become ritualized ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=131|Johnston, p. 115]]).

**Beyond the handout**
- **Research programs:** analytic performance metrics, lessons-learned centers for institutional memory, research on analysts' cognition, and testing analytic methods against each other ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=127|Johnston, pp. 111–114]]). See [[Tradecraft vs. Science in Intelligence Analysis]].
- **Access:** research needs regular, open access between researchers and the community, and "access equals trust" ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=132|Johnston, p. 116]]).

## Why It Matters
- The recommendations answer the findings in [[Analytic Culture]].
- Gartin quotes Johnston's view that analysis is "art, tradecraft, and science" ([[Gartin - The Future of Analysis (2019).pdf#page=3|Gartin, p. 3]]); the phrase is on p. 43 of the book ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=62|Johnston, p. 43]]).
