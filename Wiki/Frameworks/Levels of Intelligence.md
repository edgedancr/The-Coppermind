> [!summary]
> Intro to Intelligence sorted intelligence analysis and reporting into three levels, strategic, operational, and tactical, and by the time horizon and questions each answers.[^1] The course later added technical intelligence as a fourth type,[^2] and a guest lecture showed the same three levels at work in police intelligence.[^3]

## Components
**The levels**[^1]
- **Strategic (national or global):** strategic current events, strategic trends, and policy and existential capabilities (technology capabilities).
- **Operational (regional):** major force activities in or to the field.
- **Tactical (local or field):** "forces" in the field: diplomatic, military, economic, social, technical, and so on.

**In the course's seven points**:[^4] strategic is the largest scope and long-term, operational is regional and mid-term, tactical is line-of-sight and immediate, and **technical** covers threat capabilities, performance, and vulnerabilities, and engineering data.

**Policy vs. tactical needs:** Lowenthal says policymakers need tailored, timely background, warning, and assessments, while tactical users need "dominant battlefield awareness" in real or near-real time.[^5]

**Time horizons:** current events, then trends, then long-term assessments.[^1]

**The questions, in order of analytic depth**[^1]
1. Who? What? When? Where? How? → reports, summaries, patterns, trends
2. Why? → cause and effect
3. What does it mean? → evaluate and judge
4. ==What happens next? → forecasts and potential futures==

## How to Use It
**In police intelligence:** in an Intro to Intelligence guest lecture, Detective Mennen of the NYPD described the same levels by time horizon:[^3]
- **Tactical:** immediate. Officer safety, suspect identification, tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs), location, the pattern of a crime series happening now. Example: breaking in a door.
- **Operational:** weeks to months. Networks, finances, communications and messaging, and targeting packages that drive a case or task force. Example: the financing of terrorist organizations.
- **Strategic:** months to years. Trends, threat assessments, and resource decisions for command staff.
