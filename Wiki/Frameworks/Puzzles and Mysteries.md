> [!summary]
> Intro to Intelligence distinguished two kinds of intelligence question: **puzzles**, which can in principle be answered, and **mysteries**, which cannot be answered with certainty and must be inferred ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/8/26|Intro notes, 9/8/26]]). The course taught it under the heading "Connecting the Dots," with the warning that ==dots can be exactly the same but have completely different implications based on context== ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/8/26|Intro notes, 9/8/26]]).

## Components
| | Puzzles | Mysteries |
|---|---|---|
| **Answerable?** | In principle; knowable, even if not actually known | Not with certainty |
| **How known** | Can be observed; needn't be inferred | Must be inferred, not observed |
| **Information** | Secret, inside information | Open-source, public information |
| **Questions** | Who, what, where, when | Why, how |
| **Purpose** | Tactical (minutes, hours): diplomatic tactics and military operations | Strategic (months, years): diplomatic objectives and future military capabilities and intentions |
| **Timeframe** | History to now (facts, figures) | Alternative futures; longer term (qualitative, trends) |
| **Issues** | Missing pieces; static unknowns; information scarcity | Influence of U.S. actions still to be determined (dynamic); unknown-unknowns; overwhelming flow of information |

Source for the table: ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/8/26|Intro notes, 9/8/26]])

## How to Use It
**Examples given** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/8/26|Intro notes, 9/8/26]])
- *Puzzles:* How many missiles does Iran have in reserve? In the Cold War: the size of the Soviet economy, and the number, capabilities, locations, and launch status of Soviet missiles.
- *Mysteries:* What will Iran really do once it has a nuclear weapon system ready to use? Is China going to invade Taiwan, and when? In the Cold War: Russia's inflation rate, and whether China would intervene in North Korea if U.S. attacks moved toward the Yalu River.
