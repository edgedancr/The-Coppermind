> [!summary]
> Intro to Intelligence teaches 13 themes in U.S. intelligence, ==persistent issues from history that keep coming back==, grouped as organization, people, process, and technology ([[SRC - Intelligence in National Security, Definition in 7 Points|7 Points, pt. 6]]). The course defines each one mostly in the words of Mark Lowenthal ("L") and Gregory Treverton ("T"), with textbook page numbers ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, themes 1–13]]). 

## Components
Taglines are from ([[SRC - SI Themes and Pearl Harbor Context|SI Themes]]); definitions from the theme summary, cited by theme number.

**Organization**
1. **Role of culture** ("Importance of IC & national security organizations and understanding foreign cultures"). The CIA's operations culture is powerful but "fundamentally at odds with accountability" (T: 145); its standard is "good enough to act," the FBI's "beyond a reasonable doubt" (T: 167). Foreign cultures complicate U.S. decisions too (the instructor's note) ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 1]]). See [[Seven Tribes of Intelligence]].
2. **Collection synergy** ("There are few 'silver bullets' in intelligence"). One discipline tips off another, and combining them offsets each one's weaknesses (L: 86–87, 115) ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 2]]). See [[All-Source Intelligence]].
3. **Redundant analytic structure** ("Importance of competitive analysis"). See [[Redundant Analytic Structure]] ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 3]]).

**People**

4. **Strategic vs. tactical intelligence** ("The long view and the short-term view"). Policymakers need tailored, timely background, warning, and assessments; tactical users need "dominant battlefield awareness" in near real time (L: 3–4, 122–123) ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 4]]). See [[Levels of Intelligence]].
5. **Policymakers' use of intelligence** ("How intelligence gets used—and abused"). See [[Consumer-Producer Relations]] ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 5]]).
6. **Politicization** ("Speaking truth to power vs. giving them what they want"). See [[Politicized Intelligence]] ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 6]]).

**Process**

7. **Consumer-producer relations** ("Understanding user information needs"). See [[Consumer-Producer Relations]] ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 7]]).
8. **Secrecy vs. openness** ("Information sharing and protection of sources and methods"). Secrecy is one reason intelligence agencies exist, but it clashes with the openness of representative democracy (L: 4, 18) ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 8]]).
9. **[[Denial and Deception]]** ("Preventing and misdirecting intelligence collection and analysis") ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 9]]).
10. **[[Mirror Imaging]]** ("Assuming 'they' think or do things just like we do") ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 10]]).

**Technology**

11. **Reliance on technology for collection** ("The American way of war and intelligence is with machines"). The U.S. has relied on technical collection since the 1940s, partly because the Soviet Union was closed (L: 17–18); now most of the world can be seen directly, "eyeball INT" (T: 9) ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 11]]).
12. **Reliance on technology for analysis and dissemination** ("The IT system is 'the solution'"). Treverton: IT is a means, not an end, and "cannot make sharing happen on its own" (T: 107–108) ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 12]]).
13. **Encryption** ("Technical solutions to information security and privacy issues"). Cheap, strong encryption will increasingly limit SIGINT to [[Traffic Analysis|traffic analysis]] (T: 88–89) ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 13]]).

## How to Use It
- In a case study, ask which themes it shows. The course's theme handout is titled "SI Themes & Pearl Harbor Context," pairing the themes with that case ([[SRC - SI Themes and Pearl Harbor Context|SI Themes]]). See [[Pearl Harbor (1941)]].

## Limits and Cautions
- The definitions quote two textbooks (Lowenthal's 5th edition and Treverton's 2003 book) that aren't in the vault, so their page numbers can't be checked here ([[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary]]).
