> [!summary]
> RAND researchers Jim Mitre and Joel B. Predd propose five hard problems that the possible emergence of [[Artificial General Intelligence|artificial general intelligence]] (AGI) poses for U.S. national security ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "About This Paper"]]). They offer them as ==a common language for debating AGI's risks and opportunities, and a rubric for evaluating strategies==, because proposals that advance one problem can undermine or ignore another ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "About This Paper"]]). Reach for it when judging whether an AI strategy is built for only one possible future.

## Components
**Overarching: endemic uncertainty.** It is unclear whether performance will keep scaling with compute (see [[Scaling Laws]]), where the threshold for AGI lies, or whether it will arrive suddenly or gradually; developers might not know a critical threshold has been crossed until it has ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "Endemic Uncertainty"]]).

1. **Wonder weapons.** AGI might produce a breakthrough with a decisive first-mover advantage, such as a "splendid first cyber strike," highly accurate prediction for military operations, or dominant autonomous weapons, or a "fog-of-war machine" that makes battlefield information untrustworthy ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "The Five Problems"]]).
2. **Systemic shifts in power.** AGI might upend military balances (hiders versus finders, precision versus mass), threaten democratic decision-making through advanced propaganda, or displace labor so fast that GDP soars while wages collapse ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "The Five Problems"]]).
3. **Nonexperts empowered to build weapons of mass destruction.** Models can act as "malicious mentors" that turn complex methods, for example for a pathogen or cyber malware, into accessible instructions, though knowing how is not the same as building ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "The Five Problems"]]).
4. **Artificial entities with agency.** Growing reliance on AI may erode human agency; AGI could act on the world stage, be misaligned with its designers' intentions, or, in a loss-of-control scenario, resist being turned off ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "The Five Problems"]]). See [[AI Alignment]].
5. **Instability.** The race for AGI could raise tensions like the nuclear arms race, in which perceived capabilities and rivals' intentions matter as much as actual capabilities ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "The Five Problems"]]).

## How to Use It
1. Test a proposed AI strategy against all five: does progress on one problem come at the cost of another? ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "The Five Problems"]])
2. Don't bet on a single future; the authors call any strategy over-optimized for one paradigm "a high-risk proposition" ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "Endemic Uncertainty"]]).
3. The authors' recommendations ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "Toward a Robust Strategy"]]): keep "no-regret" options that make sense in any future (STEM education, situational awareness, protecting model weights, and safety research); develop high-regret options and contingency plans before they're needed; and run scenario exercises on post-AGI shifts in military power and the economy.

## Limits and Cautions
- The authors say the problems overlap and may not cover everything policymakers will face ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "The Five Problems"]]).
- The problems rest on different assumptions. The wonder-weapon problem assumes the most ambitious ones, which posit high-consequence events of unknown probability ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "The Five Problems"]]).
- The paper defines AGI as human-level or even superhuman intelligence across a wide variety of cognitive tasks ([[RAND - Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems (2025).pdf|Mitre & Predd, "opening"]]), which differs from SAIL's definition; see [[Artificial General Intelligence]].
