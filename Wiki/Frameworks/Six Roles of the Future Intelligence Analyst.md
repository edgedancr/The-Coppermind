> [!summary]
> An Intro to Intelligence slide, adapted from Shay Hershkovitz's 2019 book *The Future of National Intelligence: How Emerging Technologies Will Reshape Intelligence Communities*, lists six roles for the future analyst.[^1] ==The slide's notes ask which is the "hardest part to change," and whether the shift is evolution or revolution==.[^2]

## Key Points
| Role | What the analyst does[^2] | Mainly involves (slide notes)[^2] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Connector | Uses sophisticated digital tools to find, package, and deliver information to key users | Process, technology |
| Curator | Presents information to highlight decision-relevant insights and their provenance | Process, people |
| Collaborator | Works with the broad intelligence ecosystem and with machines | People, process, technology, organization |
| Creator | Imagines, hypothesizes, visualizes, and designs new or alternative perspectives | People, technology |
| Critic | Challenges methods, reasoning, logic, and inferences in intelligence products | Process, people |
| Content-expert | Draws on a well of diverse subject-matter expertise | Process, technology, people |

- Two of the deck's eight copies of the slide add "provenance" to the Curator and "or alternative" to the Creator; the notes define provenance as a record of ownership used as a guide to authenticity or quality.[^3]

## Why It Matters
- Compare SAIL's five capabilities of a [[Centaur Analyst]], and Gartin's picture of the analyst in 2030 in [[Future of Intelligence Analysis]].
