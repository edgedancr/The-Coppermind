> [!summary]
> The 9/11 Commission named the enemy as twofold: al-Qaeda, "a stateless network of terrorists," and "a radical ideological movement in the Islamic world" that will menace Americans long after Bin Ladin is gone.[^1] Its recommended strategy rests on ==a "tripod" of policies: attack terrorists and their organizations; prevent the continued growth of Islamist terrorism; and protect against and prepare for terrorist attacks==.[^1]

## Key Points
**Principles**
- The enemy is not "some generic evil" but Islamist terrorism, especially al-Qaeda, its affiliates, and its ideology.[^2] "Islam is not the enemy".[^1]
- Long-term success "demands the use of all elements of national power": diplomacy, intelligence, covert action, law enforcement, economic policy, foreign aid, public diplomacy, and homeland defense.[^3]
- Goals need concrete objectives that reasonable observers can judge.[^4] Al-Qaeda was capable but "fragile, dependent on a few key personalities",[^4] and no president can promise that another 9/11 will not happen.[^5]

**1. Attack terrorists and their organizations**
- Identify and prioritize [[Terrorist Sanctuary|terrorist sanctuaries]], with long-term commitments to Pakistan and Afghanistan and an open reckoning with Saudi Arabia.[^6] For why sanctuary matters, see [[Requirements for a Complex Terrorist Operation]].

**2. Prevent the continued growth of Islamist terrorism**
- Engage the [[Struggle of Ideas|struggle of ideas]] and offer an agenda of educational and economic opportunity.[^7]
- Build a coalition strategy, including a common approach to the humane treatment of captured terrorists drawing on [[Article 3 of the Geneva Conventions]].[^8]
- Make a maximum effort against the spread of weapons of mass destruction, which al-Qaeda has tried to acquire or make "for at least ten years".[^9]
- Keep tracking terrorist money; see [[Al-Qaeda Financing]].[^10]

**3. Protect against and prepare for attacks**
- Target [[Terrorist Travel|terrorist travel]],[^11] and have the TSA run [[No-Fly List|no-fly and selectee]] screening.[^12]
- Safeguard privacy in information sharing, make the executive justify retaining new powers such as the Patriot Act's, and create an executive-branch board to oversee civil liberties.[^13]
- Base aid on risk ([[Risk-Based Homeland Security Assistance]]),[^14] adopt the [[Incident Command System]],[^15] and promote [[Private-Sector Preparedness|private-sector preparedness]].[^16]

## Why It Matters
- This is the Commission's "what to do"; chapter 13's reorganization, including the [[National Counterterrorism Center]] and the [[Director of National Intelligence]], is its "how to do it".[^17]
