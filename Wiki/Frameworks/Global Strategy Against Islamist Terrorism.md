> [!summary]
> The 9/11 Commission named the enemy as twofold: al-Qaeda, "a stateless network of terrorists," and "a radical ideological movement in the Islamic world" that will menace Americans long after Bin Ladin is gone ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=380|9/11 Report, p. 363]]). Its recommended strategy rests on ==a "tripod" of policies: attack terrorists and their organizations; prevent the continued growth of Islamist terrorism; and protect against and prepare for terrorist attacks== ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=380|9/11 Report, p. 363]]).

## Key Points
**Principles**
- The enemy is not "some generic evil" but Islamist terrorism, especially al-Qaeda, its affiliates, and its ideology ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=379|9/11 Report, p. 362]]). "Islam is not the enemy" ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=380|9/11 Report, p. 363]]).
- Long-term success "demands the use of all elements of national power": diplomacy, intelligence, covert action, law enforcement, economic policy, foreign aid, public diplomacy, and homeland defense ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=380|9/11 Report, pp. 363–364]]).
- Goals need concrete objectives that reasonable observers can judge ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=381|9/11 Report, p. 364]]). Al-Qaeda was capable but "fragile, dependent on a few key personalities" ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=381|9/11 Report, p. 364]]), and no president can promise that another 9/11 will not happen ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=382|9/11 Report, p. 365]]).

**1. Attack terrorists and their organizations**
- Identify and prioritize [[Terrorist Sanctuary|terrorist sanctuaries]], with long-term commitments to Pakistan and Afghanistan and an open reckoning with Saudi Arabia ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=384|9/11 Report, pp. 367–374]]). For why sanctuary matters, see [[Requirements for a Complex Terrorist Operation]].

**2. Prevent the continued growth of Islamist terrorism**
- Engage the [[Struggle of Ideas|struggle of ideas]] and offer an agenda of educational and economic opportunity ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=392|9/11 Report, pp. 375–379]]).
- Build a coalition strategy, including a common approach to the humane treatment of captured terrorists drawing on [[Article 3 of the Geneva Conventions]] ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=396|9/11 Report, pp. 379–380]]).
- Make a maximum effort against the spread of weapons of mass destruction, which al-Qaeda has tried to acquire or make "for at least ten years" ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=398|9/11 Report, p. 381]]).
- Keep tracking terrorist money; see [[Al-Qaeda Financing]] ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=398|9/11 Report, pp. 381–383]]).

**3. Protect against and prepare for attacks**
- Target [[Terrorist Travel|terrorist travel]] ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=402|9/11 Report, p. 385]]), and have the TSA run [[No-Fly List|no-fly and selectee]] screening ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=410|9/11 Report, p. 393]]).
- Safeguard privacy in information sharing, make the executive justify retaining new powers such as the Patriot Act's, and create an executive-branch board to oversee civil liberties ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=411|9/11 Report, pp. 394–395]]).
- Base aid on risk ([[Risk-Based Homeland Security Assistance]]) ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=413|9/11 Report, p. 396]]), adopt the [[Incident Command System]] ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=414|9/11 Report, p. 397]]), and promote [[Private-Sector Preparedness|private-sector preparedness]] ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=415|9/11 Report, p. 398]]).

## Why It Matters
- This is the Commission's "what to do"; chapter 13's reorganization, including the [[National Counterterrorism Center]] and the [[Director of National Intelligence]], is its "how to do it" ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=378|9/11 Report, p. 361]]).
