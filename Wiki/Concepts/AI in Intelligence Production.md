SAIL taught that AI fundamentally reshapes the intelligence production process in three ways: how intelligence is collected, how threats are predicted, and how policy is made ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/20/26|SAIL notes, 10/20/26]]).

## Key Points
1. **Collection enhancement:** AI dramatically expands what analysts can see and hear: volume, speed, and cross-language translation. ==The analyst's job shifts from finding information to judging it.== Examples given: the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency's (NGA) AI-assisted GEOINT, and Babel Street and Palantir Gotham ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/20/26|SAIL notes, 10/20/26]]).
2. **Predictive threat modeling:** AI correlates historical data to forecast instability and attack likelihood, but it is structurally blind to genuinely novel threats. The "unknown unknown" problem cannot be solved by adding more data. Examples given: IARPA's ACE program and DHS threat and hazard identification ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/20/26|SAIL notes, 10/20/26]]).
3. **Policymaking under AI:** AI-generated assessments now reach the highest levels of decision-making, and the core risk is [[Automation Bias]]. Example given: CIA internal LLMs (2023–24) ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/20/26|SAIL notes, 10/20/26]]).

- **The INT names define the output:** OSINT, GEOINT, and SIGINT name the finished, human-analyzed product, not the raw act of collection ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/20/26|SAIL notes, 10/20/26]]).
- For where AI and humans belong in this process, see [[Human Decision Point]] and [[Time Compression Trap]].
