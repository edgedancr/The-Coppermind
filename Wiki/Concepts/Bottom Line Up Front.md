> [!summary]
> Bottom line up front (BLUF) means leading with the main point. In the BLUF format, the first sentence of each paragraph sums up everything in it, and the details follow from most to least important.[^1] ==Policymakers and commanders are often too busy to read every word, so leading with the point lets them skim without missing what matters==.[^1] Kaiser and Pherson note that analytic writing borrows this convention from journalism, unlike academic writing, which builds toward its conclusion.[^2]

## Key Points
**The BLUF handout**
- **The umbrella:** a good BLUF covers all the information in its paragraph "like an umbrella"; anything outside it moves to another paragraph, or the BLUF changes.[^1]
- **Parts of a product:** title, executive summary (in the example, [[Key Judgments]]), introduction, background, analysis, context, and outlook. The title, executive summary, and introduction are written last, because they summarize everything else.[^3]
- **The outlook** sums up the paper and should never introduce new ideas.[^4]
- **The title** is "the ultimate BLUF".[^5] See [[Analytic Title]].
- **No editorializing:** the analysis must rest on facts analyzed logically and, as far as possible, without bias.[^6]
- **Accuracy, brevity, and clarity** leave nothing to interpretation and reduce the chance a decision maker misreads the analysis.[^7]

**Kaiser and Pherson**
- A message-driven paper puts the title, lead sentence, and key judgments first, and the most important part of most assessments comes at the end, in the outlook and implications; see [[Inverted Triangle]].[^2]

**The Intro to Intelligence slides**
- The first sentence of an article states its recommendation or implications, and the first sentences of its paragraphs form an outline of its key points.[^8]
- ICD 203 likewise requires a "clear main analytic message up front".[^9]

> [!caution] Accuracy check
> The Williams and Molzon slide cites ICD 203 section D.6.e(5) for this rule.[^10] In the directive, (5) is customer relevance; the main analytic message up front is in (6).[^11]
> Possibly resolved by [[SRC - ICD 203, Analytic Standards]], §D.6.e(6)

## Why It Matters
- At the paragraph level, the BLUF is the [[Topic Sentence|topic sentence]]; see [[Six Qualities of an Analytic Topic Sentence]] and [[Intelligence Writing Style]].
