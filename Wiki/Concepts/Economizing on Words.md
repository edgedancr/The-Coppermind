> [!summary]
> Kaiser and Pherson remind writers that busy readers want no more words than they need to understand the point; when unsure whether to delete something, cut it ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 9-11 (scan).pdf#page=6|Kaiser & Pherson, pp. 66–67]]). ==The Williams and Molzon slides make the same point by rewriting a 113-word paragraph in 34 words== ([[SRC - Intelligence Writing (Williams and Molzon Slides)|Williams & Molzon, slides 8–9]]).

## Key Points
**Four ways to tighten** ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 9-11 (scan).pdf#page=6|Kaiser & Pherson, pp. 66–67]])
1. **Delete redundant language:** compare ideas, not words, since readers process ideas.
2. **Delete excess verbiage:** "very," "also," and "then" are prime candidates, and "in order to" can lose "in order" ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 9-11 (scan).pdf#page=7|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 68]]).
3. **Delete unnecessary detail** that means little to the audience.
4. **Find shorter language** that says the same thing ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 9-11 (scan).pdf#page=9|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 70]]).
- Test each sentence with the [[No-Kidding Rule]] ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 9-11 (scan).pdf#page=7|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 68]]).

**The slides' version**
- The writer's job is to distill the most important information into the smallest space with the greatest clarity: "Omit needless [facts and] words!" ([[SRC - Intelligence Writing (Williams and Molzon Slides)|Williams & Molzon, slide 3]]).
- Drop qualifiers like "very" or "horrible" ([[SRC - Intelligence Writing (Williams and Molzon Slides)|Williams & Molzon, slide 7]]).
- **The example:** the 113-word paragraph mixes loaded adjectives, an unrelated threat on the southern border, and garbled sentences; the 34-word rewrite keeps only the judgment and the recommendation ([[SRC - Intelligence Writing (Williams and Molzon Slides)|Williams & Molzon, slides 8–9]]).

**The BLUF handout's version:** brief writing gets to the point without extra words, which increases clarity ([[BLUF Writing Format - How to Write an Intelligence Product (Henley-Putnam).pdf#page=8|BLUF handout, p. 8]]).

## Why It Matters
- It is the third of Kaiser and Pherson's four refining steps, after reviewing paragraphs and before the [[Self-Editing and Final Review|final review]]. Compare [[Intelligence Writing Style]].
