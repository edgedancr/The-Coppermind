> [!summary]
> Intelligence officers often describe their role as "telling truth to power," and the DNI's 2017 ethics principles say "speak truth to power" ([[Lowenthal - Intelligence Is NOT About Telling Truth to Power (2021).pdf#page=2|Lowenthal, pp. 1–2]]). Mark Lowenthal calls the idea ==wrong, dangerous, and arrogant==, because intelligence conveys uncertainty rather than truth ([[Lowenthal - Intelligence Is NOT About Telling Truth to Power (2021).pdf#page=2|Lowenthal, pp. 1–3]]).

## Key Points
**Lowenthal's case**
- **Origin:** the phrase is credited to the civil rights leader Bayard Rustin, a Quaker, in 1942; a 1955 Quaker pamphlet on nonviolence took it as its title ([[Lowenthal - Intelligence Is NOT About Telling Truth to Power (2021).pdf#page=2|Lowenthal, p. 1]]).
- **Uncertainty:** analysts rarely have a complete picture. If analysis overstates certainty, policymakers are more likely to make bad decisions, and the fault lies with the analyst ([[Lowenthal - Intelligence Is NOT About Telling Truth to Power (2021).pdf#page=3|Lowenthal, p. 2]]). "Truth is an absolute," so truth leaves no room for uncertainty ([[Lowenthal - Intelligence Is NOT About Telling Truth to Power (2021).pdf#page=3|Lowenthal, p. 2]]).
- **Compromise:** most analysis comes from several analysts or agencies, and truth cannot be the outcome of a compromise ([[Lowenthal - Intelligence Is NOT About Telling Truth to Power (2021).pdf#page=4|Lowenthal, p. 3]]).
- **Misplaced reliance:** claiming truth invites policymakers to rely on analysis more than it warrants. They may reject it for good reasons, including information analysts do not have ([[Lowenthal - Intelligence Is NOT About Telling Truth to Power (2021).pdf#page=4|Lowenthal, p. 3]]).
- **A better phrase:** John Moseman's "speaking honesty to power," which admits fallibility ([[Lowenthal - Intelligence Is NOT About Telling Truth to Power (2021).pdf#page=5|Lowenthal, p. 4]]).

**Marrin's case**
- The slogan embodies the "heroic, idealized myth" of the objective analyst ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Analytic objectivity embedded in idealistic myths and slogans"]]).
- Jack Davis proposed a more realistic standard, "tell it like they THINK it is," which Marrin equates with "calling it as you see it" ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Analytic objectivity embedded in idealistic myths and slogans"]]).
- Jennifer Sims warns that analysts who claim the truth may forget that power and truth exist on both sides of the analyst-customer divide ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Analytic objectivity embedded in idealistic myths and slogans"]]).

> [!question] Whose view is this?
> Lowenthal's article is a short perspective piece. The DNI's ethics principles still use the phrase ([[Lowenthal - Intelligence Is NOT About Telling Truth to Power (2021).pdf#page=3|Lowenthal, p. 2]]).
