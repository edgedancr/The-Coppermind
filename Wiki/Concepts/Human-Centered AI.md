> [!summary]
> Human-centered AI (HCAI) is the approach of building AI to amplify human abilities rather than replace them. SAIL quoted IBM's 2022 definition:[^1]

> "Human-Centered AI (HCAI) is an emerging discipline intent on creating AI systems that amplify and augment rather than displace human abilities. HCAI seeks to preserve human control in a way that ensures [[Artificial Intelligence|artificial intelligence]] meets our needs while also operating transparently, delivering equitable outcomes, and respecting privacy." (IBM, 2022)

## Key Points
- Work as a human–AI team: ==augmentation, not abdication==.[^1]
- The **human** keeps judgment, responsibility, context, ethics, and final decision authority; the **AI** expands speed, pattern-finding, drafting, coding, analysis, and access to information.[^1]
- **Where the definition comes from:** an IBM Research explainer by Werner Geyer, Justin Weisz, Claudio Santos Pinhanez, and Elizabeth Daly, dated 31 March 2022, which argues that AI's long-term success depends on recognizing that people are critical to its design, operation, and use.[^2]
- **IBM's three research themes**:[^2] human-AI collaboration and co-creation, built on the value that "human + AI" is better than either alone;[^3] responsible and human-compatible AI, which must be fair, unbiased, secure, and ethically applied;[^4] and natural language interaction.[^5]
- **Trust through explanation:** in IBM's SCORE sales tool, explainable recommendations were critical to users trusting the system.[^4] See [[Explainable AI]].
- **The envisioned partnership:** people take the roles of specification, goal setting, steering, high-level creativity, curation, and governance, while AI augments them through inspiration, low-level detail work, and the ability to design at scale.[^6]
