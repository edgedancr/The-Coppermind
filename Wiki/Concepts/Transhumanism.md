> [!summary]
> Transhumanism, as [[John Lennox]] describes it, is the drive to use technology to overcome human limits: to "solve the problem of physical death as a technical problem" and to enhance human happiness through genetic engineering, cyborgs, and "all kinds of things."[^1] Lennox names it, with posthumanism, as the drive behind much of the push to treat AI as god-like, and argues that ==it repeats the Bible's first temptation: "you will be as gods."==[^1]

## Key Points
**Lennox's reading of Harari**[^1]
- His main example is Yuval Noah Harari's book *Homo Deus*, "the man who's God"; "the title of that book ought to warn us."
- In Lennox's summary, Harari holds that we got "from animals to humans" by natural processes, but now "we take it over," taking control of evolution and developing humans into gods; Harari adds, "well, small g gods, like the Greek gods."
- Lennox calls this "a very old concept."

**The motive**[^2]
- The BSF host suggests that a desire, deep in every human heart, to live eternally drives the passion behind transhumanism. Lennox agrees: "it's absolutely the passion that's driving it," and people like Harari say so openly.
- Some people, he says, are having their brains frozen (cryogenics) in the hope that one day they can upload their brains "onto silicon or something else" and have some kind of eternal life.

**Lennox's answer: "you're too late"**[^2]
- The problem of physical death "was solved 20 centuries ago when God raised Jesus from the dead."
- Christ gives eternal life to those who repent and trust him as savior and lord, and at the end there will be "the biggest uploading ever." Lennox claims there is "far more evidence" for this than for the transhumanist project.
- Because "what Christ promises actually is what they're dreaming of," he says transhumanism lets him present the gospel in terms its believers "immediately understand," though the promise is conditional on repentance and trust in Christ.

**The biblical parallel**[^1]
- Lennox traces "all the damage" in the world to the lie in Genesis: "If you disobey God and eat the fruit of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, you will be as gods."
- That pressure to become godlike "has been seen all through history," he says, and now appears "utterly explicitly" in the AI or post-human world.

## Why It Matters
- It is Lennox's explanation for why some people come to worship AI; see [[AI Idolatry]].[^1]
- Compare the [[Technological Singularity]], one definition of which, Lennox says, is that humans will eventually merge with machines,[^1] and [[AI Succession]].
