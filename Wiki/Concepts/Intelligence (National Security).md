> [!summary]
> Intro to Intelligence defined intelligence most simply as "information about those who would do us harm" ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#8/25/26|Intro notes, 8/25/26]]), and more fully as the capability to inform decisions in time for it to make a difference ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/1/26|Intro notes, 9/1/26]]). The course treated it as three things at once: a product, a process, and an organization ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#8/25/26|Intro notes, 8/25/26]], [[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/1/26|Intro notes, 9/1/26]]).

## Key Points
**Why intelligence agencies exist** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/1/26|Intro notes, 9/1/26]])
- To avoid surprises.
- To cultivate long-term expertise.
- To inform and support policymakers.
- To keep secret what we need to know, how we find it out, and the sources and methods behind classified information.

**Intelligence is not "truth"**
- ==Intelligence is closer to "proximate reality"==: as close as possible to objective truth, while accounting for the credibility of sources, the information still missing, and above all the unknown-unknowns ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/1/26|Intro notes, 9/1/26]]).
- "The enemy always gets a vote": even with detailed information, the enemy may think or decide differently than expected ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#8/27/26|Intro notes, 8/27/26]]).

**Who it serves:** customers include law enforcement and policymakers ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/1/26|Intro notes, 9/1/26]]). The national security strategy is the *supported* strategy, and the national intelligence strategy is the *supporting* one ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/1/26|Intro notes, 9/1/26]]).

**Qualities of good intelligence** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/13/26|Intro notes, 10/13/26]]): timely, tailored, concise, validated, digestible, and stating what is known and what is not.

**Related terms:** [[All-Source Intelligence]], [[Analytic Confidence]], [[Key Judgments]], [[Mirror Imaging]], [[Priority Creep]], [[Indicators of Compromise]], [[Computer Network Exploitation]], [[Computer Network Attack]].

## Why It Matters
- Providing actionable intelligence is "an invaluable skill in the IC" ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#8/25/26|Intro notes, 8/25/26]]).
- In an Intro to Intelligence guest lecture, CDR Blocker put it this way: "Information shapes decisions. Decisions shape history." ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#8/27/26|Intro notes, 8/27/26]])
- For how intelligence is produced, see [[Tasking, Collection, Processing, Exploitation, and Dissemination]], [[Information Model]], and [[Levels of Intelligence]].
