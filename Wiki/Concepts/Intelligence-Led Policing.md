> [!summary]
> In an Intro to Intelligence guest lecture, Detective Mennen, an NYPD intelligence detective, described intelligence-led policing, in which ==intelligence is part of the operational cycle, "not a packet handed off after the fact"== ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/1/26|Intro notes, 10/1/26]]). A later lecture contrasted the two cultures involved: law enforcement, built around prosecution, and the IC, built around prevention ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/6/26|Intro notes, 10/6/26]]).

## Key Points
**What changed** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/1/26|Intro notes, 10/1/26]])
- Digital evidence, OSINT, CCTV video, and financial records overwhelm a detective working alone.
- Centers now sit between agencies: the Northern Virginia Retail Intel Center (NVRIC), the Virginia Fusion Center, High Intensity Drug Trafficking Area (HIDTA) investigative support centers, and real-time crime centers, rooms with cameras and license plate readers that monitor crime continuously.
- The NYPD keeps liaison detectives in other regions and around the world, because people from everywhere pass through New York every day.

**Intel-led policing is the norm** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/1/26|Intro notes, 10/1/26]])
- Commanders expect analysts to identify prolific offenders and networks before the next cluster of crimes.
- In good shops, analysts and investigators sit together.
- AI and link analysis speed the first cut; vetting, law, and ethics still belong to people.
- "Operations without intelligence is activity. Intelligence without operations is a briefing."

**Day-to-day work** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/1/26|Intro notes, 10/1/26]])
- Threat analysis for events: researching past attacks on similar events to decide what to protect against, such as at the Thanksgiving Day Parade.
- Racially or ethnically motivated violent extremists (REMVE) are a major problem.
- Watching significant anniversaries, such as Hitler's birthday, because people like to act on symbolic dates.

**Prosecution vs. prevention** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/6/26|Intro notes, 10/6/26]])

| | Prosecution | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| **Culture** | Law enforcement | Intelligence Community |
| **Focus** | Action: identify, apprehend, and prosecute offenders | Analysis: anticipate, assess, and mitigate threats |
| **Primary goal** | Public safety and justice | National security and strategic foresight |

- "A police officer sees a crime to be punished. An intel officer sees a puzzle to be solved. Neither is wrong; their mandates demand different realities." One looks for admissible evidence and immediate threats, the other for logistics hubs and financial networks ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/6/26|Intro notes, 10/6/26]]).
