> [!summary]
> Intro to Intelligence taught two modes of thinking: **System 1**, which works automatically and quickly with little or no voluntary control, and **System 2**, which gives effortful attention to the mental work that demands it ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/13/26|Intro notes, 10/13/26]]). ==Humans often default to being "lazy controllers," overusing System 1== ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/13/26|Intro notes, 10/13/26]]).

## Key Points
| | System 1 (fast) | System 2 (slow) |
|---|---|---|
| **Effort** | Effortless | Effortful |
| **Control** | Involuntary (automatic) | Voluntary (deliberate) |
| **Capacity** | Effectively unlimited (parallel processing) | Limited (serial processing) |
| **Used for** | Perception, intuition, reflection | Reasoning, analysis, choice; complex computation and concentration |

Source for the table: ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/13/26|Intro notes, 10/13/26]])

- **WYSIATI ("what you see is all there is"):** recorded in my notes as being aware of what *we know*, what *we know we don't know*, and what *we don't know we don't know* ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/13/26|Intro notes, 10/13/26]]).

> [!caution] Accuracy check
> WYSIATI usually names the bias itself: judging only from the information in front of us, without accounting for what is missing. My notes record the remedy (awareness of known and unknown unknowns). Check which was taught.

> [!warning] Unsourced background — verify or remove
> The lecture heading ("Thinking Systems, Fast and Slow") and the System 1 / System 2 terms come from Daniel Kahneman's *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011). My notes don't name him.

## Why It Matters
