> [!summary]
> The *imago Dei* is the teaching that human beings are made in the image of God. SAIL taught it as a foundation for ethics in intelligence work and AI: ==it is what we are protecting, or potentially violating, in our line of work==, so we must understand it when weighing risks to humanity from AI.[^1]

## Key Points
SAIL described God, and humans made in His image, as:[^1]
- **Personal:** each person has a personal center of identity, often associated with the soul or mind. Personhood is the basis of consciousness, personal memory, self-consciousness, self-direction, purpose, and intentionality.
- **Spiritual:** God is a spirit, and humans are created as spirit-beings with an innate inclination to worship Him in spirit and truth.
- **Relational:** humans do not just relate; they need relationships, and find fulfillment only when properly related to God and to other people, the tasks Jesus described as the first and second greatest commandments.
- **Rational and creative:** exercising reason and creativity reflects the nature of God.
- **Volitional and free:** God is free and sovereign and created humans with free will, "one of the most precious but dangerous gifts," because it makes rebellion against God possible.
- **Moral, responsible, and emotional.**

**[[John Lennox]]'s account**
- Lennox says Christians believe human life is valuable "because it's made in the image of God." He argues we need "more and more" to think about what that means "vis-à-vis the status of the machines we're … constructing, which are made in our image."[^3]
- One of the biggest things scripture reveals about human beings, he says, is that they are made in God's image and "can communicate with God" and "have a relationship with God," which AI cannot do.[^3]
- So people should not think "the value of a human being consists in their intelligence": relationships, love, and beauty are things AI systems "cannot do because they are not conscious."[^3] See [[Intelligence vs. Consciousness]].
- He notes that "a concept of sin" is "very noticeably absent in the AI community," with "no spiritual dimension, very little moral dimension."[^3]
- People who become Christians, he adds, realize they are valued "at the level that Christ died for us."[^4]

## Why It Matters
- It grounds SAIL's claim that AI cannot be conscious; see [[Intelligence vs. Consciousness]].[^2]
- It frames the analyst as a "sub-creator"; see [[Intelligence as Attentive Understanding]].[^1]
