> [!summary]
> The U.S. Intelligence Community (IC) is made up of 18 organizations, headed by the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI).[^1] Intro to Intelligence grouped them by where they sit in government: independent agencies, Department of Defense (now Department of War) elements, and departmental elements whose main mission is not intelligence.[^2]

## Key Points
**The groups under Executive Order 12333, Section 1.7**[^3]
- **Independent:** ODNI and the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA).
- **Department of Defense:** the National Security Agency (NSA), National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA), National Reconnaissance Office (NRO), Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA), and the intelligence elements of the Army, Navy, Air Force, and Marine Corps.
- **Departmental:** FBI and DEA (Justice), INR (State), OIA (Treasury), I&C (Energy), and I&A and the Coast Guard (Homeland Security).

**How the course first described them**[^1]
- ==Departmental organizations don't have intelligence as their primary goal, but use it as a backbone for everything else==: FBI, DHS, State, Energy, Treasury, and DEA.
- The defense elements are more military-oriented, and include the Space Force.
- The Coast Guard has both wartime and intelligence authorities, so it fits neither category.
- ODNI is the head of intelligence, and the CIA is independent.

**Functional managers:** the CIA for human intelligence and open-source intelligence, the NSA for signals intelligence, and the NGA for geospatial intelligence.[^4] The course's IC handout adds DIA as the functional manager for measurement and signature intelligence (MASINT).[^5] See [[Executive Order 12333]].

**In 2004:** the 9/11 Commission counted 15 agencies or parts of agencies in the IC, then headed by the Director of Central Intelligence.[^6] See [[Director of National Intelligence]].
**Oversight:** [[Intelligence Oversight Committees]]. **Culture:** [[Seven Tribes of Intelligence]].

> [!caution] Accuracy check
> My notes (8/25) say the CIA "reports directly to the president." Since the 2004 intelligence reform, the CIA Director reports to the Director of National Intelligence. Check what was taught.
> Possibly resolved by [[SRC - The 9-11 Commission Report]], p. 86: before the 2004 reform, the CIA was the only IC element independent of a cabinet department, and "the CIA's number one customer is the president."

## Why It Matters
- For each member's mission, and the course's other grouping into program managers, departmental organizations, and military service organizations, see [[Intelligence Community Elements and Missions]].[^5]
- The agency an analyst works for shapes its priorities and culture; see [[Seven Tribes of Intelligence]].[^7]
