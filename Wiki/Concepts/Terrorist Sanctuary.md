> [!summary]
> The 9/11 Commission found that a 9/11-scale plot "cannot be mounted by just anyone in any place": it needs the time, space, command structure, recruits, logistics, and communications a sanctuary provides.[^1] Its first recommendation for attacking terrorists: ==the U.S. government "must identify and prioritize actual or potential terrorist sanctuaries,"== with a realistic strategy for each to keep terrorists "insecure and on the run".[^2]

## Key Points
- **What Afghanistan gave al-Qaeda:** ties among veteran jihadists, space to gather and sift recruits in isolated camps, and logistics networks running through Pakistan and the United Arab Emirates.[^3] Al-Qaeda also exploited lax internal security in Western countries, especially Germany, and used the United States itself as a staging area.[^3]
- **Where havens form:** an Intelligence Community map, made without secret intelligence, marks areas that combine rugged terrain, weak governance, room to hide or receive supplies, and low population density, near a town or city.[^3]
- **Officials' lists:** western Pakistan and the Pakistan-Afghanistan border, southern or western Afghanistan, the Arabian Peninsula and the Horn of Africa, Southeast Asia, West Africa, and European cities with expatriate Muslim communities.[^4]
- **A shift in focus:** twentieth-century strategists studied the industrial heartlands; in the twenty-first century, "the focus is in the opposite direction, toward remote regions and failing states".[^2] If Iraq became a [[Failed State|failed state]], it would go to the top of the list.[^2]
- **Three illustrations (2004):** sustain aid to Pakistan from military support to education; make a long-term commitment so that Afghanistan does not again become a sanctuary; and confront the problems in the U.S.-Saudi relationship openly.[^5]

## Why It Matters
- It is the first leg of the [[Global Strategy Against Islamist Terrorism]]; for what a sanctuary supplies, see [[Requirements for a Complex Terrorist Operation]].
