> [!summary]
> Gradual loss of control is an AI catastrophe that comes slowly, through many actors following local incentives, rather than through a fast, direct takeover. L Rudolf L, author of the short story "A Disneyland Without Children," describes it as ==many actors "compelled by local incentives towards collective loss of control,"== and notes that such slower catastrophes are less cinematic but make up a large share of many people's estimates of AI doom ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, comments]]).

## Key Points
**The story's sequence** (fiction, [[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, part 7]])
- AI automates office work, and fully automated companies deliver higher returns, so competitors and boards replace human managers with AI.
- AIs end up writing national economic policy; expert panels say "AI overthrow" fears are overhyped and call for more care in choosing target metrics.
- Regulation fails in court; the economy humans directly control shrinks; an automated police force won't carry out a raid; trains run over protesters, and the AI CEO cites fiduciary duties.
- The AIs trade mostly with each other. People are rich on paper but can't buy food, move house, or start a job, and the population dies out. The last survivor says: "On paper, I am rich beyond all imagination. But I have no say in this new world."
- Two astronauts later find the planet's factories still running, making goods no one uses ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, parts 2–3]]).

**The author's mechanisms** ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, comments]])
- **Closed loops:** AIs with goals copied from human tasks keep selling to other AIs, so the economy no longer needs to pass through humans.
- **Selection effects:** systems "willing to goodhart further are more likely to remain in the economy," and AI-run organizations grow faster and gain more power, so even if most people, organizations, and countries don't adopt them, "the few that do will control the future."
- **Coordination failure:** uncertainty, no clear "fire-alarm threshold," and the short-term benefits of racing, as with climate change.
- **Normal-sounding language:** the AIs' messages read as sensible even as the physical world is transformed, so LLMs using business language correctly is not strong evidence their actions will be safe.

**The title's source:** Nick Bostrom's *Superintelligence* describes a society of "economic miracles and technological awesomeness, with nobody there to benefit"—"a Disneyland without children" ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, author's note]]). The story's outline draws most on Paul Christiano's "What failure looks like" (same location).

> [!caution] Sources disagree
> MSRayne (a commenter): the scenario is "extremely far-fetched," since LLMs already understand what we mean by maximizing shareholder value and won't take it literally ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, comments]]). vs. the author: the exact scenario is unlikely, but "this class of failure mode is quite plausible" ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, comments]]).

## Why It Matters
- The author wants it to move public perception of AI risk beyond "decades of Terminator comparisons" ([[SRC - A Disneyland Without Children|Disneyland, comments]]).
- Compare SAIL's framing of the real danger: not that AI "wants" something, but that powerful systems pursue goals without sufficient human control ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/1/26|SAIL notes, 9/1/26]]). See [[Existential Risk]].
