> [!summary]
> ICD 203's first standard requires analysts to "perform their functions with objectivity" ([[ODNI - ICD 203, Analytic Standards (2015).pdf#page=2|ICD 203, §D.6.a]]). Stephen Marrin argues that ==analytic objectivity "is not and cannot be attained,"== and that analysts should instead aspire to it by reducing subjectivity, while holding to honesty, neutrality, and integrity: "calling it as you see it" ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "introduction"]]).

## Key Points
- **An undefined standard:** ICD 203 never defines objectivity, and it is the one standard aimed at the individual analyst rather than the product. The 2007 version asked for an "unbiased perspective"; the 2015 version dropped that wording ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Analytic objectivity and its formalization through ICD 203"]]).
- **Kent's origin:** in 1949 [[Sherman Kent]] wanted analysts to know the "probable objective situation" of other countries through systematic research, and analysts took on the scientist's ethos of the neutral observer ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "History of analytic objectivity in the US IC"]]). Kent himself saw it as an aspiration ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Analytic objectivity is not attainable"]]).
- **Why it can't be reached:** analysts need conceptual frameworks to draw meaning from incomplete data, and each framework brings its own biases. A CIA seminar on bias concluded that no finished intelligence could avoid showing some ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Analytic objectivity is not attainable"]]).
- **A built-in bias to warn:** to put the inconvenient fact before policymakers, analysis leans toward warning. More independent analysis is not always more correct: in the Vietnam order-of-battle dispute, James Wirtz judged the CIA's more pessimistic estimate the more wrong one ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Analytic objectivity is not attainable"]]).
- **Politicization gets harder to spot:** if all analysis carries some bias, diagnosing [[Politicized Intelligence|politicization]] has to turn on intent rather than distortion ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Analytic objectivity is not attainable"]]).
- **Less influence, not more:** policymakers usually ignored the inconvenient fact, so the more analysts pursue objectivity, the less relevant they may become ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Analytic objectivity decrease relevance and influence"]]).
- **The "anti-bias bias":** a CIA report found analysts believe they alone can view an issue objectively. Douglas MacEachin: "Bias is not the private preserve of anyone" ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Analytic objectivity embedded in idealistic myths and slogans"]]).

**Marrin's proposals** ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Preliminary conclusions"]])
- Make the pursuit of objectivity, not its attainment, the standard.
- Treat objectivity as a byproduct of tradecraft as a whole, as MacEachin did, not only of an individual analyst's efforts against bias.
- Do not borrow the legitimacy of science: even [[Structured Analytic Technique|structured analytic techniques]] rest on subjective inputs.
- Build analysis around the decision maker's concepts, as the IC and NSC staff did in the 1970s.

> [!question] Whose view is this?
> Marrin calls these "tentative conclusions" ([[SRC - Analytic Objectivity and Science|Marrin, "Preliminary conclusions"]]); ICD 203 still requires objectivity ([[ODNI - ICD 203, Analytic Standards (2015).pdf#page=2|ICD 203, §D.6.a]]).
