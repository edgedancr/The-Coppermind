> [!summary]
> Intro to Intelligence presented three massive challenges described by Justin Overbaugh, Deputy Under Secretary of War for Intelligence and Security: the unraveling of the international order, a growing set of adversaries able to threaten the U.S. existentially in every domain, and technical challenges in every technology domain ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/27/26|Intro notes, 10/27/26]]). The course also introduced cognitive information threats: =="the decline of truth and the rise of narratives"== ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/27/26|Intro notes, 10/27/26]]).

## Key Points
**The three challenges** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/27/26|Intro notes, 10/27/26]])
1. International rules of order "unraveling" the political domain.
2. An expanding set of adversaries who can existentially threaten the U.S. in every operating domain: nuclear, conventional, economic, cyber, undersea, and space.
3. Technical challenges in every technology domain: AI, quantum computing, and DNA engineering.
	- Harnessing quantum entanglement could change the landscape for good.
	- Project Maven, recorded as "AI-automated drones that were being developed by Google for the US Government."

> [!caution] Accuracy check
> Project Maven is usually described as a Department of Defense program that uses AI to analyze drone footage, with Google as a contractor until 2018, not as Google-built drones. Check what was taught.

**Cognitive information threats** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/27/26|Intro notes, 10/27/26]])
- Rationality words have declined in public narratives, and intuition (feeling) words have taken their place.
- The internet has made sentiment analysis possible at the level of whole societies.
