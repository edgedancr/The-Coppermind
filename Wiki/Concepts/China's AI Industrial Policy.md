> [!summary]
> RAND researchers describe how China, aiming to lead the world in AI by 2030, is deploying industrial policy across the full [[AI Technology Stack|AI technology stack]], from chips to applications.[^1] They judge that ==the policy will likely accelerate China's AI progress but can't easily overcome U.S.-led export controls==, and that China will likely remain at least a close second to the United States.[^1]

## Key Points
**Goals**[^2]
- Beijing wants AI to advance economic development and military capability. Its AI discourse focuses on economic and industrial applications, not the "race to AGI" framing sometimes used in Washington.
- By 2030, it aims for AI to be a $100 billion industry that creates more than $1 trillion of added value in other industries, upgrading sectors such as health care, manufacturing, and agriculture, and powering robotics, autonomous vehicles, and unmanned systems.

**State support at each layer**[^3]

| Layer | Examples |
|---|---|
| Energy | 429 GW of new generating capacity in 2024, more than 15 times the U.S. addition |
| Chips | Huawei's Ascend chips; the $47 billion "Big Fund"; research on EUV lithography |
| Compute | A National Integrated Computing Network; "Eastern Data, Western Computing" hubs |
| Research and labs | National research grants; state-backed labs such as Zhejiang Lab and the Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence |
| Funding | An $8.2 billion National AI Industry Investment Fund; bank lending programs |
| Local government | AI pilot zones in 20 cities; "computing vouchers" for start-ups |
| Open source and data | The OpenI platform, the Gitee code host, data exchanges, and open datasets |
| Applications | "AI+" action plans; a push into robotics and "embodied intelligence" |

**Will it work?**[^4]
- Three bottlenecks decide the outcome: domestic chips and a sanction-resistant supply chain; talent; and energy, which China is likely to meet.
- It could backfire. Pushing firms onto immature domestic tools slows frontier work (iFlytek reported a three-month delay switching from Nvidia to Huawei chips); scarce chips may be misallocated; and state-backed firms attract foreign restrictions, as DeepSeek did.
- AI differs from sectors like shipbuilding and electric vehicles: it changes quickly and depends on talent and data, which respond poorly to capital subsidies. ==Private firms such as DeepSeek, not state firms, have led== China's AI development.
- By one estimate, U.S. AI companies receive more than ten times as much private investment as Chinese ones.
