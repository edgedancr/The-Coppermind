> [!summary]
> SAIL taught that AI compresses the lethal engagement cycle, in three main ways.[^1]

## Key Points
1. **Battlefield awareness and sensor fusion:** AI fuses air, land, sea, space, and cyber feeds into a near-real-time common operating picture. This compresses Boyd's [[OODA Loop]], letting commanders act inside the adversary's decision cycle.[^1]
2. **Targeting and the kill chain:** AI is now active in finding, fixing, tracking, and targeting, but ==the decision to engage, legally and ethically, requires a human==.[^1] See [[Kill Chain]] and [[Human Decision Point]].
3. **Autonomous systems and adversarial threats:** peer competitors are removing humans from the engagement loop, while U.S. systems remain vulnerable to [[Data and Model Poisoning|data poisoning]], sensor spoofing, and model manipulation.[^1]

[[John Lennox]] gives an example of the downside of military AI: "you can send out a drone swarm and they will identify and decide what enemy to attack and do it all without any human intervention at all."[^2] He compares advanced technologies to "a sharp knife": "You can use it for surgery or you can use it for murder," and the more advanced the technology, "the more you're going to have massive ethical problems associated with the downside."[^2]
