> [!summary]
> SAIL approached artificial intelligence (AI) by asking what each word means. *Artificial* means created by man, implying imitation or the absence of naturalness: a human skill or craft made real. *Intelligent* means having the capacity to solve problems.[^1]

## Key Points
- **A broad definition** from Marc Andreessen: "The application of mathematics and software code to teach computers how to understand, synthesize, and generate knowledge in ways similar to how people do it... A way to make everything we care about better."[^2]
- **IBM's definition:** AI is "trying to simulate with a computer something that would match or exceed human intelligence," where intelligence generally means the ability to learn, infer, and reason.[^3]
- **IBM's nested view:** machine learning sits inside AI, deep learning inside machine learning, and generative AI and [[Foundation Model|foundation models]] build on deep learning. AI began as a research project; expert systems, built with languages like Lisp and Prolog, spread in the 1980s and 1990s; machine learning and deep learning became popular in the 2010s.[^4] See [[Generative AI]].
- **The progression:** statistics → machine learning → deep learning → [[Reinforcement Learning|reinforcement learning]] → generative AI → agentic AI.[^1] See [[Machine Learning]], [[Deep Learning]], and [[Agentic AI]].
- **Acts like a human, doesn't think like one:** see [[Intelligence vs. Consciousness]].[^2]
- **By scope:** [[Narrow AI]] does a specific task really well;[^5] [[Artificial General Intelligence]] would do every human task better than every human.[^2]
- **Hard to measure:** traditional software gives objective data; AI gives subjective data.[^6] See [[LMSYS Chatbot Arena]].
