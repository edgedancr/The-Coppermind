> [!summary]
> In its discussion of "How to Read Tolkien and Why," SAIL argued that Strategic Intelligence (SI) is a liberal-arts practice of understanding human beings, cultures, and motives, and that literature trains the habits by which intelligence is done well.[^1] ==Frameworks and methods are the means; faithful understanding is the goal.==[^1]

## Key Points
- **Come to the thing itself:** to understand an essay or a book, read it and take the time to think about it.[^1]
- **Technique is not insight:** do not mistake reduction, deconstruction, or a clever framework for genuine insight. Analysts are stewards and observers, not dominators and destroyers.[^1]
- **The analyst as sub-creator:** "We construct assessments, but we do not construct reality." Primary creation is reality; sub-creation is the analytic assessment. Christian humility does not make analysis timid; it makes it properly ordered.[^1] See [[Imago Dei]].
- **Dominion vs. domination:** power must remain ordered to truth, stewardship, and human dignity. There is a difference between letting reality speak and forcing it into a chosen interpretation.[^1]
	- *Dominion:* stewardship, applicability, letting reality speak.
	- *Domination:* mastery, allegory (putting your will into the story for others to see, rather than letting them interpret for themselves), imposing will.
- **Faithful understanding:** good analysis requires both intellectual skill and moral formation. The faithful analyst is intellectually curious, theologically humble, imaginatively open, and morally alert.[^1]
