> [!summary]
> In an Intro to Intelligence guest lecture titled "Chip Wars," CDR Blocker argued that semiconductors are "the oil of the 21st century" and ==the most strategically important technology of our time==, because the Internet of Things, cyber, AI, and every advanced weapon system depend on them ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/29/26|Intro notes, 10/29/26]]).

## Key Points
All from CDR Blocker's guest lecture ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/29/26|Intro notes, 10/29/26]]).

**History**
- **Origins (1940s–1970s):** the transistor (Bell Labs, 1947), the integrated circuit (Kilby and Noyce), Moore's Law, and Cold War demand.
- **U.S. dominance (1970s–80s):** Intel and the microprocessor; chips embedded in military systems.
- **Japan's challenge (1980s):** DRAM dominance (NEC, Toshiba, Hitachi) and trade disputes; the U.S. pivoted to design-heavy chips.
- **Taiwan and South Korea (1990s–2000s):** TSMC and the foundry model (Morris Chang); Samsung and SK Hynix lead memory. Globalized supply chains brought efficiency and dependency.
- **China (2000s–2010s):** the realization that manufacturing is about engineering, not IP; China can steal the technology but not the innovation. The CCP invests in SMIC, YMTC, and Huawei HiSilicon, but remains 3–5 generations behind in cutting-edge logic.
- **Chips as strategic weapons (2020–):** COVID disrupted supply chains; U.S. export controls (2022–23) restricted China's access.

**The terrain**
- *Chips:* logic and processors, CPUs and GPUs (Taiwan); memory, DRAM and NAND (South Korea); specialized accelerators (NPUs; quantum emerging).
- *Tools:* EUV lithography (ASML, Netherlands); process equipment (Applied Materials, Lam, U.S.); manufacturing gear (Tokyo Electron, Japan).

**Why chips are strategic**
- Control of chips is control of military and economic power. Cyber risks include hardware backdoors and supply-chain infiltration.
- The AI revolution is a GPU revolution: the largest models need tens of thousands of GPUs, so access to chips is access to AI progress. U.S. export controls on NVIDIA A100 and H100 GPUs are explicitly about limiting China's AI growth.
- National security uses: autonomous weapons and target recognition, intelligence analysis at scale (SIGINT, GEOINT, MASINT), and cyber defense automation.

> [!caution] Accuracy check
> Two lines: Moore's Law as "technology doubles every 10 years" (usually stated as transistor counts doubling about every two years), and Kilby having "founded" Texas Instruments (he worked there; he didn't found it).

## Why It Matters
- No country controls the whole supply chain, which creates both interdependence and vulnerability. The U.S.–China rivalry over chips will shape AI, cyber power, and IoT infrastructure ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/29/26|Intro notes, 10/29/26]]).
