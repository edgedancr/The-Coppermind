> [!summary]
> A CIA analyst writing as Bobby W. argues that what analysts can produce and what consumers think they can produce "are often two different things": analysts give "scouting reports on opposing teams," while policymakers expect "the score of the game".[^1] ==Analysts forecast linear trends well but not breaks from the norm==, so they should be candid about their limits and "adopt a more modest analytic ethos".[^2]

## Key Points
**Three limits**[^3]
- **Trend lines and discontinuities:** extrapolations are usually right, which breeds false confidence. In 1983 DCI William Casey's Senior Review Panel traced the worst misjudgments to a [[Prejudice Toward Continuity|"prejudice toward continuity"]].
- **Timing and tipping points:** analysts can describe rising risk but not when the break will come, such as when the planes would hit the Twin Towers.
- **Humans and systems:** people with free will and complex systems are not deterministic, and accuracy dropped sharply for forecasts more than a few years out.

**A new ethos: the analyst as question answerer**[^4]
1. What is the context and reality of the problem I am facing?
2. How do new developments affect my problem?
3. What are the ways this situation could play out?
4. How do we get from here to there, and what should I look out for? Answer with [[Indicators and Signposts|signposts and indicators]].
5. How would "they" react to my different policy options?

**Doing better**[^5]
- Promote foundational research even over current production (see [[Current vs. Long-Term Intelligence]]), and let speculative if-then analysis "come in from the doghouse."
- Alternative analysis kept apart from mainline narratives has failed; instead, keep a "fire watch" for the first signs that a less likely future is coming (see [[Red Team Analysis]]).
- Hold analysts accountable for the accuracy of their forecasts, not only for good process, and build teams that think differently.
- Hire and train for the traits of [[Superforecaster|superforecasters]].
- AI and big data will help linear assessments but will not overcome the limits on prediction.

> [!question] Whose view is this?
> The author is identified only as "Bobby W.," an analyst in CIA's Directorate of Analysis; the journal notes the views are not official.[^6]

## Why It Matters
- It gives a reason for [[Estimative Language]] and [[Analytic Confidence]], and a caution to set against the optimism of [[Future of Intelligence Analysis]].
