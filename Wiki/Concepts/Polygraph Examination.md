> [!summary]
> Intro to Intelligence taught that ==the polygraph does not grant clearances==: it is a data-collection tool that helps adjudicators resolve each case fairly and consistently ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/29/26|Intro notes, 9/29/26]]). The instrument does not detect lies; it detects arousal of the sympathetic nervous system ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/29/26|Intro notes, 9/29/26]]).

## Key Points
All from ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/29/26|Intro notes, 9/29/26]]).

**Its place in the clearance process:** the polygraph elicits unrecorded historical and behavioral data, and the background investigation verifies documented historical data. Both feed adjudication, which leads to access to the trusted workforce.

**The instrument (five sensors):** two pneumographs (chest and stomach), electrodermal activity (fingers or palm), a cardiovascular sensor (arm), and a seat sensor.

**Elicitation:** the systematic process of "drawing out something hidden," by isolating the psychological stress of deception from normal baseline activity.

**What it covers**
- *Background:* illegal drug involvement, abuse of legal drugs, and serious criminal conduct. Deliberate falsification of security forms is one of the biggest red flags.
- *Counterintelligence:* espionage or sabotage against the U.S., intentionally compromising classified information, involvement in terrorist activity, secret foreign contacts, and deliberate damage to U.S. government IT systems.

**The 2–4 hour appointment**
1. *Administrative intake:* consent and a signed biographical worksheet.
2. *Responsibility fixing:* establishing the roles of examiner and examinee (audio and video recorded).
3. *Review phase:* a walkthrough of the instrument, the SF-86, and every question that will be asked. This is the time to ask for clarification, so nothing is flagged that isn't really a concern.
4. *Testing phase:* sensors attached; data collection begins.
5. *Diagnostic and resolution:* clarifying any irregular responses.

## Why It Matters
- Detective Mennen's advice on clearances was the same in spirit: tell the truth about drugs, social media, foreign contacts, and finances ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/1/26|Intro notes, 10/1/26]]). See [[Law Enforcement Intelligence Internships]].
