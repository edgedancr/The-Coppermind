> [!summary]
> Lowenthal's chapter on ethical and moral issues in intelligence, as summarized in Intro to Intelligence's grammar summaries, raises seven issues, from what working in secret does to people, to when covert action is just (Lowenthal ch. 13, as summarized in [[SRC - Grammar Summaries, Weeks 2-7|Grammar, wk 4]]). On covert action, ==the basic ethical issue is the legitimacy of such operations== (Lowenthal, L: 319, as summarized in [[SRC - Grammar Summaries, Weeks 2-7|Grammar, wk 4]]).

## Key Points
All from Lowenthal ch. 13, as summarized in [[SRC - Grammar Summaries, Weeks 2-7|Grammar, wk 4]].
1. **Costs of secrecy** (L: 307–8): "how operating in a secret milieu affects people," perhaps tempting them to "cut corners." Secrecy also limits building public support for intelligence.
2. **Middle ground** (L: 309): a relationship like that of the two Cold War antagonists occupies "a gray middle ground between peace and war," a time when collection and covert action are the best means of combat.
3. **Standards after the fact** (L: 311): imposing new standards on officers who "acted in good faith under old standards" or under a previous law.
4. **Responsibility to the source** (L: 313): in human intelligence collection, how deep a recruiter's obligation to a source is, how far the recruiter must go to protect the asset, and how long, and under what circumstances, the responsibility lasts.
5. **What constitutes torture** (L: 318): Lowenthal notes that within U.S. law torture is forbidden by the Eighth Amendment's ban on "cruel and unusual punishment," but that the war on terrorists has raised a debate over what counts as torture.
6. **Covert action that is just** (L: 319): covert actions are "interventions by one state in the affairs of another." See [[Covert Action]].
7. **The [[Helms Dilemma]]** (L: 322–23): Director of Central Intelligence Richard Helms, caught between keeping the CIA's involvement in Chile secret and his obligation to testify fully and honestly before Congress.
