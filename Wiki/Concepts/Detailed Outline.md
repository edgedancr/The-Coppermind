> [!summary]
> Kaiser and Pherson treat outlining as part of thinking, not only writing: the more detailed the outline, the better the odds of insightful and accurate analysis ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 4 (scan).pdf#page=3|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 21]]). ==The goal is a roadmap down to the paragraph level==, whose main points become the paper's topic sentences ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 4 (scan).pdf#page=4|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 22]]).

## Key Points
- **Start with a working title and a lead sentence,** or a working summary for a longer paper. A writer who cannot write the lead sentence needs to do more analysis ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 4 (scan).pdf#page=3|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 21]]). See [[Analytic Title]].
- **A precise [[Analytic Mission Statement|mission statement]]** makes the paper easier to organize, because a well-organized paper takes the shortest path to its judgments ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 4 (scan).pdf#page=3|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 21]]).
- **Fit the analysis to the facts,** not the facts to the analysis ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 4 (scan).pdf#page=3|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 21]]).
- **Why an outline stalls** ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 4 (scan).pdf#page=4|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 22]]):
	1. The writer has not focused on the audience's interests.
	2. The writer has not yet immersed themselves in the information.
	3. The writer has the facts but has not assembled them into a coherent story.
- **A filing system:** file each piece of information, not each report, by whether it concerns what happened, why, the impact, where it might go, or what it means for the audience; then look for patterns and tag each data point with a descriptor ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 4 (scan).pdf#page=4|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 22]]).
- **A shared wiki page** lets collaborating analysts pool data and interpretations as they work, which keeps individual mindsets from hardening ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 4 (scan).pdf#page=5|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 23]]).
- **Iterate:** new information and insights change the outline, and the cycle does not end until the paper is published ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 4 (scan).pdf#page=7|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 25]]).

## Why It Matters
- It turns the [[Analytic Line of March]] into paragraphs; Kaiser and Pherson's worked example outlines a paper on colonial Massachusetts for "the King of France" ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 4 (scan).pdf#page=6|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 24]]).
