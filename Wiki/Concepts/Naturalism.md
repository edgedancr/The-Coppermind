> [!summary]
> Naturalism, as [[C. S. Lewis]] uses the term, is the view that Nature, "the space–time–matter system," is the only thing in existence, with "no God and no life of some quite different sort somewhere outside Nature",[^1].[^2] In "On Living in an Atomic Age," Lewis argues that ==this, not the atomic bomb, is the important question==, and that naturalism cannot be believed because it undercuts the reasoning that tells us about Nature,[^1].[^2]

## Key Points
**Where naturalism leads, in Lewis's view**
- If Nature is all, every story ends "in NOTHING": astronomers and physicists offer no hope that life will last, and "Nature is a sinking ship".[^1]
- The bomb's real question is then only whether we hurry that end on ourselves.[^1]

**Three responses, if Nature is all**[^3]
1. Suicide: return "the unwelcome gift" of a consciousness that demands meaning.
2. Have as good a time as possible, though only the coarsest pleasures survive knowing that beauty and love are accidental patterns of atoms.
3. Defy the universe: "Let it be merciless, I will have mercy."
- Lewis says most materialists alternate between the second and third. The third is "incomparably the better," but both "shipwreck on the same rock": if our standards come from a meaningless Nature, they are as meaningless as it is.

**The self-refutation argument**[^2]
- If naturalism is true, our minds are "merely arrangements of atoms in skulls"; we never think a thought because it is true or do an act because it is right.
- ==We know Nature only by trusting our minds.== If the sciences taught that minds are chance arrangements of atoms, the sciences would be too, and we would have no reason to believe them.
- So, Lewis concludes, we are "spirits, free and rational beings," not derived from Nature: "We are strangers here." If this world were the only one, he asks, how did we discover "that Nature's line is crooked?"

**Nature as sister, not mother**[^4]
- If Nature and we share a Creator, she is a "sparring partner"; her gleams of beauty are "not to be worshipped but to be enjoyed." She has nothing to teach us; we live by our own law of love and temperance.

## Why It Matters
- Compare Lewis's account of humans as free, rational spirits with SAIL's teaching that humans are spiritual, rational, and volitional beings made in God's image; see [[Imago Dei]].[^5]
