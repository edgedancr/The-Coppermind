> [!summary]
> An analytic title states a paper's message, not just its topic. Kaiser and Pherson advise naming the key actor or trend and using an active verb to introduce the "so what?"; ==a writer who cannot condense the message into a pithy title lacks a clear message== ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 4 (scan).pdf#page=3|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 21]]). The BLUF handout calls the title "the ultimate BLUF," a contract to give the reader no more and no less than it promises ([[BLUF Writing Format - How to Write an Intelligence Product (Henley-Putnam).pdf#page=7|BLUF handout, p. 7]]).

## Key Points
- **Descriptive vs. analytic:** "Assessing the Political Strengths and Weaknesses of President John Doe" only names a topic; "President John Doe Struggling to Assert Power" makes a judgment ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 4 (scan).pdf#page=3|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 21]]).
- **Working title first, final title last:** a working title focuses the paper from the start, and the writer refines it once the paper is done ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 4 (scan).pdf#page=3|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 21]]). The titles handout gives the same advice ([[SRC - Developing Analytic Titles|Analytic Titles handout, "Create a Working Title"]]), and the BLUF handout says to write the title last ([[BLUF Writing Format - How to Write an Intelligence Product (Henley-Putnam).pdf#page=7|BLUF handout, p. 7]]).
- **Shorten it:** "Significant Local Opposition to Extremists in Antarctica" becomes "Residents Oppose Extremists in Antarctica" ([[Kaiser and Pherson - Analytic Writing Guide, ch. 9-11 (scan).pdf#page=11|Kaiser & Pherson, p. 72]]).
- **Accurate, brief, and clear:** "Cloak of Mist: The Keys To Disappearing" tells a busy reader nothing about its subject; "The Keys to Disappearing: Essential Tools for Covert Agent Tradecraft" does ([[BLUF Writing Format - How to Write an Intelligence Product (Henley-Putnam).pdf#page=7|BLUF handout, p. 7]]). A wordy title about covert action becomes "Covert Action: The Lessons of Past Failures and Successes" ([[BLUF Writing Format - How to Write an Intelligence Product (Henley-Putnam).pdf#page=8|BLUF handout, p. 8]]).
- **The titles handout's checklist** ([[SRC - Developing Analytic Titles|Analytic Titles handout, "The Final Title"]]):
	- State the subject and scope accurately, and name the key variables.
	- Avoid abbreviations and constructions such as "study of" or "analysis of."
	- Use no more than 10 to 15 substantive words.
- **The Grammar Summaries' version:** pithy titles with action verbs carry the paper's basic message; if the paper cannot be reduced to one, its thesis may not be narrow enough ([[SRC - Grammar Summaries, Weeks 2-7|Grammar, wk 3]]).

## Why It Matters
- The title is often a writer's first and only chance to catch a decision maker's attention ([[BLUF Writing Format - How to Write an Intelligence Product (Henley-Putnam).pdf#page=7|BLUF handout, p. 7]]). See [[Bottom Line Up Front]] and [[Detailed Outline]].
