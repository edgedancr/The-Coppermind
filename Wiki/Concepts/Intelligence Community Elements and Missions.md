> [!summary]
> Intro to Intelligence's handout on IC organizations lists the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) and the 17 other members of the U.S. Intelligence Community, in three groups, with each one's core mission ([[SRC - IC Organizations, Leadership, Missions, and Functions|IC Organizations, table]]). Some members are **functional managers**, which, under Executive Order 12333, ==set strategy, training, and tradecraft standards for one intelligence discipline across the whole community== ([[SRC - IC Organizations, Leadership, Missions, and Functions|IC Organizations, intro]]).

## Key Points
**ODNI** oversees and leads integration in the IC, is the principal intelligence adviser to the President, the National Security Council, and the Homeland Security Council, and directs the National Intelligence Program (EO 12333, sec. 1.3) ([[SRC - IC Organizations, Leadership, Missions, and Functions|IC Organizations, table]]).

**Program managers** ([[SRC - IC Organizations, Leadership, Missions, and Functions|IC Organizations, table]])
- **CIA:** functional manager for HUMINT; all-source intelligence for the President and NSC; special activities as the President directs.
- **DIA:** functional manager for MASINT; all-source intelligence for the President, the armed forces, and force planners.
- **NSA:** functional manager for SIGINT; protects the nation's information systems and collects foreign SIGINT.
- **NGA:** functional manager for GEOINT; a Defense combat support agency providing information about the Earth.
- **NRO:** the "eyes and ears" in space; builds and operates space-based reconnaissance (GEOINT and SIGINT).
- **FBI:** head of U.S. counterintelligence; focuses on terrorists, foreign intelligence services, WMD proliferators, and criminal enterprises.

**Departmental organizations** ([[SRC - IC Organizations, Leadership, Missions, and Functions|IC Organizations, table]])
- **DEA:** drug-related information from its enforcement work.
- **Energy:** nuclear terrorism, proliferation, and foreign technology threats.
- **Homeland Security:** threats to the homeland, from border security and CBRN to infrastructure and extremism.
- **State (INR):** all-source intelligence for the Secretary of State.
- **Treasury:** the financing of terrorists, insurgents, rogue regimes, and proliferators.

**Military service organizations:** Air Force, Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Coast Guard, and Space Force intelligence, each supporting its service's missions; the Coast Guard also supports law enforcement, and the Space Force also serves as a service cryptologic component to NSA and a service cyber component to U.S. Cyber Command ([[SRC - IC Organizations, Leadership, Missions, and Functions|IC Organizations, table]]).

**All-source producers:** the CIA's Directorate of Intelligence, DIA's, and State's INR serve specific policymakers and work together most often on National Intelligence Estimates (Lowenthal, 8th ed., p. 168) ([[SRC - IC Organizations, Leadership, Missions, and Functions|IC Organizations, intro]]). See [[Redundant Analytic Structure]].

- Thomas Fingar notes that implied status differences between collectors and analysts ("blue-collar versus white-collar") sometimes kept analysts and whole agencies from certain information and from combining insights across disciplines ([[SRC - IC Organizations, Leadership, Missions, and Functions|IC Organizations, intro]]).
