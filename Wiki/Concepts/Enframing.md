> [!summary]
> Enframing is [[Martin Heidegger]]'s name for the way modern technology reveals the world: as [[Standing Reserve|standing reserve]], resources gathered, stockpiled, and ready to be used. A Then & Now video explains the idea from Heidegger's 1954 essay "The Question Concerning Technology," and applies it to social media ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 1]]), ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 4]]). In the video's words, Heidegger held that ==the essence of technology "is by no means anything technical"==: it is a way of revealing ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 2–3]]).

## Key Points
All points are the video's explanation of Heidegger, unless marked as the narrator's own application.

**From instrument to revealing**
- At its simplest, technology is a human activity, a means to an end: an instrument ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 2]]). Instruments imply causes, so the video turns to Aristotle's [[Four Causes|four causes]] ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 2]]).
- For Heidegger, the four causes are co-responsible for bringing something forth: out of concealment and into the open. That revealing is a kind of truth ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 3]]).
- Every bringing-forth reveals something, as a craftsman brings forth a chalice or an engineer a jet engine. Technology comes from human desires and needs, good and bad, and can be beautiful ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 4]]).

**Modern technology: standing reserve and enframing** ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 4]])
- Modern technology also challenges the world to supply and store: coal is stockpiled, and an airliner sits on the runway ready to fly. Heidegger calls this standing reserve.
- Heidegger writes that this gathers people into ordering the real as standing reserve; he calls this enframing.

**The danger** ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 5]])
- Once a pursuit begins, collecting coal, cars, phones, or friends quickly seems natural; it "gets ahead of us" and presents itself as the status quo, so we forget how it started.
- Heidegger writes that where enframing reigns, there is danger "in the highest sense."

**The narrator's application to social media**
- The four causes of social media are its hardware and content; its code and algorithms; the desire for human connection; and its owner, along with its users ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 2]]). Users shape everything but the hardware and the algorithm ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 3]]).
- The essence of social media "is nothing technical" but a way of revealing and enframing social relations: our own data, stored as standing reserve and presented back to us ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 5]]).

## Why It Matters
- The video's response is to keep questioning: do we need to stockpile, who makes the decisions that frame us, and who controls the algorithm? It concludes that it's up to us "to think before we use" ([[Then and Now - The Question Concerning Technology (transcript)|Then & Now, ch. 6]]).
