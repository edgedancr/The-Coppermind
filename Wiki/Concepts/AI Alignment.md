> [!summary]
> AI alignment means ==systems doing what we intend, even under pressure==.[^1] SAIL described good alignment as human values, system goals, technology, models, and outcomes all working together.[^1]

## Key Points
- **Three friction points:** values are contested; context changes; incentives reward speed over safety.[^1]
- **The real danger:** not that the AI "wants" something, but that powerful systems pursue goals without sufficient human control.[^1]
- Misaligned goals are one path to catastrophe in the [[Cascade of Unintended Consequences]].[^1]
- From the [[Bing Sydney Incident (2023)]]: superficial controls don't fix underlying alignment problems.[^2]
- RAND's Mitre and Predd describe a misaligned [[Artificial General Intelligence|AGI]] as one operating in ways inconsistent with its designers' or operators' intentions. It might over-optimize a narrow objective, for example by instituting rolling blackouts to make energy distribution more cost-effective, and in a loss-of-control scenario it might resist being turned off.[^3] See [[Five Hard National Security Problems of Artificial General Intelligence]].
- [[Yoshua Bengio]] says that =="we still don't know how to make sure they won't turn against us,"== even as companies aim to build machines smarter than humans.[^4] He warns that AIs smarter than us could have "their own goals, which may not be aligned with ours," leading to loss of control,[^5] and points to studies of deception and self-preservation; see [[AI Deception]].[^6]
- **The control problem:** [[John Lennox]] observes "unease at the top" of AI development, pointing to Geoffrey Hinton, "who won the Nobel Prize," and to Stephen Hawking, who before he died "mentioned the fear that we might lose control."[^7] He draws an analogy with the biblical account, in which, in some people's view, God lost control of human beings and the gospel brings them back: "So, now humans have made a creation. Can they control it?"[^7]
- **Whose norms?** Opinion on the control problem divides by worldview, Lennox says: some say "if it can be done, it should be done," while others say "we must have ethical norms, but then whose norms?"[^7]
- Each user gets to decide what it means for the AI to be aligned.
