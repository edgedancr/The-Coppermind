> [!summary]
> Intro to Intelligence taught the collection disciplines, or "INTs," as the main ways the IC gathers the raw data used to produce finished intelligence, each with its own sources, strengths, and weaknesses ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/15/26|Intro notes, 9/15/26]]). Collection, the first stage of the [[Intelligence Cycle]], can draw on open sources or clandestine ones, such as technical means ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/15/26|Intro notes, 9/15/26]]).

## Key Points
All from ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/15/26|Intro notes, 9/15/26]]) unless noted.
- **OSINT (open-source intelligence):** anything publicly available to an ordinary citizen. It needs no specialized collection techniques. The CIA is its functional manager, responsible for advancing tools, technology, and training across the whole IC. ==Personal information must be anonymized==: it can be cited, but with pseudonyms, redaction, or other protection.
- **HUMINT (human intelligence):** data from human sources: overt collection from personnel, clandestine operations, political intrigue, official contacts with foreign governments, and direct observation. The CIA is its functional manager.
	- *Strengths:* insight into people's plans and intentions; cheap in money, though the human cost can be very high.
	- *Weaknesses:* humans can be unreliable, and collection often puts lives at risk.
- **SIGINT (signals intelligence):** COMINT (communications intelligence: any communications medium, digital or analog broadcast), ELINT (electronic intelligence: anything emitted from electronics, by antenna or unintentionally), and FIS (foreign instrumentation signals). SIGINT tries to *interpret* signals, while MASINT tries to prove that signals exist. See [[Traffic Analysis]].
- **MASINT (measurement and signature intelligence):** radio frequencies, radar, and the like.
- **GEOINT (geospatial intelligence):** information visually depicting events; it can be done remotely. Downsides: very expensive, can be overly compelling, and weather and spoofing can mislead it (see [[Denial and Deception]]).
- **FININT (financial intelligence):** follow the money. A foreign country suddenly spending heavily at a research facility should be investigated.
- **ABI and POL:** *pattern of life* is the set of behaviors and movements tied to an entity over time; *activity-based intelligence* is a method that rapidly integrates data from many sources to find patterns and detect change through spatial and temporal correlation.
- **ADTECH:** information about where you look on a screen, and the "digital exhaust" or "behavioral surplus" people leave online (websites visited, ads viewed, metadata).
