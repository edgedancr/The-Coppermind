> [!summary]
> On August 7, 1998, al-Qaeda truck bombs struck the U.S. embassies in Nairobi, Kenya, and Dar es Salaam, Tanzania, about five minutes apart ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=87|9/11 Report, p. 70]]). The 9/11 Commission notes that, unlike earlier attacks by allied groups, these were ==planned, directed, and executed by al-Qaeda under Bin Ladin's direct supervision== ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=84|9/11 Report, p. 67]]).

## What Happened
- **Casing:** from December 1993, an al-Qaeda team led by Ali Mohamed, a former Egyptian army officer who had served in the U.S. Army, cased targets in Nairobi; by January 1994 Bin Ladin and his military committee had reviewed the reports and judged the embassy an easy target because a car bomb could park close by ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=85|9/11 Report, p. 68]]).
- **Delays and exposure:** in August 1997 the Kenya cell panicked after press reports and U.S. and Kenyan searches of its manager's home, and the new manager warned the cell was "in grave danger" ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=85|9/11 Report, pp. 68–69]]).
- **Launch:** the teams came together within a month of Bin Ladin's February 1998 [[World Islamic Front Fatwa|fatwa]], suggesting the decision to attack had been made by then ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=86|9/11 Report, p. 69]]).
- **The attacks:** Nairobi killed 12 Americans and 201 others, almost all Kenyans, and injured about 5,000; Dar es Salaam killed 11, none of them Americans ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=87|9/11 Report, p. 70]]).

## What Went Wrong
- In 1997, a year before the attack, U.S. and Kenyan agents searched the home of the Nairobi cell's manager, Wadi al Hage, and tapped his telephone; the cell adjusted and carried on ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=85|9/11 Report, pp. 68–69]]).

## Lessons
- KSM said the bombings convinced him Bin Ladin was truly committed to attacking the United States, a watershed for the [[Planes Operation|9/11 plot]] ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=166|9/11 Report, p. 149]]).
- The U.S. response is covered in a later batch of the Commission Report.
