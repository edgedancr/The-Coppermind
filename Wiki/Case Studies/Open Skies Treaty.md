> [!summary]
> In an Intro to Intelligence guest lecture, CDR Blocker used the Open Skies approach to transparency as a case study in "rebuilding credibility and norms after Operation Spiderweb" ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/6/26|Intro notes, 10/6/26]]). He framed ==the collapse of a norm, not a treaty,== as the central issue: for seventy years the U.S. and Russia had kept a norm of transparency that helped prevent miscalculation and catastrophic escalation ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/6/26|Intro notes, 10/6/26]]).

## What Happened
All from CDR Blocker's guest lecture ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/6/26|Intro notes, 10/6/26]]).
- **2020:** U.S. withdrawal; Russian withdrawal; informal norms persist.
- **June 2025, Operation Spiderweb:** Ukrainian drones destroyed Russian bombers sitting out in the open.

> [!caution] Accuracy check
> My notes put Russia's withdrawal under 2020. Russia announced its withdrawal in January 2021, effective December 2021. Check the slide.

## What Went Wrong
- The question Blocker posed: how should the U.S. respond now that the very principle of transparency may no longer be stabilizing ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/6/26|Intro notes, 10/6/26]])?
- He also asked: what is transparency in security ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/6/26|Intro notes, 10/6/26]])?

## Lessons
**The policy dilemma:** should the U.S. restore, redefine, or replace the Open Skies approach to transparency in great-power relations ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/6/26|Intro notes, 10/6/26]])?

**Decision framework** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/6/26|Intro notes, 10/6/26]])
- *Strategic stability:* does it reduce misperception and the risk of escalation?
- *Alliance assurance:* does it strengthen NATO and partner confidence?
- *Adversary signaling:* what message does it send to Russia and China?
