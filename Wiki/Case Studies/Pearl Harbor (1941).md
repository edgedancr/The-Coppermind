> [!summary]
> Intro to Intelligence used the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor on December 7, 1941, as a case study in flawed intelligence assumptions ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/24/26|Intro notes, 9/24/26]]). U.S. intelligence concluded from tests of captured Japanese munitions that their torpedoes could not work in the harbor's shallow water, and ==assumed Japan could not innovate to solve the problem== ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/24/26|Intro notes, 9/24/26]]).

## What Happened
**The road to Pearl Harbor** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/24/26|Intro notes, 9/24/26]])
- 1931: Japan invades Manchuria.
- 1933: Hitler seizes power in Germany.
- 1936: Germany remilitarizes the Rhineland.
- 1937: Japan invades China proper, most notoriously in the Nanking Massacre.
- 1938: Germany annexes Austria.
- 1939: the Nazi–Soviet pact and the invasion of Poland.
- 1940: Germany invades Denmark and France; the Battle of Britain.
- 1941: Operation Barbarossa, the invasion of the Soviet Union.
- December 7, 1941: Pearl Harbor.

## What Went Wrong
- U.S. intelligence's assumptions, as recorded ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/24/26|Intro notes, 9/24/26]]):
	- Tests of captured Japanese munitions showed their torpedoes could not be used in Pearl Harbor because the water was too shallow, and Japan was assumed unable to innovate around that. Thus, it was thought that Japan would not be able to attack from the sea, only from the air.
