> [!summary]
> In chapter 10 of its report, the 9/11 Commission examined three disputed questions about the government's first responses after the attacks: the detention of "special interest" aliens, the departure of Saudi nationals, and whether Iraq was involved.[^1] This page reports what the Commission found on each.

## What Happened
- **Detainees:** 768 aliens were arrested as "special interest" detainees while the FBI followed up leads; Attorney General Ashcroft called his aim "risk minimization," closed their hearings, and kept their identities secret.[^2]
- **Saudi flights:** Saudi nationals left the United States on charter flights after the attacks.[^3]
- **Iraq:** President Bush asked whether Saddam Hussein's regime might have been involved, and at Camp David Deputy Secretary Wolfowitz argued for striking Iraq.[^4]

## What Went Wrong
The Commission's findings:
- **Detainees:** they were "lawfully held on immigration charges"; 531 were deported, and the Justice Department's inspector general found "significant problems" in how they were treated.[^5]
- **Saudi flights:** no flights left before national airspace reopened on September 13, no evidence of political intervention, and the FBI screened the passengers satisfactorily; no one with known links to terrorism left on them.[^6]
- **Iraq:** a September 18 memo from Clarke's office found no "compelling case" that Iraq had planned or perpetrated the attacks, citing only anecdotal evidence.[^7] President Bush told journalist Bob Woodward that the decision not to invade Iraq was made on September 15, with Afghanistan as the priority.[^8]
