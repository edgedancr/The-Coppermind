> [!summary]
> On August 20, 1998, two weeks after the [[East Africa Embassy Bombings (1998)|embassy bombings]], U.S. Navy cruise missiles struck al-Qaeda camps near Khowst, Afghanistan, and the al Shifa pharmaceutical plant in Khartoum, Sudan; the strikes missed Bin Ladin, probably by a few hours ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=133|9/11 Report, pp. 116–117]]). The 9/11 Commission found that their failure, the political climate, and "the nature of the al Shifa evidence" likely had a cumulative effect on later decisions about using force ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=135|9/11 Report, p. 118]]).

## What Happened
- **Khowst:** intelligence said terrorist leaders, including Bin Ladin, would gather at a camp there; "the strike's purpose was to kill Bin Ladin and his chief lieutenants" ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=133|9/11 Report, p. 116]]).
- **Al Shifa:** the case rested on a soil sample from near the plant that tested positive for EMPTA, a precursor of the nerve agent VX ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=133|9/11 Report, pp. 116–117]]). The NSC's senior director for intelligence programs initially cautioned that "we will need much better intelligence on this facility," calling the Bin Ladin link "rather uncertain" ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=134|9/11 Report, p. 117]]).
- **The result:** the strikes killed 20–30 people in the camps but no terrorist leaders ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=134|9/11 Report, p. 117]]).

## What Went Wrong
- ==No independent evidence has emerged to corroborate the CIA's assessment of al Shifa== ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=135|9/11 Report, p. 118]]).
- Critics called the strikes too aggressive, and some called them a "wag the dog" distraction from domestic scandal; the Commission found no reason to question officials' testimony that they acted solely on national security grounds ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=134|9/11 Report, pp. 117–118]]).

## Lessons
- Afterward, Richard Clarke's [[Delenda Plan]] called for rolling strikes, but the Small Group was unpersuaded that primitive "jungle gym" camps were worth expensive missiles, or that strikes that missed would not boost Bin Ladin's stature ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=137|9/11 Report, p. 120]]).
