> [!summary]
> The 9/11 Commission describes several leads in 2001 that could have exposed the plot: two future hijackers, Khalid al Mihdhar and Nawaf al Hazmi, who were already known to the CIA; the Phoenix memo; the arrest of Zacarias Moussaoui; and reporting on [[Khalid Sheikh Mohammed]].[^1] ==No one working these leads "connected the case in his or her in-box to the threat reports"== agitating senior officials.[^2]

## What Happened
- **Khallad (January 2001):** a source identified Khallad, a planner of the [[USS Cole Bombing (2000)|USS Cole bombing]], in a photo from the January 2000 Kuala Lumpur meeting with Mihdhar; the CIA neither renewed the search for Mihdhar nor told the FBI.[^3]
- **New York meeting (June 2001):** an FBI analyst showed Cole agents photos from Kuala Lumpur but would not share NSA reports because of their caveats, and a CIA analyst who knew of Mihdhar's U.S. visa was not asked and did not volunteer it.[^4] Mihdhar received a new visa two days later and flew to New York on July 4.[^5]
- **The search (August 2001):** Mihdhar and Hazmi were added to the [[TIPOFF Watchlist|TIPOFF watchlist]] on August 24, but the FBI lead was marked "Routine," criminal agents were kept off the search because of [[The Wall (FBI)|"the wall"]], and it went to one agent on his first counterterrorism lead.[^6]
- **The [[Phoenix Memo]] (July 2001)** went unread by headquarters managers before [[September 11 Attacks (2001)|9/11]].[^7]
- **Moussaoui (August 2001):** arrested on an immigration violation after seeking 747 simulator training, but FBI headquarters judged there was not enough to seek a FISA warrant to search his belongings.[^8] KSM says Moussaoui was meant for a "second wave"; the Commission suspects he was being primed as a possible pilot in the [[Planes Operation|planes operation]] itself.[^9]
- **KSM:** the CIA did not connect reporting that "Mukhtar" was planning attacks with an August 28 cable giving that as KSM's nickname.[^10]

## What Went Wrong
- **Who managed the case?** In each lead, "no one was firmly in charge".[^11] The CIA official "John" called the CIA a "zone defense" that watched "where," not "who";[^12] see [[Zone Defense and Man-to-Man]].
- **Sharing:** the Commission concluded that "everyone involved was confused about the rules" for sharing intelligence.[^13] See [[Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act]].

## Lessons
- The Commission believes that with more resources and a different approach Mihdhar and Hazmi "might have been found," though luck would also have been needed.[^7]
- It found "little evidence that the progress of the plot was disturbed by any government action": "Time ran out".[^2] The ten operational opportunities are listed in [[Failures of Imagination, Policy, Capabilities, and Management]].
