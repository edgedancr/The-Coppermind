> [!summary]
> On September 11, 2001, nineteen hijackers took over four commercial flights: two hit the World Trade Center, one hit the Pentagon, and one crashed in Pennsylvania as its passengers fought to retake it ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=24|9/11 Report, pp. 7–14]]).

## What Happened
- **The flights** ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=18|9/11 Report, pp. 1–14]]): American 11 (Boston) hit the North Tower at 8:46:40 and United 175 (Boston) the South Tower at 9:03:11; American 77 (Washington Dulles) hit the Pentagon at 9:37:46; United 93 (Newark) crashed in a field in Shanksville, Pennsylvania, after a passenger revolt, about 20 minutes' flying time from Washington. Five hijackers were on each flight except United 93, which had four.
- **The response:** see [[Air Defense on September 11 (2001)]].
- **Background**
	- Bin Ladin's February 1998 [[World Islamic Front Fatwa|fatwa]] called for killing Americans anywhere ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=64|9/11 Report, p. 47]]); see [[Al-Qaeda]].
	- Khalid Sheikh Mohammed (KSM), the plot's "principal architect," proposed the [[Planes Operation|planes operation]] to Bin Ladin in 1996 ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=162|9/11 Report, pp. 145, 149]]).
	- Al-Qaeda moved money by [[Hawala|hawala]] ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=188|9/11 Report, p. 171]]), and in October 2000 bombed the [[USS Cole Bombing (2000)|USS Cole]] ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=207|9/11 Report, p. 190]]).
	- All 19 tickets were bought between August 25 and September 5, 2001 ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=266|9/11 Report, p. 249]]).
- **U.S. approaches beforehand:** Richard Clarke's [[Delenda Plan]] ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=137|9/11 Report, p. 120]]) and the CIA's [[The Plan (CIA)|"Plan"]] ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=159|9/11 Report, p. 142]]); see also [[Tarnak Farms Capture Plan (1998)]] and [[August 1998 Missile Strikes]].

> [!caution] Accuracy check
> The Report gives United 93 two destinations: "going to Los Angeles" (p. 4) and "bound for San Francisco" (p. 10, and the timeline chart). The grammar summaries follow p. 4.

## What Went Wrong
- **FBI priorities:** field offices set their own priorities, measured performance by arrests and convictions, and served "local priorities, not national priorities," such as white-collar crime, drugs, and gangs ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=91|9/11 Report, p. 74]]). See [[FBI Counterterrorism Before September 11]].
- **Sharing:** [[The Wall (FBI)|"The Wall"]] kept intelligence from flowing between the FBI's intelligence and criminal sides ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=95|9/11 Report, pp. 78–80]]), though [[Joint Terrorism Task Force|Joint Terrorism Task Forces]] joined local police and the FBI on counterterrorism ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=98|9/11 Report, pp. 81–82]]).
- **The summer of threat ("blinking red") and the late leads:** see [[Summer of Threat (2001)]] and [[Late Leads Before September 11 (2001)]].

## Lessons
- The 9/11 Commission concluded that the attacks revealed four kinds of failure: in imagination, policy, capabilities, and management ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=356|9/11 Report, p. 339]]). See [[Failures of Imagination, Policy, Capabilities, and Management]] and [[Institutionalizing Imagination]].
- The course's summary concludes that ==the IC knew the threat was developing but lacked the tactical intelligence to show where it was aimed== ([[SRC - Grammar Summaries, Weeks 2-7|Grammar, wk 5]]). See [[Levels of Intelligence]].
- Intro to Intelligence taught 9/11 as a lesson in preparation: there is "no option to be lazy or stupid" when attackers are ready to exploit any vulnerability ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/10/26|Intro notes, 9/10/26]]).
- After 9/11, "national intelligence" made all intelligence a shared enterprise, whether collected at home by the FBI or abroad by the CIA ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/10/26|Intro notes, 9/10/26]]). See [[Executive Order 12333]] and [[Stovepipe]].
