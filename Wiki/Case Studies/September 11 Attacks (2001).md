> [!summary]
> On September 11, 2001, nineteen hijackers took over four commercial flights: two hit the World Trade Center, one hit the Pentagon, and one crashed in Pennsylvania as its passengers fought to retake it.[^1]

## What Happened
- **The flights**:[^2] American 11 (Boston) hit the North Tower at 8:46:40 and United 175 (Boston) the South Tower at 9:03:11; American 77 (Washington Dulles) hit the Pentagon at 9:37:46; United 93 (Newark) crashed in a field in Shanksville, Pennsylvania, after a passenger revolt, about 20 minutes' flying time from Washington. Five hijackers were on each flight except United 93, which had four.
- **The response:** see [[Air Defense on September 11 (2001)]].
- **Background**
	- [[Usama Bin Ladin|Bin Ladin]]'s February 1998 [[World Islamic Front Fatwa|fatwa]] called for killing Americans anywhere;[^3] see [[Al-Qaeda]].
	- [[Khalid Sheikh Mohammed]] (KSM), the plot's "principal architect," proposed the [[Planes Operation|planes operation]] to Bin Ladin in 1996.[^4]
	- Al-Qaeda moved money by [[Hawala|hawala]],[^5] and in October 2000 bombed the [[USS Cole Bombing (2000)|USS Cole]].[^6]
	- All 19 tickets were bought between August 25 and September 5, 2001.[^7]
- **U.S. approaches beforehand:** Richard Clarke's [[Delenda Plan]][^8] and the CIA's [[The Plan (CIA)|"Plan"]];[^9] see also [[Tarnak Farms Capture Plan (1998)]] and [[August 1998 Missile Strikes]].

> [!caution] Accuracy check
> The Report gives United 93 two destinations: "going to Los Angeles"[^10] and "bound for San Francisco".[^11] The grammar summaries follow p. 4.

## What Went Wrong
- **FBI priorities:** field offices set their own priorities, measured performance by arrests and convictions, and served "local priorities, not national priorities," such as white-collar crime, drugs, and gangs.[^12] See [[FBI Counterterrorism Before September 11]].
- **Sharing:** [[The Wall (FBI)|"The Wall"]] kept intelligence from flowing between the FBI's intelligence and criminal sides,[^13] though [[Joint Terrorism Task Force|Joint Terrorism Task Forces]] joined local police and the FBI on counterterrorism.[^14]
- **The summer of threat ("blinking red") and the late leads:** see [[Summer of Threat (2001)]] and [[Late Leads Before September 11 (2001)]].

## Lessons
- The 9/11 Commission concluded that the attacks revealed four kinds of failure: in imagination, policy, capabilities, and management.[^15] See [[Failures of Imagination, Policy, Capabilities, and Management]] and [[Institutionalizing Imagination]].
- The course's summary concludes that ==the IC knew the threat was developing but lacked the tactical intelligence to show where it was aimed==.[^16] See [[Levels of Intelligence]].
- Intro to Intelligence taught 9/11 as a lesson in preparation: there is "no option to be lazy or stupid" when attackers are ready to exploit any vulnerability.[^17]
- After 9/11, "national intelligence" made all intelligence a shared enterprise, whether collected at home by the FBI or abroad by the CIA.[^17] See [[Executive Order 12333]] and [[Stovepipe]].
