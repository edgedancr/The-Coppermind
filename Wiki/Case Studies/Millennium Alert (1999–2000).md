> [!summary]
> In late 1999, Jordanian intelligence disrupted an al-Qaeda-linked bombing plot, and on December 14 a Customs inspector caught Ahmed Ressam bringing explosives across the Canadian border, apparently to bomb Los Angeles International Airport.[^1] The 9/11 Commission calls the period ==the one time before 9/11 when the government "seemed to be acting in concert" against terrorism==.[^2]

## What Happened
- **Jordan:** on November 30, 1999, Jordanian intelligence intercepted a call in which Abu Zubaydah said "the time for training is over," and arrested 16 people.[^3]
- **Disruption abroad:** Tenet spoke to 20 foreign counterparts, and arrest operations were mounted in eight countries.[^4]
- **Ressam:** an Algerian who had entered Canada with a falsified passport, trained in Afghanistan, and chose LAX as "the largest and easiest" target.[^5] He later obtained a genuine Canadian passport through a vendor who stole a blank baptismal certificate, which the Commission presents as "a case study in terrorist travel".[^6]

## What Went Wrong
- After the alert the government relaxed, and counterterrorism "went back to being a secret preserve" for a few offices.[^7]

## Lessons
- The Commission credits the success to worry over Y2K and, "at least equally important," to "simply shared information": the plots were front-page news, and ==terrorism had a face, that of Ahmed Ressam==.[^7] See [[Need to Share]] and [[Failures of Imagination, Policy, Capabilities, and Management]].
