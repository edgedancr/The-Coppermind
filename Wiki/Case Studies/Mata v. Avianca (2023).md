> [!summary]
> In *Mata v. Avianca*, a lawyer used ChatGPT to help write a legal brief that cited prior cases, including *Varghese v. China Southern Airlines*, a case that did not exist.[^1] SAIL used it to teach that ==capability is not reliability==.[^1]

## What Happened
- ChatGPT generated a hallucinated citation, a fabricated case that *looked* real: a plausible plaintiff name (Varghese), a real airline (China Southern Airlines), and proper legal citation formatting.[^1]
- The model was not retrieving legal records; it was filling in the gaps of what it needed with fiction.[^1]

## What Went Wrong
- The case is not just about an AI making a mistake; it is about over-trusting and misusing [[Generative AI|generative AI]], treating a powerful tool as an authoritative source.[^1]
- Using such tools in high-risk professional settings without verification exposes others to real harm: "Using AI without understanding its limits is not innovation; it is risk transfer."[^1]

## Lessons
- **Capability:** AI can generate professional-quality legal language. **Reliability:** AI cannot be trusted to verify facts or sources on its own.[^1] See [[Capability vs. Reliability]].
- Always think, calculate, then verify.[^1] See [[Think-Calculate-Verify Loop]].
