> [!summary]
> In *Mata v. Avianca*, a lawyer used ChatGPT to help write a legal brief that cited prior cases, including *Varghese v. China Southern Airlines*, a case that did not exist ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/1/26|SAIL notes, 9/1/26]]). SAIL used it to teach that ==capability is not reliability== ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/1/26|SAIL notes, 9/1/26]]).

## What Happened
- ChatGPT generated a hallucinated citation, a fabricated case that *looked* real: a plausible plaintiff name (Varghese), a real airline (China Southern Airlines), and proper legal citation formatting ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/1/26|SAIL notes, 9/1/26]]).
- The model was not retrieving legal records; it was filling in the gaps of what it needed with fiction ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/1/26|SAIL notes, 9/1/26]]).

## What Went Wrong
- The case is not just about an AI making a mistake; it is about over-trusting and misusing generative AI, treating a powerful tool as an authoritative source ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/1/26|SAIL notes, 9/1/26]]).
- Using such tools in high-risk professional settings without verification exposes others to real harm: "Using AI without understanding its limits is not innovation; it is risk transfer." ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/1/26|SAIL notes, 9/1/26]])

## Lessons
- **Capability:** AI can generate professional-quality legal language. **Reliability:** AI cannot be trusted to verify facts or sources on its own ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/1/26|SAIL notes, 9/1/26]]). See [[Capability vs. Reliability]].
- Always think, calculate, then verify ([[SAIL Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/1/26|SAIL notes, 9/1/26]]). See [[Think-Calculate-Verify Loop]].
