> [!summary]
> **Intelligence oversight committees:** the two congressional committees over U.S. intelligence: the **House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence (HPSCI)** and the **Senate Select Committee on Intelligence (SSCI)**.[^1] In the course's discussion answer citing Lowenthal, the SSCI has exclusive oversight of the DNI and the CIA and assesses U.S. intelligence capabilities to monitor treaties; HPSCI oversees the [[National Intelligence Program]] agencies; and the Senate and House Armed Services Committees oversee the Defense Department's intelligence agencies and the [[Military Intelligence Program]].[^2] Lowenthal describes oversight as committees or groups making sure fair and legal actions are taken by other, lower-level individuals or groups.[^3] The 9/11 Commission notes that Congress created the select committees in 1977, after the Church and Pike investigations.[^4]

> [!caution] Sources disagree
> The SSCI "has exclusive oversight of the DNI and CIA" (Grammar, wk 5) vs. the intelligence committees "do not have exclusive authority over intelligence agencies," and the Senate Armed Services Committee also has jurisdiction over the CIA.[^5]

> [!caution] Accuracy check
> My notes call them the "two governing bodies over intel." They oversee the IC rather than govern it. Check the wording that was taught.
> Possibly resolved by [[SRC - Grammar Summaries, Weeks 2-7]], weeks 5–6.[^6]

> [!caution] Accuracy check
> The discussion answer says HPSCI oversees the NIP ("civilian") agencies while the Armed Services Committees oversee the Defense agencies. The two intelligence committees are usually described as overseeing the whole IC, including NIP-funded Defense agencies such as NSA, NGA, and NRO. Check Lowenthal.
> Possibly resolved by [[SRC - The 9-11 Commission Report]], p. 103: the intelligence committees share jurisdiction, with Armed Services covering the Defense Department's intelligence agencies.
