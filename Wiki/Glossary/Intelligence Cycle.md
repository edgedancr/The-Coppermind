> [!summary]
> **Intelligence cycle:** the stages by which intelligence is produced. Intro to Intelligence listed **collection** (gathering raw data from open or clandestine sources), **processing** (converting it into a usable format, such as translation or decryption), **analysis and production** (turning processed information into finished intelligence, such as drafting reports, evaluating sources, or resolving data conflicts), and **dissemination**.[^1]

Lowenthal's version, as the Grammar Summaries give it, starts with **requirements** (which policy issues the [[United States Intelligence Community|Intelligence Community]] is expected to support, their relative priority, and possibly which collection methods to use) and calls the second stage **processing and exploitation**.[^2]

Judith Meister Johnston and [[Rob Johnston]], citing the CIA, give five steps: **planning and direction**, collection, processing, analysis and production, and dissemination.[^3] They argue the traditional cycle misleads: it assumes every task works the same way, omits iteration, assigns no responsibilities, and ignores the analyst's burden. Treverton's "Real" Intelligence Cycle and Lowenthal's model show more iteration.[^4] See [[Systems Model of the Intelligence Cycle]].

SAIL's version of the cycle differs; see the flag on [[Intelligence-to-Action Convergence Matrix]].
