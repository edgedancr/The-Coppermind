> [!summary]
> **Intelligence cycle:** the stages by which intelligence is produced. Intro to Intelligence listed **collection** (gathering raw data from open or clandestine sources), **processing** (converting it into a usable format, such as translation or decryption), **analysis and production** (turning processed information into finished intelligence, such as drafting reports, evaluating sources, or resolving data conflicts), and **dissemination** ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/15/26|Intro notes, 9/15/26]]).

Lowenthal's version, as the Grammar Summaries give it, starts with **requirements** (which policy issues the Intelligence Community is expected to support, their relative priority, and possibly which collection methods to use) and calls the second stage **processing and exploitation** (Lowenthal, L: 67–75/57–58, as summarized in [[SRC - Grammar Summaries, Weeks 2-7|Grammar, wk 2]]).

Judith Meister Johnston and Rob Johnston, citing the CIA, give five steps: **planning and direction**, collection, processing, analysis and production, and dissemination ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=64|J. M. Johnston & R. Johnston, pp. 45–46]]). They argue the traditional cycle misleads: it assumes every task works the same way, omits iteration, assigns no responsibilities, and ignores the analyst's burden. Treverton's "Real" Intelligence Cycle and Lowenthal's model show more iteration ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=68|J. M. Johnston & R. Johnston, pp. 49–50]]). See [[Systems Model of the Intelligence Cycle]].

> [!caution] Sources disagree
> On the number of steps and the first one: Intro to Intelligence listed four, starting with collection ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/15/26|Intro notes, 9/15/26]]); Lowenthal starts with requirements (Lowenthal, L: 67–75/57–58, as summarized in [[SRC - Grammar Summaries, Weeks 2-7|Grammar, wk 2]]); Johnston and Johnston list five, starting with planning and direction.

SAIL's version of the cycle differs; see the flag on [[Intelligence-to-Action Convergence Matrix]].
