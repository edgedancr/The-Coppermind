> [!summary]
> **Denial:** trying to prevent people from "seeing" something that is present, as with encryption, refusing to comment, or camouflage. **Deception:** attempting to convey anything besides the whole truth, as with made-up alibis ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/17/26|Intro notes, 9/17/26]]). Lowenthal frames both as uses of knowing an opponent's collection: denial avoids it, and deception feeds it false or incomplete information. "The completely innocent state and the state with very good D&D both may look identical to the observer" (L: 82–83, [[SRC - Themes in US Intelligence, Summary of Texts|Themes summary, theme 9]]). Rob Johnston argues that while deception is the exception in most fields, in intelligence analysis =="deception is the rule; the validity of the data is always in doubt"==, so analysts are trained to look for anomalies and outliers ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=54|Johnston, p. 35]]).
