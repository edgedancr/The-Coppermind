> [!summary]
> **Current vs. long-term intelligence:** current intelligence stays relevant for only a day or two, or a few weeks; long-term reports address a more distant time horizon, usually with greater depth and context. ==Policymakers generally prefer to read current intelligence, and analysts prefer to produce long-term reports== (Lowenthal, L: 154/122, as summarized in [[SRC - Grammar Summaries, Weeks 2-7|Grammar, wk 3]]). The 9/11 Commission describes the CIA's analytic culture in the 1990s giving way from a university culture to "the culture of the newsroom," under pressure to pass fresh reports to policymakers ever faster ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=108|9/11 Report, p. 91]]).
