> [!summary]
> **Covert action:** James Diehl taught that covert action requires a presidential finding ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/20/26|Intro notes, 10/20/26]]). Executive Order 12333 bars any covert action intended to influence U.S. political processes, public opinion, policies, or media (Sec. 2.13) ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#9/10/26|Intro notes, 9/10/26]]). The 9/11 Commission distinguishes acting "clandestinely (secretly)" from acting "covertly (with their connection to the United States hidden)" ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=366|9/11 Report, p. 349]]). It recommended moving lead responsibility for paramilitary operations, clandestine or covert, to the Defense Department, with the CIA keeping other clandestine and covert operations ([[National Commission on Terrorist Attacks - The 9-11 Commission Report (2004).pdf#page=432|9/11 Report, p. 415]]). Lowenthal calls covert actions "interventions by one state in the affairs of another," whose basic ethical issue is their legitimacy (Lowenthal, L: 319, as summarized in [[SRC - Grammar Summaries, Weeks 2-7|Grammar, wk 4]]); see [[Ethical and Moral Issues in Intelligence]].
