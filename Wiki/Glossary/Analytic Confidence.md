> [!summary]
> **Analytic confidence:** how strongly an analytic conclusion is supported ([[Intro to Intelligence Class Notes (Fall 2026)#10/13/26|Intro notes, 10/13/26]]). Lowenthal ties it to the scope and quality of the information behind a judgment: **high** when the information is high quality or the issue allows a solid judgment; **moderate** when the information can be read several ways, there are alternative views, or it is credible but not corroborated; **low** when it is scant, questionable, or fragmented, or rests on problematic sources (Lowenthal, L: 176–177/143, as summarized in [[SRC - Grammar Summaries, Weeks 2-7|Grammar, wk 3]]). ICD 203 says confidence may rest on the logic and evidence behind a judgment, including the quantity and quality of sources and the analyst's understanding of the topic, and it bars stating a confidence level and a degree of likelihood in the same sentence; see [[Estimative Language]] ([[ODNI - ICD 203, Analytic Standards (2015).pdf#page=3|ICD 203, §D.6.e(2)]]).
