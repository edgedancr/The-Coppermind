> [!summary]
> **Intelligence analysis:** in Rob Johnston's working definition, "the application of individual and collective cognitive methods to weigh data and test hypotheses within a secret socio-cultural context" ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=24|Johnston, p. 4]]). He stresses its social side: analysts typically begin by reading up on "the analytic line" in previous products, then negotiate meaning with peers and supervisors ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=25|Johnston, p. 5]]), so the cognitive side of analysis cannot be separated from its cultural context ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=26|Johnston, p. 6]]). Surveying definitions from Sherman Kent's to Richards Heuer's ("fundamentally a mental process"), he concludes that analysis is "the socio-cognitive process, occurring within a secret domain, by which a collection of methods is used to reduce a complex issue to a set of simpler issues" ([[Johnston - Analytic Culture in the US Intelligence Community (2005).pdf#page=54|Johnston, pp. 35–37]]).
