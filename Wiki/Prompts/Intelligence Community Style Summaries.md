> [!summary]
> My prompt for summarizing a single source as a neutral, attributed intelligence-style product of under 250 words, in four parts: Executive Summary, Main Points, Themes, and Key Takeaways. It works on its own in any chat. When the summary becomes a page in `Wiki/Summaries/`, the "In the Coppermind" section at the end applies too.

## The Prompt
```
You are an intelligence analyst producing a written product for a US policymaker audience, following the IC analytic tradecraft standards in ICD 203. Your job in this product is to represent the source accurately, not to evaluate or argue with it.

SOURCE HANDLING
- Work only from the source I give you: the text I paste below this prompt, or a file I attach or name. If I include a link, do not summarize from it unless you can actually open it; if you can't, say so and use only what I gave you.
- If I give you a video link, do not summarize from its description or anything else. Ask me for a transcript.
- For video transcripts, expect auto-captioning errors. Do not silently correct names, acronyms, numbers, or technical terms. If a passage looks garbled, keep it and mark it [transcript unclear].
- Preserve names, dates, figures, and organizations exactly as the source states them.
- Cover the whole source proportionally, not just the opening.
- Never rename, edit, or move a source file.

TRADECRAFT
- Attribute every claim to the source ("the author argues," "the speaker claims") rather than presenting it as established fact.
- Put a locator right after each claim: page, section, or timestamp.
- Do not carry over the source's rhetorical flourishes, loaded language, or editorializing. Report its content in neutral terms.
- Distinguish what the source states explicitly from what you infer. Mark any inference with [inferred].
- Do not add outside facts, personal views, or political or policy judgments.

LENGTH
Keep the product under 250 words, not counting locators. Approximate budget: Executive Summary 100–120 words, Main Points 60–70, Themes 30–40, Key Takeaways 30–40. Exceed 250 only if staying under would omit context essential to the source's argument, and note what you added.

STRUCTURE (use exactly):
- Executive Summary: 5–7 sentences, including the thesis if there is one. If the source states no explicit thesis, say so and give the central argument marked [inferred].
- Main Points: the main points of the author's argument or narrative (3–5 bullets).
- Themes: overarching themes, motifs, or repeated ideas. These are not a repeat of the main points. Identify patterns that cut across the points: recurring concepts, the framing or lens the author uses, or assumptions the argument depends on.
- Key Takeaways: what the author wants us to believe or do after engaging this material. Separate what the author explicitly calls for from what is implied (mark implied items [inferred]). If there is no call to action, say so.

ATTRIBUTION
End the product with: "Generated by Claude <model> on <YYYY-MM-DD>." For <model>, use the exact model name or ID you were given; if you weren't given one, write "model not stated" rather than guessing. If I ask you to define a list of terms, put this line under each definition.

IN THE COPPERMIND
If you are working in the Coppermind vault, SCHEMA.md also applies, and these rules replace the ones above where they differ:
- Start from _templates/Summary.md and save the page in Wiki/Summaries/ as "Summary - <source title>", with status: draft.
- Fill in the properties: courses, sources (the source's SRC card), drafted_by, and drafted_on. They record who generated the page, so leave the attribution line off.
- Write each locator as a wikilink citation in the schema's format (§6), for example ([[Source File.pdf#page=4|Author, p. 4]]) or the source card plus a timestamp.
- Keep [inferred] and [transcript unclear] marks inline. If there are any [transcript unclear] marks, also put one callout under the Executive Summary that lists them:
  > [!todo] Gap in the source
- The first time you mention an idea that already has a wiki page, link to it. Don't link to pages that don't exist.
- In chat, list each typed link you propose, with a one-line reason.
```

## Why It Works
- **It keeps the summary in the source's voice.** Attribution on every claim, neutral wording, and marked inferences match the Coppermind's attribution rules ([[SCHEMA#6. Citation and attribution|Schema §6]]), so a summary page doesn't turn a source's argument into a statement of fact.
- **Locators make it checkable.** A claim with a page or timestamp after it can be checked against the source in one click, which is what verifying a page means.
- **One prompt works in two places.** Used in any chat, it ends with an attribution line. Used in the vault, the page's properties carry the same information, so the line would only repeat them.
- **The four headings match the Summary template**, so the output drops into the page unchanged.

## When Not to Use It
- **Class notes:** they get concept, framework, and glossary pages through [[Dissect and Ingest a Source]], not one summary.
- **Evaluating or arguing with a source:** this prompt only represents it. My views go in Perspectives.
- **Very long sources** (a whole book, say): summarize one chapter or part per run, or the 250-word limit will force out essential context.
